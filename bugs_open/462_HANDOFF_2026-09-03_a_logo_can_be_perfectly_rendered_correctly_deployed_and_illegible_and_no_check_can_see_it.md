# 462 — a logo can be perfectly rendered, correctly deployed, and illegible, and no check in the estate can see it

**Filed** 2026-09-03 by the `bugs_open/417` lane (logo text policy), at the owner's explicit
instruction, after `bugs_open/424`'s lane measured the transparency mechanism working correctly and
handed the legibility question over.
**Severity** medium. Nothing is broken, nothing errors, no status is wrong. **That is the defect:**
the artefact is delivered, every gate passes, and a visitor cannot see the logo.
**Class** missing check / gap between two sound decisions. **Not a failure of the contrast pipeline
— an absence of any image-luminance check at all.**

**START HERE:** §3 is the whole job. §1 is the measurement, §2 is the proof nothing catches it, and
§2a is the part that matters most — the exclusion that leaves this uncovered is a *correct* decision
whose stated reason does not apply to this case.

## 1. The measurement `[MEASURED 2026-09-03]`

`websitepromotion.co.uk` serves a logo that is present, transparent, correctly deployed on 25 of 25
served pages, and close to invisible.

| | websitepromotion.co.uk | seotools.co.uk (control) |
|---|---|---|
| served, with 404 invented-path control | 200, 41,062 B, RGBA | 200, 26,975 B, RGBA |
| fully transparent pixels | 84.3% | 92.2% |
| **median contrast vs the white header** | **1.43:1** | 2.38:1 |
| darkest 5% | 1.70:1 | 5.03:1 |
| **darkest pixel ANYWHERE in the mark** | **2.55:1** | **7.64:1** |
| WCAG non-text floor | 3.0:1 | 3.0:1 |

**The load-bearing row is the last one.** On websitepromotion, *no pixel anywhere in the entire mark
reaches 3:1*. It is not "mostly pale with legible edges" — there is no legible part. There is also a
0.69% magenta despill halo left by the background-removal step.

### 1a. The control, and why this is a VARIANCE defect rather than a constant

`seotools.co.uk`'s logo was generated by the **same pipeline, on the same day, hours apart**, and
reaches 7.64:1 on its darkest strokes. The measurement could have come out "every generated logo is
pale" — it did not.

That matters for §3: **the model can already produce a mark that reads on a white header, and
sometimes does.** So a prompt clause is the weakest remedy, because it addresses variance with
wording. What is missing is a check that looks at the picture.

### 1b. Reproducing the figure

sRGB relative luminance (WCAG 2.x), opaque pixels only (α>200), against the header's white:

```python
from PIL import Image
def lum(r,g,b):
    f=lambda c:(c/255)/12.92 if c/255<=0.03928 else (((c/255)+0.055)/1.055)**2.4
    return 0.2126*f(r)+0.7152*f(g)+0.0722*f(b)
im=Image.open('logo.png').convert('RGBA')
cs=sorted((1.05)/(lum(*p[:3])+0.05) for p in im.getdata() if p[3]>200)
print(f'median={cs[len(cs)//2]:.2f}  darkest5%={cs[int(len(cs)*0.95)]:.2f}  max={cs[-1]:.2f}')
```

⚠ Fetch the bytes from the **served URL** with a 404 invented-path control, and assert the byte
count — the `kubectl exec` base64 route truncates silently and PIL still reports the header
correctly off a truncated file (417's RUNBOOK, "Fetch a generated asset's BYTES").

**The white operand is MEASURED, not assumed** `[MEASURED 2026-09-03]`. Read off the served
`index.html`: `--color-header-bg: #ffffff`, and it is genuinely *consumed* — four
`background: var(--color-header-bg)` usages, one of them `var(--color-header-bg, var(--color-surface))`
whose fallback is `--color-surface: #FFFFFF`. So the backdrop is white on either the token or the
fallback path. This check matters because a custom property that is *defined* and never *applied*
reads identically in source (the estate has been caught by exactly that); the operand had to be
confirmed at the usage, not at the declaration.

## 2. Why nothing catches it — three citations, verified at HEAD 2026-09-03

1. **The audit's remit is text.** `platform/orchestration/actions/request_render_audit_action.go:4-5`
   states what it measures: *"text contrast against the effective background, images that failed to
   load, horizontal overflow."* An image's own luminance is not in the list.
2. **The finding it can file is keyed to a CSS selector and routed to a CSS fixer.**
   `write_render_audit_findings_action.go:12-13` files `contrast_failure` with dedup key
   `contrast_failure:<page-path>#<class>` at **`css-patch-agent`**. That is an element's *styling*,
   not an image's pixels. Repainting a class cannot fix a pale PNG.
3. **Images are handled broken / not-broken only.** `registry.go:1257`: *"attributed broken images →
   undeployed_asset/asset-deployer; over_image, overflow and unattributed images are counted,
   deliberately not filed."*

So a logo that is **generated, matted, stored, deployed, referenced and rendered** — every one of
which is checked — passes every gate while being unreadable.

### 2a. ⚠ The exclusion that leaves this uncovered is CORRECT, and its stated reason does not apply here

This is the part a reader should not skim, because it is the difference between "extend the
`over_image` path" (wrong) and "a new check" (right).

`write_render_audit_findings_action.go:36-37` gives the reason `over_image` findings are counted but
never filed:

> *"over_image contrast approximations — the adapter's own header calls the backdrop **unknown**;
> filing approximations at a fixer manufactures churn."*

That is a **sound** decision. Text sitting over an arbitrary photograph has no single backdrop
colour, so any ratio is an approximation, and filing approximations at an automated fixer would
generate churn.

**The logo case inverts every term of it.** The logo *is* the image, and its backdrop is the
header's own solid background colour — **known exactly**, from the site's own theme. Nothing is
approximated: both operands are available. So the reasoning that correctly excludes `over_image`
does not reach this case at all.

**This is a gap between two sound decisions, not a flaw in either.** The audit declines image
luminance because its remit is text; the findings writer declines image-adjacent contrast because
the backdrop is unknown. Neither is wrong. The logo falls between them.

## 3. Fix candidates, ranked by what makes the bad state UNREPRESENTABLE

Not ranked by cheapness — ranked by what closes the door.

**Candidate 1 — measure at store time and refuse, alongside the existing matte guard.** The logo
pipeline already computes a fail-closed statistic over the finished pixels and refuses rather than
storing a bad artefact: `internal/adapters/imagegenerator/dynamic_adapter.go:683` returns an error
when `stats.BorderKeyed < keyGroundMinBorderKeyed` (`= 0.95`, `:708`), with the statistic itself
computed in `keyground.go:209`. A contrast statistic
against the site's header background is the same shape, in the same place, with the same failure
mode — and the retry ladder already exists to re-roll. **This makes an illegible logo unstorable**,
which is the only candidate that does.
⚠ Needs the header's background colour at generation time; confirm it is reachable there before
committing to this (the theme is on the site, but check whether the adapter has it — if not, that
plumbing is the real cost of this candidate, and it is what a design review should weigh).
⚠ Coupled to `424`'s guard: two fail-closed statistics on one ladder can exhaust `max_attempts=3`
between them. `seotools` already needed 2 of 3 attempts to pass the transparency guard alone. Decide
the interaction deliberately — this is 424's open decision #3, and this candidate sharpens it.

**Candidate 2 — a post-hoc finding, routed to imagery rather than `css-patch-agent`.** A new
finding type (the mark's own luminance against a known solid backdrop, so NOT an `over_image`
approximation — see §2a) filed at a handler that can regenerate an image. Sees the defect; does not
prevent it. Weaker than 1 because the bad artefact still ships and is served until the loop closes.

**Candidate 3 — a prompt clause asking for a mark that reads against the header.** Cheapest, and
weakest: §1a shows the model can already do it and sometimes does, so this addresses variance with
wording. **Worth noting this is exactly the shape 417 was opened about** — a prompt instruction as a
substitute for a control. Not recommended alone; reasonable *alongside* 1.

## 4. How to verify a fix

1. **The negative case must fail.** websitepromotion's current logo (max 2.55:1) must be refused or
   flagged. If a candidate passes it, the candidate is wrong — this is the motivating case and the
   check must be re-run against it, not merely against fresh output.
2. **The positive control must pass.** seotools' 2026-09-03 mark (max 7.64:1) must not be refused;
   a check that rejects everything is not a check.
3. **Measure at the served bytes**, with a 404 invented-path control and an asserted byte count.
4. **Confirm the backdrop operand is the real one** — the header's actual rendered background, not
   an assumed white, and confirmed at the *usage* rather than the declaration (see §1b).
   websitepromotion's header resolves to `#ffffff` on both the token and the fallback path; **do not
   generalise that to other sites** — `--color-header-bg` is per-site theme output, and a dark header
   inverts which marks are legible.
