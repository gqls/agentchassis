Certifications

A Type II report covers a period of operation; a Type I describes intent at a moment. Ask for the report date, because an expired attestation tells you less than a recent one.

ISO 27001 covers information security in general; ISO/IEC 42001 is specific to managing AI systems. Neither is a promise about outcomes, but their absence means no external body has examined the process.

Sector rules bind you, not only your supplier. If your use case touches health, payment or public-sector data and the vendor cannot name the relevant certification, the compliance gap lands on you.

Data handling

Opt-out training policies have repeatedly become a point of dispute after the fact. Opt-in is the safer default because it makes the quiet path the private one.

Zero data retention means the request is not stored once the response is returned. Without it, how long your data lives is a policy commitment rather than a technical property.

Your data reaches whoever your supplier's suppliers are. A published sub-processor list is what makes that chain checkable rather than assumed.

A deletion right you cannot exercise is not a deletion right. Ask what the process is, and how long it takes, before the day you need it.

Governance and oversight

Automated decisions nobody can override tend to be discovered at the point they are already wrong. A named person in the loop is the cheapest correction mechanism there is.

If you cannot reconstruct why a system reached an output, you cannot explain it to a customer, a regulator or a court.

The useful commitment names a timescale and says who gets told. "We will notify you" without either is not something you can plan around.

Transparency

Undisclosed use is what erodes trust fastest when it surfaces later. Disclosure costs very little and is increasingly expected as a matter of course.

Published testing lets you judge where a system is weak. Its absence is not evidence of bias. It means nobody outside the vendor is in a position to tell.
