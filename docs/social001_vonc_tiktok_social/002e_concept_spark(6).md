# Spark — AI Game Master Social Platform

## Concept Label
**Spark** — AI-driven provocation engine where the world is the content and your take is the game.

## Positioning Statement
Not another social app. A **daily game powered by what's happening in the world**. You don't post. You don't scroll. You play. The AI finds the most interesting things happening right now and turns them into challenges. You respond. Others respond. The best responses win. You discover what kind of thinker you are. You find your people. You get better at seeing what others miss.

The word "game" is the differentiator. Games have rules, scores, rounds, winners. Games are fun. Games don't need your personal data or social graph to work. "What game are you playing today?" is fundamentally different from "what are you posting?" or "what are you watching?"

## The "Another Social App" Problem

### What's genuinely different (honest assessment)

**The AI game master role.** No platform has AI as orchestrator of human creativity rather than creator of content. Genuinely new but hard to communicate in a screenshot — you have to experience it.

**The "what's your take?" entry point.** Every other platform asks you to create from scratch or consume passively. Spark puts something in front of you and asks for a reaction. Lower barrier than any competitor.

**Ephemeral challenge structure.** Time-limited, game-structured, scored. Closer to a game show than a forum. Different from Reddit (permanent, unstructured, karma-based).

**Rooms not feeds.** Architecturally different from every major platform. Hard to communicate before someone tries it — "we don't have a feed" sounds like a missing feature until you experience room energy.

**Showcase + competitive dual mode.** TikTok is showcase only. Reddit is competitive only. Twitter tries both badly. Both under one roof with connecting mechanics is new.

### What's NOT different enough
If someone sees a text prompt with comments underneath — "this is Reddit." A feed of short videos with reactions — "this is TikTok." AI-generated content — "this is every AI app from 2024." The visual first impression in the first 3 seconds determines categorisation.

### The differentiation is experiential, not conceptual
Spark's difference is felt, not explained. The AI pushing back on your take. Room energy rising. Your response getting remixed. Getting an archetype label that's weirdly accurate. No marketing copy can do this justice. **The first visit experience IS the pitch.** The product sells itself in 60 seconds of use.

### Why now?
AI capability specifically enables this:
- Real-time provocation framing — cheap fast LLMs generate 2,000 candidates/day, select best 20. Previously required massive editorial team.
- AI sparring partner — genuinely new cold-start solution. Previous platforms could only fill empty rooms with fake accounts.
- Real-time synthesis — "Crowd Speaks," recaps, director's cut replays require processing hundreds of responses quickly. 2024/2025 capability.
- Behavioural profiling — archetype/niche detection from watching reactions and responses. Quality of insight much better with current models.

The game master role requires AI capabilities that only became cheap and fast enough in last 1-2 years. Couldn't have worked in 2020.

## Launch Strategy — Content as the Front Door

### The content-first approach
Don't launch Spark as a social platform. Launch vonc.com as a **content destination that happens to have interactive features.**

The scraping system and AI framing engine produce compelling content before any human participates:
- 15-20 fresh provocations per day on trending topics
- The AI's own take on each
- Mashup connections between unrelated trends
- Prediction prompts on real-world outcomes
- Data visualisations, weird stats, surprising facts

That's already a content site more interesting than most aggregators — framing is sharp and opinionated (provocateur, not journalist). Someone can land and just read. But at the bottom of every provocation: "What's your take?" The conversion point from reader to participant.

### The SEO play
Each provocation is a page with a URL. "Is [brand]'s new product genius or delusional?" ranks for searches about that brand. AI's take provides text content. User responses (when they arrive) provide fresh, unique, keyword-rich content search engines love. Over time, vonc.com becomes where you find interesting framings of current trends. The Reddit model of SEO growth but with AI-optimised framing.

### The social sharing play
Every provocation and response generates a shareable card — not "join Spark" but self-contained content. Card shows: provocation, top response, and a challenge: "47 people responded. Think you'd do better?"

How TikTok grew — not telling people to download TikTok, but making videos that appeared everywhere else. Spark's cards do the same for text/opinion content.

### Vertical focus vs broad appeal
vonc.com starts broad. Provocations naturally cluster into topics. Someone shares a food provocation card on a cooking Facebook group — people click through to a food challenge, see other food challenges, gravitate to that cluster. Their Spark experience is food-flavoured because that's how they arrived. Nobody feels prescribed.

Niche communities form organically (AI accelerates with niche detection). This is the Reddit model — nobody joins "Reddit," they find a specific subreddit through search or a shared link.

### The archetype as viral Trojan horse

The archetype system might be the most viral-ready feature, and it works with zero community.

Personality quizzes spread massively. BuzzFeed built a business on "What kind of [thing] are you?" Myers-Briggs is a cultural phenomenon. Hogwarts houses persist decades later. People love categorising themselves and comparing with friends.

The Daily Gauntlet produces an archetype that is:
- **Earned** through actual behaviour (not self-reported quiz answers)
- **Dynamic** (changes over time)
- **Multi-dimensional** ("Surgeon-Oracle with Wildcard tendencies")
- **Shareable** in a visually appealing card

"I just did the Spark Gauntlet and I'm a Surgeon-Wildcard. What are you?" — works on day one, one user, no community needed. Recipient clicks, does 5 minutes of provocations, gets their archetype, shares with their friends. Growth loop independent of social features.

**Archetypes could be the Trojan horse. Come for the personality quiz. Stay for the challenges. Return for the community.**

### What makes someone tell a friend?
People share things that are:
1. **Surprising** — "the AI argued back with a point I hadn't thought of" / "it guessed my personality from 5 responses"
2. **Self-flattering** — sharing a "Genius"-rated response, a correct prediction, a glow-up reel
3. **Useful** — "best summary of what's happening with [trend]" — multiple perspectives on current topics
4. **Identity-reinforcing** — "I'm a Surgeon type" — if archetypes become culturally recognisable like MBTI or Hogwarts houses

### First week content calendar (before anyone cares)
- **Daily:** 15-20 fresh provocations with AI takes (SEO + social sharing content)
- **Daily:** The Daily Gauntlet — produces archetype (viral personality quiz)
- **Daily:** 3-5 "weird stat" micro-content pieces (shareable, surprising, snackable)
- **Weekly:** One mashup connection (premium, newsletter/blog pickup potential)
- **Weekly:** A prediction market post (engagement + long-term return visits)

Content cadence makes vonc.com worth visiting as a traditional blog. Interactive elements are the conversion layer on top.

## Games, Daily Puzzles, and Gamification (to explore further)

**Daily puzzles and games as retention mechanics.** The Daily Gauntlet is already a daily puzzle of sorts. But there's a broader space to explore:

- **Wordle-style daily challenges** — one specific puzzle per day that everyone attempts, shareable results grid. Could be opinion-based ("rank these 5 things") or prediction-based ("guess the stat") or creative ("caption this in exactly 7 words")
- **Daily games tied to scraping output** — "higher or lower" with real stats the scraping system found, trivia from today's trends, "which of these two things happened more recently?"
- **Competitive puzzle formats** — timed challenges where speed matters, bracket-style elimination puzzles
- **Streak-based game mechanics** — daily participation streaks (Duolingo model), accuracy streaks (prediction runs), creativity streaks (days with a remixed response)
- **Seasonal game events** — monthly tournaments, themed challenge weeks, championship brackets
- **Mini-games as retention bridges** — lightweight play between major challenges, keeping people on-platform during quiet periods

Games and puzzles are potentially the strongest daily habit mechanism. The Gauntlet is the start but there's room for a whole ecosystem of daily play patterns that could rival dedicated puzzle apps (Wordle, NYT Games) while being tied to real-world content. **Explore this in detail — could be the primary retention driver.**

## What's In It For Users — The Motivation Hierarchy

### Tier 1: Identity and Status (the deep drivers)

**"I want to be known for something."** The human need to matter, to have a recognised place. Platform lets someone become "the person who's really good at minimalist cooking." Served by: archetypes, Stage showcases, glow-up mechanic, niche communities, emergent profiles.

**"I want to be seen as competent and respected."** Peer respect, not fame. 50 people in your niche thinking you're good. Served by: curator role, duel victories, prediction accuracy, mentor status, Rising scout reputation.

**"I want to lead and influence."** Setting direction without needing massive followings. Leadership through behaviour: a curator whose taste predicts Moments, someone whose takes get remixed into long chains, an effective mentor shaping the next generation.

### Tier 2: Belonging and Connection (the social drivers)

**"I want to find my people."** A small group who get it. Served by: niche discovery (AI brings people to you), crews, collab matchmaking, The Drift social signals. Key: people don't want to find a community — they want to be found by one.

**"I want to enjoy myself."** Entertainment, laughter, quick games, brief delight. The Drift, micro-games, "AI tried" format, reaction system, sound/haptics.

**"I want to pass time without feeling I wasted it."** Even passive engagement builds taste profile, prediction record, reaction streak. Daily Gauntlet as brain training not mindless scrolling.

**"I want to show friends cool things."** Social currency of discovery. Moments designed for sharing. Share format as content card, not app link. Sharer looks good for finding it.

### Tier 3: Growth and Learning (the aspirational drivers)

**"I want to get better at something."** Showcase, get feedback, see what others did, study breakdowns. Glow-up makes progress visible. Workshop for practice. Retention through skill development.

**"I want to stay current."** Cultural currency. Scraping-powered engine keeps content topical. Being active = being up to date. Social preparedness as utility.

**"I want to discover unexpected things."** Mashups, cross-niche challenges, complementary crew matching. Surprise as a feature.

### Tier 4: Financial Reward (the livelihood drivers)

**Revenue share on showcases.** Challenge-driven supply control prevents content mills.

**Subscription channels.** Premium breakdowns, private Stage rooms with personal feedback. Platform takes cut. Reputation system as credential.

**Collab marketplace.** Brands post sponsored Stage challenges. Creators selected on niche reputation, not follower count. Meritocratic sponsorship path.

**Prediction staking.** Reputation tokens, not money. Status value, not cash value.

**Expert consultations (vertical).** Domain experts offer paid sessions. Platform facilitates, takes cut.

**Principle:** Financial reward follows demonstrated value. Nobody buys prominence.

### The Motivation Mix

- **Casual visitor** — Tier 2. Drift, micro-games, reactions. May never enter a room.
- **Engaged participant** — Tiers 1+2. Rooms, Arena, Stage. Building profile.
- **Aspiring creator** — Tiers 1+2+3. Developing skill. Glow-up. Workshop.
- **Professional creator** — All tiers. Earning from reputation. Platform as livelihood.

Acquire at Tier 2. Natural progression does the rest.

## The User Journey

### The First Visit — Zero to One

#### First 5 seconds
No signup wall, no mission statement, no cookie banner dominating the screen. A provocation card. Something happening in the world right now, framed sharply, with a text input and a ticking timer. Underneath: "The AI's take" — a response that's decent but beatable. The "surely I can do better" baseline.

The provocation must be: immediately comprehensible (no jargon, no context), interesting enough to pause on, and with an obvious entry point ("I could say something about this").

#### First 30 seconds — they type something
No account needed. They type, hit enter. Two things happen: their response appears with a small animation and the "launched" sound. Then the AI responds after a brief 2-3 second pause — a counter-argument or follow-up question. "Interesting — but what about [angle you didn't consider]?" Not a chatbot conversation. A sparring move.

#### First 2 minutes — the loop
They respond to the AI. AI pushes back. 2-3 exchanges. Then a subtle notification: "4 other people are responding to this right now." A number, not usernames. Scrolling reveals other responses with reaction buttons. They haven't signed up but they've created content, had a conversation, and can see other humans engaging.

#### First 5 minutes — the soft ask
After the exchange: a card showing today's other active provocations (lobby in miniature). And: "Want to save your responses? Create a free account in 10 seconds." Minimal — email or social login. No profile, no preferences, no wizard. Account just saves what they've done. If they don't create one, their response still exists. No punishment for anonymous participation.

#### What they leave with
An experience. They wrote something, were challenged, saw others doing the same. If the provocation was good, they carry the question: "what IS the right answer?" That thought brings people back.

### Days 1-3: The Discovery Phase

They return. The page remembers them. Today's provocations plus: "Yesterday's challenge closed. Your response got 7 reactions. Want to see how it ranked?" That's a pull.

They see challenge results — top responses, AI recap, their position (15th of 40). Not great, not embarrassing. They can see what top responses did differently. Learning without being taught.

#### Finding their mode (gradual introduction)
- **Day 1:** Arena only. Provocation-response-sparring loop.
- **Day 2:** They notice a Stage room in the lobby — different colour, different energy. Enter out of curiosity. Instead of "what's your take?" it's "show us something." Clicks for makers, less so for analysts. Now they've seen both modes.
- **Day 3:** Daily Gauntlet surfaces — "5 provocations, 5 minutes, discover your Spark type." Positioned as game, not tutorial. Completing it gives first archetype label: "You're an emerging Surgeon — precise, analytical, cuts to the core." Identity hook for return.

#### What the platform learns (silently)
- Which provocations made them engage vs scroll past
- Arena vs Stage lean
- Response style — long/short, analytical/creative, fast/considered
- Reaction patterns — generous or sparing with "Genius"? Heavy on "Cursed"?
- Visit times, session length, return triggers

This seeds the emergent profile. Not shown to them yet — too early. But shapes what comes next.

### Week 1: The Habit-Forming Phase

Goal: establish at least one habitual behaviour.

#### Daily Gauntlet as anchor habit
Short (5 min), scored, produces personality insight. 3 Gauntlets in a row = streak. Streaks are psychologically powerful — breaking one feels like losing something. By Day 5, Gauntlet is tuned to their interests. Scoring gets nuanced: "Response speed on fashion prompts is 3x faster than tech. Originality peaks on food topics."

#### Engineered social moments

**First real reaction.** Someone reacts to their response. Notification, animation. "Someone reacted 'Genius' to your take." First social validation. Tiny but disproportionately important. Platform may gently prioritise showing new users' responses to active reactors.

**First "you're not alone" moment.** Platform shows similar profiles exist. "12 people on Spark have a similar profile to yours." Not a connection push — just awareness. Curiosity planted.

**First chain participation.** Someone remixes their response. "Someone built on your take." They see their idea transformed — pushed further. Stronger than a reaction because it implies creative value. "I started something."

#### Discovering depth (through exploration, not instruction)
- Tap someone's response, see their emergent profile, realise they have one too
- Notice the Prediction Arcade, try a prediction
- Enter a Stage room, see "Teach Me" reactions and breakdowns
- See Rising section spotlighting newcomers like them
- Browse The Drift during a commute

Platform reveals itself through use. Each discovery feels like finding a hidden room. Reward for curiosity.

### Weeks 2-4: The Investment Phase

Habits established. Now: deepen investment.

#### Profile becomes meaningful
"Joined 12 days ago. 18 challenges (14 Arena, 4 Stage). Archetype: The Surgeon. Strongest: food, fashion. Contrarian 68%. Response speed: top 30%. 2 chains started. Prediction accuracy: 55%. AI says: 'Precise thinker who finds structural flaws. Could take more creative risks.'"

Shareable. A conversation piece. They might deliberately try non-contrarian takes to see if it shifts. Platform as lens for self-understanding.

#### Niche gravity
Platform has identified their interests. Niche Stage rooms appear: "Tonight's food challenge: impress us using only pantry staples." They find 20 people who care about food as much as they do. Showcases that inspire. Breakdowns that teach. First "Teach Me" reaction on their own response.

This is the "found by a community" moment. Doesn't feel like joining a group — feels like realising you were already part of one. Niche becomes home base.

#### First "I'm getting better" moment
Glow-up tracker has enough data. "Your first food showcase got 4 reactions. Most recent: 23. Here's the trajectory." Seeing own improvement separates casual from committed. Same psychology as fitness trackers.

#### Social bonds forming
Same names in niche rooms. Recognisable styles. Shared crews. Remixed each other's work. Social graph forming through repeated proximity in shared contexts. Platform surfaces: "You and [username] have been in 6 challenges this week. You complement each other — analytical + creative. Want to collab for tomorrow's food Stage?"

### Months 2-3: The Commitment Phase

Regular user. Developed profile, established niche, recurring collaborators, prediction track record, visible glow-up.

#### Cross-niche exploration
AI suggests outside comfort zone: "You dominate food challenges. Tonight's design challenge has a similar structure — constraint-based creativity. Try it?" User discovers unexpected strengths. Archetype evolves: "The Surgeon → The Surgeon-Wildcard."

#### Mentorship path
Experienced enough that newer users look up to them. Breakdowns get "Teach Me" reactions. Invited to mentor in niche rooms. AI facilitates: "3 newcomers this week respond to themes you explored in month 1. Your breakdown could help." Teaching deepens own understanding.

#### Recognition plateau and what sustains engagement
Novelty worn off. Habits established. Platform provides satisfying answers:
- **Serialised challenges** — "I predicted this 3 months ago, reveal is next week"
- **Glow-up milestones** — "Your 50th showcase. Here's your journey"
- **New formats** — untried format rotates in
- **Competitive seasons** — monthly/quarterly resets with cumulative achievements
- **Tier 4 visibility** — "Creators at your level earn £X from brand collabs this month." Aspiration planted, not pushed.

### Month 6+: The Established User

Rich history. Profile tells a story. Glow-up reel worth sharing. Network of collaborators. People tune in. Possibly earning from platform.

**They'd feel genuine loss if Spark disappeared.** Not because of money — because of identity built, community found, skills developed, archive of creative journey. Deepest form of retention.

This depth isn't reserved for top 1% of creators. Archetypes, niches, glow-up tracking, curator role give everyone meaningful identity — from daily Drift browser with sharp taste to Stage regular with 50 showcases.

### The Journey as Design Principle

At no point does the user feel onboarded, trained, funnelled, or manipulated. Every step feels like their own choice. Platform reveals through use, not instruction.

**The sequence: intrigue → creation → validation → habit → identity → community → mastery → purpose.**

Each stage builds on the previous. At each stage, the user could stop and feel satisfied. Someone doing only Daily Gauntlet + Drift is complete. Someone with a subscription channel is differently complete. The platform accommodates both without making either feel wrong.

## Two Modes: Arena and Stage

Two complementary energies in parallel. Not separate — they feed each other.

### Arena — The Competitive Game
Provocations, challenges, duels, roasts. "What's your take?" Combative, analytical. Sharpest response wins.

### Stage — The Showcase
Springboard challenges, creative demonstrations. "Show us what you've got." Creative, aspirational. Most impressive creation wins.

### How They Feed Each Other
- Stage showcase → controversial → becomes Arena provocation
- Arena winner in a domain → AI suggests Stage showcase
- Daily Gauntlet alternates both modes
- Lobby shows both — Arena pulsing, Stage glowing

### The Positive Flywheel
Arena is reactive (external stimulus). Stage is generative (creations become stimulus). Over time, platform generates its own culture — techniques, styles, movements originating on Spark. Deeper retention than reacting to other platforms.

## Shared Mechanics

### Rooms Not Feeds
**Lobby** — 3-5 rooms, energy indicators, format badges, time remaining, hottest preview. Browsing the lobby is its own entertainment.

**Room zones** — Floor (active) and Gallery (spectating, reacting). Zero barrier between them.

Challenges expire. Recaps are lasting artifact. Disposable = low barrier to participation.

### Director's Cut Replay
AI produces compressed replay of challenge. Shareable spectator content.

### Serialised Challenges
Span weeks. Real-world scenario, community predicts, reality unfolds. Appointment viewing.

### Multi-Format Rotation
Monday: text. Tuesday: image. Wednesday: audio. Thursday: caption AI image. Friday: video. Weekend: free-for-all.

### Predictions and Time Capsules
3/6/12 month predictions. Monthly reveals. "Bold but wrong" badge.

### "The Crowd Speaks"
AI synthesises all responses into composite collective voice.

### Moments
Remarkable outcomes elevated to permanent, shareable record. Growth mechanic.

## AI Role: Producer, Not Performer

AI is stage, lighting, director — never actor. Humans are talent. When something is funny or impressive, a human did it.

## AI Cost Architecture

### Design Principle
Most AI cost is fixed (content production scales with challenges, not users). Per-user interactive AI is the only linearly scaling cost and needs throttling by design.

### The Always-Running Background Layer

| Layer | What it does | Model | Volume | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Raw Feed | Scrapers pull trends/inspiration | Not AI | N/A | Existing infrastructure |
| 2. Framing Engine | 5-10 provocations per scraped item | Mistral/Llama 8B CPU | ~2,000 calls/day, ~1M tokens | Pennies (own hardware) |
| 3. Curation Gate | Pick best 15-20 for the day | Llama 70B GPU or human | 50-100 calls/day | Moderate, low volume |
| 4. Mashup Engine | Non-obvious connections | Foundation (Claude/GPT-4) | 2-5 calls/day | Negligible |
| 5. Serialisation | Narrative thread tracking | Database + cheap model | 1-5 calls/day | Negligible |
| 6. Niche Detector | Cluster similar showcasers | Embeddings + ML | Periodic batch | Low |

**Background total: ~1-2M tokens/day cheap local + handful of foundation calls. Under £5/day at scale.**

### Per-Challenge Cost (~15-20 challenges/day)

| Feature | Calls/challenge | Model | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Room hosting | 5-10 | Cheap local | Mostly templated |
| Recap | 1 | Medium (Llama 70B) | 2-5K tokens in, 500 out |
| Crowd synthesis | 1 | Medium | Similar to recaps |
| Director's Cut | 1 | Cheap local | Structured, not creative |

### Per-User Cost (the scaling dimension)

| Feature | Calls/user/day | Model | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI sparring | 3-5 per session | Cheap local | **Highest** — linear with users |
| Gauntlet scoring | 5 per Gauntlet | Cheap local | High volume, batch-friendly |
| Profile updates | 1 (nightly batch) | Cheap/ML | Low |
| Glow-ups, mentor, collab | Occasional | Cheap local | Low |

### Scaling Scenarios

| Scale | Cheap local tokens/day | Foundation cost/day | Infrastructure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 DAU | ~1.25M | ~20p | Existing hardware |
| 5,000 DAU | ~15M | ~£1 | 2-3 CPU or 1 GPU instance |
| 100,000 DAU | ~200-300M | ~£5-10 | 10-20 CPU or 3-5 GPU instances |

### Cost per user at scale: £0.003-0.008/day (0.3p-0.8p)

A £3-5/month subscription covers AI costs many times over. At 5% conversion: £15,000/month revenue against £15,000-24,000/month compute. Break-even on subscriptions before advertising or brand revenue. Comparable to Reddit's ~$0.01-0.02/DAU.

### What's cheap (fixed, don't scale with users)
Provocation engine, per-challenge costs, reputation/archetypes, reaction system, crowd energy, leaderboards.

### What to watch (variable, scale with users)
AI sparring (full LLM call per interaction), Gauntlet scoring at volume (batch-friendly mitigation).

### Sparring cost management
Rate limit (3 exchanges then "AI rests"), smallest viable model (3B may suffice), cache common counter-arguments, transition away as community grows.

## Provocation Engine — Technical Layers

1. **Raw Feed** — scrapers pull trends, inspiration. Social-interest, not newspaper news.
2. **Framing Engine** (Mistral/Llama CPU) — 5-10 candidates per item. Bulk.
3. **Curation Gate** (Llama 70B GPU / human initially) — picks sharpest. Learns from engagement.
4. **Mashup Engine** (foundation models, infrequent) — non-obvious connections. Premium.
5. **Serialisation Tracker** — narrative threads. Mostly database.
6. **Niche Detector** (Stage) — clusters, rooms, collabs, glow-ups.
