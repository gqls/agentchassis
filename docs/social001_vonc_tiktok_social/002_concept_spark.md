# Spark — AI Game Master Social Platform

## Concept Label
**Spark** — AI-driven provocation engine where the world is the content and your take is the game.

## The Problem With Current Platforms

TikTok nailed removing the decision of what to watch next — swipe or stay. But it's passive consumption. The creator/consumer divide is massive. Content is ephemeral in the worst way: you see something brilliant, can never find it again, and it didn't *do* anything except entertain you for 15 seconds.

## Psychological Hooks

- **Unpredictability of reward** — slot machine psychology, but with multiple kinds of unexpected reward
- **Micro-status games** — frequent, achievable small wins rather than demoralising follower counts
- **The IKEA effect** — people value things more when they contributed. No platform has a "I made this 20% better" mechanic
- **Collective effervescence** — the feeling of being part of something happening right now with other people
- **Curiosity gaps** — the brain can't resist an open question

## Core Mechanic: Challenge-Response Loops

The scraping engine monitors the world constantly — social trends, product launches, fashion shifts, weird happenings, cultural moments. AI doesn't report these. It **frames them as provocations**:

- "Brand X just did this. Roast it or defend it in under 30 seconds."
- "This product claims to do Y. Prove it's genius or prove it's stupid."
- "Two contradictory things happened today. Pick a side."
- "This CEO said [quote]. What they actually meant was ___."
- "This chart says everything is fine. What's it hiding?"

AI generates the prompt, sets constraints, sometimes generates a first response to seed the thread.

## The Feed Experience

Open the app. See a provocation card with a countdown timer — this challenge is live for the next 2 hours. You can:

### React
One-tap reactions, not likes. Opinionated: "Genius / Delusional / Suspicious / Based / Cursed". Fun to tap. Data-rich.

### Respond
30-second video, image, text, meme. Tight constraints always. Tight constraints breed creativity.

### Remix
Take someone else's response and build on it, subvert it, flip it. Remix chain is visible — watch an idea mutate through 8 people.

### Predict
Not money — reputation tokens. "I think this take will age well." Testing social intelligence.

### Passive Viewing
The feed works for passive browsing too — see what's happening, watch challenges unfold, read responses without contributing. The mix of passive accessibility and active participation is the draw. You can drift through watching and then get pulled into something that hooks you.

## Rooms Not Feeds — The Arena Metaphor

Every major social platform uses infinite scroll. Content comes to you in a stream. You're a passenger. This is optimised for passive consumption and it's why every platform eventually feels the same.

### The Lobby
When you open Spark, you see **the lobby** — 3-5 active challenge rooms, each with:
- Visual energy indicator (how many people, how fast responses are coming)
- Format badge (text/image/audio/video)
- Time remaining
- One-line preview of the hottest response so far

You choose which room to enter. Or you browse the lobby itself — watching the energy of rooms rise and fall is its own passive entertainment. Like being in a pub watching the quiz from the bar vs joining a team.

### Why Rooms Change Everything

**Commitment.** Entering a room primes you to participate. Scrolling primes you to judge and move on.

**FOMO that works for you.** You can see rooms you're not in going wild. Pull to come back.

**Atmosphere.** Each room has its own visual and audio character. Dark backgrounds with sharp accents for competitive challenges. Warm tones for creative ones. Ambient crowd hum that gets louder as more responses come in. The difference between a website and an experience.

**Natural capacity.** A room with 50-200 people is vibrant. Too big? Split into parallel rooms — Arena A and Arena B — with a finals round where the best from each compete. Tournament structure emerges naturally.

### Crowd Energy Feedback

The platform shows collective energy in real time:
- A challenge card starts cool/blue when it drops
- As responses pour in, it heats up — colours shift, animations intensify
- A response getting a wave of reactions creates a visible ripple
- Two responses neck and neck? The card splits — tug-of-war animation
- A response gets 50 reactions in 30 seconds? The whole room "shakes"

Game design applied to social. Every multiplayer game does this. Social platforms just increment a number.

### The AI Host — Always In The Room
When a room is quiet or new, the AI is the host warming up the crowd. Not trying to be funny — setting the stage. "Three people here. First response just dropped. Room's heating up." Live structural narration. When a good response lands, the AI calls it out. Sports commentator for creativity. Structural commentary plays to AI's strengths.

## The Empty Room Problem — Cold Start Design

This is the make-or-break question. Every community platform has died here. You land, nobody's talking, you leave, and because you left there's even less reason for the next person to stay. Death spiral from zero.

### The Wrong Approach
Fake accounts, bot activity, astroturfing. People can tell. It feels hollow.

### The Right Question
**What's valuable to a single person, alone, with no community at all?**

### First 10 Seconds — Before They Know What Spark Is

Someone lands on vonc.com. No context. No pitch. They need something immediately engaging that doesn't depend on other humans.

The first thing they see: a **single provocation card, aimed directly at them.** Sourced from today's actual trends, framed sharply:

> "A company just patented the smell of rain. Your reaction in one sentence."

No signup required. No context needed. Just a box to type in. The provocation is interesting enough that a reasonable person thinks "...actually, I do have a take on that." They type. They hit enter.

**Now they're invested.** They've created something. The IKEA effect kicks in immediately.

### The AI Sparring Partner

When there's no community, the AI steps up — not as a fake crowd, but as a **sparring partner**. Transparently AI, engaging with the response directly:

"Interesting angle. But here's the counterargument — [genuine counterpoint]. Still standing by your take?"

A one-on-one with an AI that's pushing back on your opinion. More engaging than typical AI chat because it's adversarial in a playful way. It's not trying to help. It's trying to challenge.

Everything produced — response, counter-response, exchange — is stored. The next person arriving ten minutes later sees the exchange. They can react, respond, remix. The first person was the community seed without knowing it.

### Spark Solo — The Solo Experience As Its Own Mode

Not a degraded community experience. A distinct, compelling mode:

**The Daily Gauntlet.** Five provocations, one after another. You respond. AI pushes back. Scored on response time, originality of angle (compared to predicted common response), consistency across five. End result: a profile. "You're a contrarian-analytical type. Fastest on tech, slowest on culture." People love learning about themselves — it's why personality quizzes go viral.

**The Prediction Arcade.** No community needed. AI surfaces real trends: "This product launched last month. Three possible outcomes for 3 months from now. Pick one." Build a prediction portfolio solo. Compete against yourself and the base rate.

**The Remix Sandbox.** AI generates a deliberately mediocre take. Your job: make it better. AI scores the improvement. "You took an AI-generated meme from 'mildly amusing' to 'actually sharp' — 4x improvement." Trains the core Spark skill.

**The "Beat the AI" Archive.** Every provocation today, with the AI's own response attempt. Try to beat it. When community exists, community judges. For now, you vs the machine.

### The Solo-to-Social Transition

Should feel like a room gradually filling, not a switch flipping.

You finish a Daily Gauntlet provocation. Underneath your response, instead of just AI's counter, a small note: "2 other people responded to this today. Want to see their takes?" You tap. Two real responses. You react. Now you're in a micro-community without realising the transition happened.

As more people arrive, solo mechanics become **warm-up modes**:
- Daily Gauntlet → morning routine before live challenges
- Prediction Arcade → long-game layer alongside ephemeral rooms
- Remix Sandbox → workshop mode

Nothing gets thrown away. Solo experience *evolves into* community experience.

### The Self-Filling Content Flywheel

Even with zero users, vonc.com isn't empty:
- Scraping system runs 24/7 — always fresh provocation material
- AI has been "playing" in empty rooms — its own responses, clearly labelled as AI
- "The AI's take" visible as baseline — humans can react, disagree, improve

AI responses serve triple duty:
1. Fill visual space so nothing feels dead
2. A provocation themselves — "surely I can do better than that"
3. Establish tone — showing by example what the platform values

No deception. The platform is honest: "The AI started this conversation. Humans are making it better. Want to help?"

### The Invite Mechanic — Challenge, Not Invitation

When someone has a good solo session, make sharing trivially easy. But the share isn't "join Spark." It's "look at this exchange I had with the AI about [topic]. Think you could do better?"

Share as a challenge, not an invitation. Recipient clicks, sees provocation, sees original response, wants to prove they can top it. Now they're in.

### Scale Thresholds — Each Phase Feels Complete

- **1 person:** Solo mode is the product. AI sparring. Daily Gauntlet. Predictions. Fully functional.
- **5-20 people:** Micro-community. Few responses per provocation. Reactions start mattering. Still intimate.
- **50-200 people:** A single challenge room is vibrant. Chains happen. Duels possible. Social mechanics kick in.
- **500+ people:** Multiple simultaneous rooms. Crews. Tournaments. Full vision.

Each phase feels complete, not incomplete. At 5 people it should feel great with 5, not "waiting for 500."

### The Landing Page — Day 1

Not an explanation. Not "welcome to Spark." Nobody reads that.

A single provocation card, filling the screen. Today's sharpest framing. A text input below. A timer ticking.

That's it. Type your take or scroll down to see the AI's attempt and today's other provocations.

**Engage first. Understand second. Commit third.**

## Ephemeral By Design

Challenges expire. Best responses are crowned. The whole thing fades. But the AI generates a **recap** — a curated synthesis of what the community produced. Recaps are the lasting artifact. Not individual posts, but collective output.

Nobody feels precious about their content. It's disposable by design. You're not building a portfolio, you're playing a game. Massively lowers the barrier to participation.

## AI Role: Producer, Not Performer

### The Core Principle
The AI is the **stage, the lighting, and the director** — never the actor. Humans are always the talent. AI builds the arena around them.

Genuinely different positioning from every AI-social product. Everyone else tries to make AI the creator. Spark makes AI the **producer**. Producers don't go on stage.

### AI's Visible Surface Area (what it does)
- The provocation card (well-framed questions)
- The countdown and rules (structural)
- The scoreboard and reputation (data processing)
- The recap (synthesis, not commentary)
- The mashup connections (pattern recognition)
- The escalation decisions (when to extend, bracket, spawn)
- The room host narration (structural, not humorous)
- Crowd synthesis ("here's what the crowd collectively thinks")
- Crew suggestions and matchmaking
- Duel referee and "tale of the tape" comparisons
- Solo sparring partner (during cold start / solo mode)
- Daily Gauntlet scoring and profiling

### AI Is Deliberately Absent From
- The responses
- The humour
- The personality
- The takes

When something is funny on Spark, a human did it. No uncanny valley.

## The AI Slop Problem

### The danger
AI trying and failing to be funny or creative is worse than no AI at all. Users smell AI slop instantly.

### Strategy: restrict AI to genuine strengths

**AI is great at:**
- Structuring provocations — framing real events as compelling challenges
- Data synthesis — pulling together stats, trends, contradictions
- Pattern recognition — non-obvious connections between unrelated events
- Curation and ranking — surfacing quality
- Neutral summarisation — recapping without editorialising
- Constraint design — setting constraints that bring out human creativity
- Real-time scorekeeping
- Format and template generation
- Crowd response synthesis — distilling hundreds of responses into a coherent collective view
- Adversarial sparring — pushing back on opinions with counterarguments

**AI is bad at (keep it away from):**
- Being funny
- Hot takes requiring cultural intuition
- Sarcasm, irony, deadpan
- Memes that are supposed to be genuinely funny
- Anything that's supposed to feel spontaneous or authentic

### Clever use of AI slop
- **Deliberately bad AI takes as seeds** — "Here's what the AI thinks. Surely you can do better." Sloppiness becomes provocation. AI is the straight man.
- **"AI tried" as a format** — show what AI generated, let humans roast it. Self-aware platforms are more likeable.
- **Quality gating** — AI generates many provocations, curation layer filters aggressively. High volume generation, brutal selection.

## Radical Anonymity

### During Challenges
No usernames visible during a challenge. You see response quality. You react to content, not reputation. Only after the challenge closes do identities get revealed.

Same principle as blind auditions in orchestras — dramatically increases diversity of outcomes. Genuinely meritocratic. Newcomers can win immediately.

### Reputation Without Advantage
Streaks, titles, prediction records accumulate. But they don't give algorithmic boost. Bragging rights, not unfair advantage.

### Optional Reveal
After a challenge, choose to reveal or stay anonymous. "Oh, that was the same person who won Tuesday" is more interesting than knowing in advance.

## Key Mechanics

### Chain Reactions
Visible, rewarded remix chains. Someone responds, someone riffs, someone riffs on the riff. Chain displays as visual timeline. Everyone shares credit. The chain itself is the content. Entry points at every stage.

### Duels
Any two participants agree to one-on-one. 60 seconds each. Room votes. Winner takes reputation. Creates rotating heroes. AI role: referee, neutral summary, "tale of the tape" comparison card.

### "What Happens Next?" — Serialised Challenges
Challenges spanning weeks. AI sets up real-world scenario, community predicts, reality unfolds, next round launches. Soap opera built from reality. Appointment viewing. AI generates "previously on..." recaps.

### Misfit Mashup
AI mashes two unrelated trending things, community makes sense of the collision. Plays to AI's genuine pattern-recognition strength. Human responses supply humour and insight.

### Multi-Format Rotation
- **Monday:** Text-only (280 chars)
- **Tuesday:** Image-only (no captions)
- **Wednesday:** Audio-only — voice notes
- **Thursday:** "Caption this AI-generated image"
- **Friday:** Video (30 seconds max)
- **Weekend:** Free-for-all / community-voted format

### Crews
Temporary 3-5 person teams within a challenge. Brainstorm together, post collective response. Dissolves after. Escape room energy. AI suggests compositions based on complementary styles.

### Time Capsule Predictions
AI surfaces trends, community predicts 3/6/12 months out. Monthly revelation events. "Bold but wrong" is its own fun badge. Archive becomes content.

### "The Crowd Speaks"
AI synthesises all responses into a composite collective voice. Shown alongside top individual. "Agree with the crowd or the top individual?" Makes everyone feel they influenced the collective.

## The Lurker On-Ramp

### Judging Is Participation
After challenges close, random users judge — pick top 3. Judging builds reputation: "Sharp eye" or "Contrarian judge." 30 seconds, meaningful.

### Prediction Without Creation
Before responses open, predict what kind of response will win. Meta-game without creating.

### Reaction Streaks
Reacting builds a profile. "You react 'Suspicious' more than 90% of users." Passive consumption becomes identity-building.

## The Provocation Engine — Technical Layers

### Layer 1: Raw Feed
Scrapers pull trends, product launches, social interest shifts, cultural moments, weird data points. High-volume, low-curation. Not newspaper news — social-interest material.

### Layer 2: Framing Engine (cheap models — Mistral/Llama)
Each raw item generates 5-10 candidate provocations. Different angles, formats, combativeness levels. Bulk generation.

### Layer 3: Curation Gate (stronger model or human initially)
Picks the sharpest candidates. Selection criteria: "will this provoke interesting responses?" Best provocations are slightly unfair, slightly reductive — that's what makes people want to correct, riff, or outdo. Learns from engagement data over time.

### Layer 4: Mashup Engine (foundation models, infrequent)
Looks across raw feed for non-obvious connections. Expensive models, sparingly — a few calls per day. Premium content.

### Layer 5: Serialisation Tracker
Maintains narrative threads for ongoing stories. Knows when next episode is due. Mostly database work with occasional summarisation.

**Cost structure:** Layers 1-3 on cheap local models, scalable. Layer 4 on foundation models, sparingly. Layer 5 mostly database. Human curation fills gaps early.

## Vertical Integration

Core mechanics stay the same. Flavour tunes per vertical:

- **Vet/pet:** "My cat does this weird thing — explain it." Caption pet scans. Wholesome tone.
- **Finance:** Prediction-heavy. Bulls vs bears duels. Analytical status.
- **Fashion:** Image-dominant. "Style this AI-generated outfit for real life." Remix chains adding accessories.
- **Food:** Recipe challenges with constraints. "This restaurant put X on the menu — genius or crime?"
- **vonc.com:** The unconstrained general arena. Any topic, any trend. Where all mechanics get tested before vertical deployment.

## Content Tone

- Interesting, not dark. Slightly adult, never gruesome, violent, or xxx
- Social interest trends and fashions rather than newspaper news
- Curation is a top priority — quality over volume
- Competitive and predictive but no money betting
- Games and gameplay between challenges

## The Key Differentiator

Most platforms ask "what do you want to share?" Spark asks **"what's your take on this?"**

You don't need an idea. You need an opinion. Everyone has opinions.

The AI isn't a utility — it's the **game master**. It sets the board, keeps score, escalates, provokes. A role AI hasn't occupied in social platforms yet.

## Cost Considerations (future)

- GPU Llama for mid-tier tasks, cost-constrained
- Foundation LLMs for infrequent high-intelligence tasks (mashups, complex synthesis)
