# Spark — AI Game Master Social Platform

## Concept Label
**Spark** — AI-driven provocation engine where the world is the content and your take is the game.

## The Problem With Current Platforms

TikTok nailed removing the decision of what to watch next — swipe or stay. But it's passive consumption. The creator/consumer divide is massive. Content is ephemeral in the worst way: you see something brilliant, can never find it again, and it didn't *do* anything except entertain you for 15 seconds.

## Psychological Hooks

- **Unpredictability of reward** — slot machine psychology, but with multiple kinds of unexpected reward
- **Micro-status games** — frequent, achievable small wins rather than demoralising follower counts
- **The IKEA effect** — people value things more when they contributed. No platform has a "I made this 20% better" mechanic
- **Collective effervescence** — the feeling of being part of something happening right now with other people
- **Curiosity gaps** — the brain can't resist an open question
- **Identity construction** — people use platforms to figure out who they are publicly, to have "a thing," to be known for something
- **The showcase instinct** — the desire to be seen, to demonstrate skill and taste, to find your people

## Two Modes: Arena and Stage

Spark has two complementary energies running in parallel. Not separate apps — two modes within the same platform that feed each other.

### Arena — The Competitive Game
Provocations, challenges, duels, roasts. AI frames real-world events as adversarial prompts. "What's your take?" Energy is combative and analytical. The sharpest response wins.

### Stage — The Showcase
Springboard challenges, creative demonstrations, skill showcases. AI frames real-world inspiration as creative prompts. "Show us what you've got." Energy is creative and aspirational. The most impressive creation wins.

### How They Feed Each Other
- Someone showcases a cooking creation in Stage → it's impressive but controversial → becomes an Arena provocation: "This breaks every traditional rule. Genius or pretentious?"
- Someone wins Arena fashion challenges repeatedly → AI notices: "You've won 3 fashion challenges. Have you considered showcasing in Fashion Stage?"
- Daily Gauntlet (solo mode) alternates Arena-style opinion prompts and Stage-style creation prompts — you discover which mode fits naturally
- The lobby shows both — Arena rooms pulsing with combative energy, Stage rooms glowing with creative energy. Different vibes, same community, same mechanics

### The Positive Flywheel
Arena is reactive — something happens, you react. Engaging but dependent on external stimulus. Stage is generative — people create new things, those creations become stimulus for others. Over time, the platform starts generating its own culture. Cooking techniques, visual styles, aesthetic movements that originate on Spark. A platform that generates its own culture has deeper retention than one that reacts to other platforms.

## Arena Mechanics

### Core: Challenge-Response Loops
The scraping engine monitors social trends, product launches, fashion shifts, cultural moments. AI frames them as provocations:

- "Brand X just did this. Roast it or defend it in under 30 seconds."
- "This product claims to do Y. Prove it's genius or prove it's stupid."
- "Two contradictory things happened today. Pick a side."
- "This CEO said [quote]. What they actually meant was ___."
- "This chart says everything is fine. What's it hiding?"

### Arena Reactions
Opinionated, judgement-oriented: **"Genius / Delusional / Suspicious / Based / Cursed"**

### Chain Reactions
Visible, rewarded remix chains. Someone responds, someone riffs, someone riffs on the riff. Chain displays as visual timeline. Everyone shares credit. Entry points at every stage.

### Duels
Two participants, 60 seconds each, room votes. Winner takes reputation. AI role: referee, neutral summary, "tale of the tape" comparison card.

### Misfit Mashup
AI mashes two unrelated trending things, community makes sense of the collision. Plays to AI's genuine pattern-recognition strength.

## Stage Mechanics

### Core: Showcase Challenges
The scraping system finds inspiration material — beautiful architecture, new cooking techniques, emerging music, fashion collections, new technology. AI frames these as springboards, not battles:

- "This architect designed a building with no straight lines. Show us your most creative space."
- "This chef is doing unexpected things with fermentation. Show us your most adventurous kitchen creation."
- "This new sound is emerging from [place]. Make something with it — 30 seconds."
- "Autumn is hitting differently this year. Show us your version of it."
- "This designer made a bag from recycled ocean plastic. Show us sustainable style that actually looks good."

Arena asks for opinions. Stage asks for **creations and demonstrations**. The response isn't a take — it's a showcase of skill, taste, creativity, or personality.

### Stage Reactions
Positive and aspirational: **"Fire / Inspired / Stealing This / Teach Me / Vibe"**

- "Stealing This" — highest compliment in creator culture: "this is so good I want to incorporate it into my own life"
- "Teach Me" — opens a door for the creator to share more (see Mentorship below)

### Tune In (Stage-specific follow mechanic)
When someone showcases something you love, you can **"tune in"** — a signal you want to see their future showcases. Different from traditional following:
- Tuning in doesn't guarantee visibility — AI still curates based on quality
- Prevents celebrity hierarchy — 10,000 tune-ins doesn't mean automatic feed placement
- Tune-in count is visible but not dominant — one signal among many
- Profile leads with what you've showcased, not follower count

### Niche Discovery — "Find Your Corner"
AI watches showcase patterns and identifies clusters: "30 people regularly showcase minimalist cooking. They don't know each other yet." AI creates a **dedicated Stage room** for that niche — a space where similar creators showcase to each other.

"You all care about minimalist cooking. Tonight's Stage challenge: the most impressive meal using exactly 3 ingredients. Go."

Niches form through active facilitation, not passive algorithm. Not "you might also like" but "here are your people, now create together."

### The "Glow-Up" Mechanic — Progress as Content
Spark tracks showcase history and surfaces evolution: "6 months ago, your first photography showcase got 3 reactions. Yesterday's got 200. Here's your journey."

The platform generates a **glow-up reel** — curated compilation of showcases over time showing development. Rewards persistence. Shareable — "look how far I've come" is one of the most engaging narratives on any platform. AI is genuinely good at tracking longitudinal patterns and surfacing milestones.

### Mentorship Loops — "Teach Me" Becomes Real
When 12 people react with "Teach Me," the creator gets notified and can post a **breakdown** — behind-the-scenes of their showcase. How they made it, tools, thinking.

AI helps structure breakdowns: "People specifically want to know about your lighting technique" (based on attention patterns). AI as producer — helping creators understand what their audience wants to learn.

Over time, a knowledge layer builds beneath the showcase layer. Not just "look at this cool thing" but "here's how you could do it too." Turns admirers into learners who then showcase their own attempts. The flywheel spins.

### Collaborative Showcases — Collabs
Two or more people combine skills. A photographer and a stylist. A musician and a visual artist. A cook and a food photographer.

AI matchmaker suggests based on complementary skills: "Your food photography is strong. This person's plating is incredible. Want to collab on tonight's food showcase?"

The collab process is visible — people watch creators building together in real time. On TikTok, collaboration process is invisible. On Spark, making-of becomes content.

### "Rising" Feed — Spotlighting Newcomers
Dedicated section exclusively showcasing people in their first week or who've never hit a reaction threshold. AI curates carefully — not every first-timer, but those showing genuine spark.

Being "Rising'd" becomes a rite of passage. Gives early users immediate taste of being seen — the single most addictive creator platform experience.

### The Taste Graph — Curation as Self-Expression
Reaction history across Stage showcases builds a **taste profile**: "This user consistently identifies breakout creators before anyone else." "This user's 'Fire' reactions correlate 70% with what later becomes a Moment."

People with demonstrated good taste become visible **curators**. Their reactions carry visible weight (a response with a curator's "Fire" gets a small badge). Curators earn their own reputation track.

Gives non-creators a prestigious role. Gallery owners, A&R people, editors, talent scouts — respected for taste, not creation. No social platform has replicated that. You don't have to make things to be important on Spark. You can be the person who finds things.

## Shared Mechanics (both Arena and Stage)

### Rooms Not Feeds — The Arena/Stage Metaphor

#### The Lobby
When you open Spark, the lobby shows 3-5 active rooms — Arena rooms pulsing with combative energy, Stage rooms glowing with creative energy. Each shows:
- Visual energy indicator (people count, response velocity)
- Mode badge (Arena/Stage) and format badge (text/image/audio/video)
- Time remaining
- One-line preview of hottest response

You choose. Or browse the lobby itself — watching energy rise and fall is its own passive entertainment. Like being in a pub watching the quiz from the bar vs joining a team.

#### Why Rooms Change Everything
- **Commitment.** Entering primes participation. Scrolling primes judgement.
- **FOMO.** Visible rooms going wild that you're not in.
- **Atmosphere.** Each room has visual and audio character. Ambient crowd hum scaling with occupancy.
- **Natural capacity.** 50-200 is vibrant. Too big? Split into parallel rooms with finals round.

#### Crowd Energy Feedback
- Challenge card starts cool/blue, heats up as responses pour in
- Reactions create visible ripples
- Neck and neck responses: tug-of-war animation
- 50 reactions in 30 seconds: the room "shakes"

#### Room Zones: Floor and Gallery
- **Floor:** Active participants — responding, remixing, duelling, showcasing
- **Gallery:** Spectators — watching, reacting (reactions still count), scouting
- Zero barrier between gallery and floor — step in when something hooks you

### Ephemeral By Design
Challenges expire. Best responses crowned. Everything fades. AI generates a **recap** — curated synthesis. Recaps are the lasting artifact. Nobody feels precious. It's disposable by design. You're playing a game, not building a portfolio.

### "What Happens Next?" — Serialised Challenges
Span weeks. AI sets up real-world scenario, community predicts, reality unfolds, next round launches. Soap opera built from reality. Appointment viewing. "Previously on..." recaps.

### Multi-Format Rotation
- **Monday:** Text-only (280 chars)
- **Tuesday:** Image-only (no captions)
- **Wednesday:** Audio-only — voice notes
- **Thursday:** "Caption this AI-generated image"
- **Friday:** Video (30 seconds max)
- **Weekend:** Free-for-all / community-voted format

### Time Capsule Predictions
AI surfaces trends, community predicts 3/6/12 months out. Monthly revelation events. "Bold but wrong" is its own fun badge.

### "The Crowd Speaks"
AI synthesises all responses into a composite collective voice. Alongside top individual. "Agree with the crowd or the top individual?"

### Moments — When Something Transcends
When a challenge produces something remarkable, AI elevates it into a **Moment** — own page, permanent record, shareable. Growth mechanic: outsiders think "I want to be in that room."

### Director's Cut Replay
After a challenge, AI produces a compressed curated replay of how it unfolded — key turning points, duel highlights, chain virality, prediction reveals. Spectator content that works after the fact. Shareable. AI is strong at this synthesis.

## Radical Anonymity (Arena-specific)

During Arena challenges, no usernames visible. React to content, not reputation. Identities revealed after. Blind audition principle. Optional reveal. Reputation accumulates but doesn't give algorithmic boost.

Stage mode works differently — identity is part of the showcase. You're putting yourself forward. But tune-in counts don't dominate, and the Rising feed ensures newcomers get seen.

## Identity and Profiles

### Emergent Profiles
Your profile isn't something you create — it **emerges from behaviour**. No bio to write. Instead:

"Joined 3 weeks ago. 47 challenges entered. Strongest in tech provocations. Contrarian 72% of the time. 3 duel wins. Prediction accuracy: 61%. Stage showcases: 12, mostly food photography. Rising'd in week 1. The AI says: 'Finds the hidden angle others miss but occasionally overthinks simple questions.'"

A mirror. You learn about yourself. Evolves constantly. Can't be faked or curated like an Instagram bio.

### Type Archetypes (emerge from data, not assigned)
- "The Surgeon" — precise, analytical, strips arguments to core
- "The Wildcard" — unpredictable, highest remix rate
- "The Oracle" — quiet but accurate predictor, rare but impactful responses
- "The Catalyst" — responses generate longest chains
- "The Judge" — mostly spectates, reaction pattern most predictive of outcomes
- "The Maker" — Stage-dominant, consistent showcaser, growing following
- "The Scout" — curator type, identifies breakout creators early
- "The Mentor" — breakdowns are among the most saved content

Archetypes shift over time. Become a thing people discuss outside the platform — organic growth.

## The Drift — Passive Content Stream

A mode for when you don't have energy to enter a room. A stream of:
- Sharpest responses from today's challenges (good standalone)
- Weird facts from the scraping system
- Micro-predictions (2-second tap votes)
- Remix chain highlights compressed to single visual
- Showcase glow-up highlights
- "AI tried" moments — AI's failed attempts as entertainment
- Countdown teasers for upcoming challenges

The Drift is Spark's TikTok-style feed — passive, entertaining, low-commitment. Everything pulls from or pushes toward active mechanics. A good response has an "enter the room" link. The Drift is the snack.

### Engagement Depth Spectrum
1. **The Drift** — passive scroll, snackable, 30 seconds to 5 minutes
2. **The Lobby** — active watching, room energy as entertainment, 5-10 minutes
3. **Room Gallery** — in the room spectating and reacting, 10-20 minutes
4. **Room Floor** — actively responding, remixing, duelling, showcasing, 20+ minutes
5. **Solo modes** — Daily Gauntlet, Prediction Arcade, Sandbox, any duration

Each level is complete in itself. Each has natural gravity pulling toward the next.

## AI Role: Producer, Not Performer

### The Core Principle
The AI is the **stage, the lighting, and the director** — never the actor. Humans are always the talent. AI builds the arena around them. Producer, not performer.

### AI's Visible Surface Area
- Provocation and springboard framing (Arena and Stage)
- Countdown, rules, format constraints (structural)
- Scoreboard and reputation (data processing)
- Recaps and director's cut replays (synthesis)
- Mashup connections (pattern recognition)
- Escalation decisions (extend, bracket, spawn)
- Room host narration (structural commentary)
- Crowd synthesis
- Niche detection and room creation (Stage)
- Crew/collab matchmaking suggestions
- Duel referee, "tale of the tape"
- Glow-up tracking and milestone surfacing (Stage)
- Mentor prompt structuring (Stage)
- Rising curation (Stage)
- Solo sparring partner (cold start)
- Daily Gauntlet scoring and profiling

### AI Is Deliberately Absent From
- The responses and showcases
- The humour
- The personality and takes
- Creative content meant to stand on its own

When something is funny, impressive, or beautiful on Spark — a human did it.

## The AI Slop Problem

### The danger
AI trying and failing to be funny or creative is worse than no AI at all. Users smell AI slop instantly.

### Strategy: restrict AI to genuine strengths
**Great at:** structuring provocations, data synthesis, pattern recognition, curation and ranking, neutral summarisation, constraint design, scorekeeping, crowd synthesis, adversarial sparring, longitudinal tracking, matchmaking.

**Bad at (keep away):** being funny, hot takes, sarcasm/irony, memes meant to be genuinely funny, anything meant to feel spontaneous or authentic.

### Clever use of AI slop
- Deliberately bad AI takes as challenge seeds — AI as straight man
- "AI tried" as a format — let humans roast it
- Quality gating — high volume generation, brutal curation selection

## The Empty Room Problem — Cold Start Design

### The Right Question
What's valuable to a single person, alone, with no community at all?

### First 10 Seconds
A single provocation card filling the screen. Today's sharpest framing. A text input. A timer. No signup needed. **Engage first. Understand second. Commit third.**

### AI Sparring Partner
No community? AI steps up as transparent sparring partner. Pushes back on opinions. Not fake crowd — clearly AI. Everything produced is stored for next arrivals.

### Spark Solo
**Daily Gauntlet** — 5 provocations, AI pushback, scored on speed/originality/consistency. Generates personality profile.
**Prediction Arcade** — solo prediction portfolio against base rates.
**Remix Sandbox** — improve AI's mediocre attempts, scored on improvement.
**"Beat the AI" Archive** — every provocation with AI's attempt. Try to beat it.

### Solo-to-Social Transition
Room gradually fills, not a switch. "2 others responded today. Want to see?" Solo mechanics become warm-up modes as community grows. Nothing thrown away.

### Self-Filling Content Flywheel
Scraping runs 24/7. AI "plays" in empty rooms — clearly labelled baseline responses. Triple duty: fill space, provoke improvement, set tone. Honest: "AI started this. Humans make it better."

### Invite as Challenge
Share isn't "join Spark" — it's "think you can beat my response to this?" Challenge, not invitation.

### Scale Thresholds (each phase feels complete)
- **1 person:** Solo mode. Fully functional.
- **5-20:** Micro-community. Reactions matter.
- **50-200:** Vibrant single room. Chains and duels.
- **500+:** Multiple rooms. Crews. Tournaments.

## Moderation — Provocative Without Toxic

### AI Referee
In-room AI enforces rules as game master, not censor. Yellow card framing: "Play the ball, not the person." Dim-and-flag rather than delete. Community votes on edge cases. Room temperature management.

### Positive Selection
Highlight clever/funny/insightful responses prominently. Shape "good" by example rather than policing "bad." Incentive structure does the moderation work.

### Provocation Design as Moderation
Framing controls tone. "This person is an idiot — agree?" invites attacks. "This decision will look genius or catastrophic — which?" invites analysis. Curation gate filters for quality of engagement, not just volume. A/B test framings against toxicity rates.

### Ephemeral Nature Limits Damage
Bad responses in a 2-hour challenge don't get indexed or shared. Only Moments get shared, and those are curated. Short half-life on toxicity.

### "Cursed" as Community Signal
One-tap "Cursed" reaction on toxic content. Accumulates, sinks. No argument, no pile-on, no troll-feeding engagement.

## The Provocation Engine — Technical Layers

### Layer 1: Raw Feed
Scrapers pull trends, launches, social shifts, cultural moments, inspiration material. Not newspaper news — social-interest material. Feeds both Arena (controversies, debates) and Stage (inspiration, emerging techniques, beautiful things).

### Layer 2: Framing Engine (cheap models — Mistral/Llama)
Each item generates 5-10 candidate provocations (Arena) and springboards (Stage). Different angles, formats, intensity levels. Bulk generation.

### Layer 3: Curation Gate (stronger model or human initially)
Picks sharpest candidates. Arena criteria: "will this provoke interesting responses?" Stage criteria: "will this inspire impressive creations?" Learns from engagement data.

### Layer 4: Mashup Engine (foundation models, infrequent)
Non-obvious connections across raw feed. Expensive models, sparingly. Premium content.

### Layer 5: Serialisation Tracker
Narrative threads for ongoing stories. Mostly database work.

### Layer 6: Niche Detector (Stage-specific)
Identifies clusters of similar showcasers. Creates dedicated niche rooms. Suggests collabs. Tracks glow-ups.

**Cost structure:** Layers 1-3 on cheap local models. Layer 4 on foundation models, sparingly. Layers 5-6 mostly database. Human curation fills gaps early.

## Content Tone

- Interesting, not dark. Slightly adult, never gruesome, violent, or xxx
- Social interest trends and fashions rather than newspaper news
- Curation is a top priority — quality over volume
- Competitive and predictive but no money betting
- Positive and aspirational alongside sharp and analytical
- Games and gameplay between challenges

## The Key Differentiator

Most platforms ask "what do you want to share?" Spark asks **"what's your take on this?"** and **"show us what you've got."**

You don't need an original idea. You need an opinion or a creation. Everyone has both.

The AI isn't a utility — it's the **game master and producer**. It sets the board, keeps score, escalates, provokes, inspires, scouts, matches, curates. A role AI hasn't occupied in social platforms yet.
