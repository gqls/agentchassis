# Domain Content Strategy Framework

## Purpose

A systematic approach to determining what content each domain needs to compete with the best sites in its niche. The difference between a £50/month content site and a £500/month one is almost entirely about whether the content answers what visitors actually need — and that varies completely by domain.

This framework applies to any domain and produces a concrete content plan tailored to the specific niche, audience, and competitive landscape.

---

## The Three Layers

### Layer 1: What Is This Site?

These questions establish the fundamental nature of the domain and who visits it.

**1. Who types this keyword or finds this site?**

Not "everyone interested in [topic]" — specifically, is it a business buyer, a consumer, a researcher, a professional? The answer shapes everything. A B2B audience expects data, specifications, and supplier information. A consumer audience expects clarity, trustworthiness, and ease of comparison.

**2. What do they want to DO when they arrive?**

The core intent question. There are really only a few options:

- Find a provider or supplier
- Compare options
- Get a price or quote
- Learn how something works
- Solve a specific problem
- Make a purchase decision

The domain name itself usually reveals the intent. "Wholesalers" implies finding suppliers. "Comparison" implies comparing options. "Guide" implies learning.

**3. What would make them leave satisfied?**

This is the question most AI content misses entirely. A visitor looking for suppliers leaves satisfied if they found potential suppliers they can contact. A visitor comparing options leaves satisfied if they feel confident about which to choose. Generic informational content fails this test if the visitor came with a transactional intent.

**4. How does this niche make money?**

Not the site — the niche itself. Understanding the money flow tells you where your monetisation fits and what a lead or referral is worth. High-value B2B transactions mean lead generation is lucrative. Consumer services with lower transaction values mean volume and affiliate commissions matter more.

---

### Layer 2: What Content Does It Need?

These questions determine the site structure and page topics.

**5. What are the top 5 sites currently ranking for these keywords, and what pages do they have?**

This is research, not copying. If the top 3 results all have a supplier directory, a pricing guide, and industry news — those are table-stakes pages you need. If none of them have a contract comparison tool, that's your opportunity.

**6. What does the buying/decision journey look like?**

Every visitor is somewhere on a path:

- Awareness: "Do I need this? What is this?"
- Research: "Who are the options? What should I look for?"
- Comparison: "Which option is best for my situation?"
- Action: "Contact this provider / buy this product"

You need content for each stage, but the later stages (comparison and action) are where the money is.

**7. What questions do people actually ask in this niche?**

Not what you think they ask — what they demonstrably search for. Sources include:

- "People also ask" boxes in Google
- Industry forums and Reddit threads
- Trade publications and professional bodies
- Autocomplete suggestions
- Answer sites like Quora

These real questions become your content topics.

**8. What would make this site the one people bookmark?**

The authority question. A site becomes bookmarkable when it has recurring value — updated pricing data, a genuinely useful tool, a directory that stays current, or content that evolves with the industry. Static content gets visited once; recurring value builds traffic over time.

---

### Layer 3: What Makes Each Page Compete?

These questions apply to every individual page.

**9. What is the single best page on the internet for this topic, and why?**

Before writing any page, know what you're competing against. If the top result is a government statistics page with live data, you're not going to outrank it with a blog post. But if the top result is a thin article from 2019, you absolutely can beat it.

**10. What can this page contain that others don't?**

The differentiator. Options include:

- Original data (even aggregated or calculated)
- Comparison tables with real numbers
- Interactive tools (calculators, finders, postcode lookups)
- Expert quotes or professional input
- Case studies and worked examples
- Current pricing or availability information

The page doesn't need all of these, but it needs at least one thing that isn't just reworded information from elsewhere.

**11. What format best serves the intent?**

- Pricing comparison → table
- "How to choose" guide → structured prose with criteria
- Supplier/provider directory → filterable list
- Process explanation → step-by-step with visuals
- Cost breakdown → calculator or worked example

Forcing every page into the same blog-post format is a common mistake. Match the format to what the visitor actually needs.

**12. What should the visitor do next?**

Every page needs a natural next action. On a supplier listing page: contact the supplier, request a quote, compare with another. On a "how to choose" guide: use the comparison tool, read reviews, check pricing. This serves both user experience and monetisation.

## The Generic Question Set

For systematic use across any domain, these 15 questions produce a complete content brief:

1. What industry or niche does this domain name represent?
2. Who is the primary visitor — consumer, business buyer, professional, researcher?
3. What is their primary intent — find a provider, compare options, get pricing, learn, solve a problem?
4. What would make a visitor leave this site satisfied?
5. What are the top 5 competing sites for the main keywords, and what content do they have?
6. What content do all top competitors share (table stakes)?
7. What content is missing from competitors (opportunity)?
8. What is the money flow in this niche — who pays whom, and how much?
9. What monetisation model fits — lead gen, affiliate, advertising, directory listings, or a combination?
10. What are the 8-15 pages this site needs, and what is the intent of each page?
11. For each page: what format best serves the intent (comparison table, guide, directory, tool, calculator)?
12. For each page: what original element can this page offer that existing results don't?
13. What is the recurring-value hook that makes this site worth revisiting?
14. What is the seasonal pattern of demand in this niche? Some niches have strong seasonal cycles (heating/energy peaks in autumn, pet insurance spikes after Christmas puppy season, garden services peak in spring). This affects when to publish content, when to expect traffic, and how to interpret revenue fluctuations. A site that launches in the off-season may look like it's failing when it's actually just waiting for the demand cycle. Understanding the cycle also lets you publish seasonal content ahead of the demand spike, giving it time to index before traffic arrives.
15. What is the trust threshold for this niche? Some niches (finance, health, legal, insurance) require content that demonstrates genuine expertise and authority — Google's E-E-A-T standards hit these niches hardest. A gas wholesaler comparison needs to show it understands commercial energy contracts. A vet comparison needs to show it understands animal health. Thin, generic AI content will be penalised more heavily in these high-trust niches than in lower-stakes ones like home décor or hobby sites. The trust threshold determines how much research depth each page needs, whether you need expert review or quotes, and whether the site needs an "about" page with credible authorship.

Questions 5-7 require actual competitive research — looking at what's ranking and what's missing. A pipeline that just generates content from the domain name alone will produce generic output. A pipeline that researches the competitive landscape first will produce content that actually competes.

The questions that most separate a £300/month site from a £30/month site are 7, 12, 13, and 15 — finding what's missing, adding something original, giving people a reason to come back, and matching the trust level the niche demands.

## Example 1: gaswholesalers.com

### Layer 1: What Is This Site?

**Visitor:** B2B buyers — facility managers, business owners, energy procurement managers. They want to find and compare wholesale gas suppliers, understand pricing, and negotiate better contracts.

**Intent:** Find suppliers, compare pricing, understand contracts.

**Leaves satisfied when:** They've identified 2-3 suppliers to contact, understand current market pricing, and know what to look for in a contract.

**Money flow:** Gas supply contracts are high-value (£10K-500K/year per business). Energy brokers earn commission as a pence-per-kWh uplift on contracts, typically 0.05p to 2p per kWh. For a mid-size business using 200,000 kWh/year, a broker's annual commission at 0.5p/kWh would be £1,000. This means brokers will pay for qualified leads — a raw lead (name, business, postcode) is worth roughly £10-25, while a qualified lead with meter data and contract end date is worth £30-60.

### Layer 2: Content Plan

**Competitive landscape:** The top competitors in business energy comparison (Bionic, Utility Bidder, etc.) are large generalist operations covering gas, electricity, water, and telecoms. A site focused purely on gas wholesaling can go deeper on that specific topic than the generalists' gas sections.

**Decision journey:**

- Awareness: "What is gas wholesaling? Do I need a wholesaler vs a standard supplier?"
- Research: "Who are the main UK gas wholesalers? What should I look for?"
- Comparison: "Which wholesaler offers the best terms for my volume and business type?"
- Action: "Contact this supplier / request a quote"

**Content the site needs:**

| Page | Intent | Format |
|------|--------|--------|
| UK Gas Wholesaler Directory | Find suppliers | Filterable list by region, contract type, business size |
| Current Wholesale Gas Prices | Check market rates | Data table, updated regularly (the recurring-value hook) |
| How to Switch Business Gas Supplier | Process guidance | Step-by-step guide with timeline |
| Gas Contract Comparison Guide | Understand options | Comparison table: fixed vs variable, contract length, exit fees |
| Gas Supply for Restaurants | Industry-specific guidance | Guide with worked cost examples |
| Gas Supply for Manufacturing | Industry-specific guidance | Guide with volume considerations |
| Gas Supply for Office Buildings | Industry-specific guidance | Guide with typical usage profiles |
| Business Gas Safety Regulations | Compliance information | Reference guide with links to official sources |
| Commercial Gas Meter Requirements | Technical information | Explainer with meter types and when each applies |
| How to Read Your Business Gas Bill | Problem-solving | Annotated bill walkthrough |
| Gas Wholesale Market Outlook | Industry context | Quarterly or monthly analysis |
| Get Quotes from Gas Wholesalers | Conversion/action | Lead generation form |

**What makes it compete:** An updated pricing page with wholesale market rates, a genuine supplier directory with verified information, and detailed contract comparison content would be more useful than the generalist comparison sites' gas sections. The niche focus is the advantage.

**Original elements:** Current wholesale pricing data (even if semi-manual updates), a contract cost calculator ("enter your annual usage, see estimated costs across wholesalers"), industry-specific consumption benchmarks.

### Monetisation

The primary model is lead generation — "get quotes from gas wholesalers" forms that send enquiries to energy brokers or suppliers. Energy brokers earn their commission as a pence-per-kWh uplift on contracts (typically 0.05p to 2p per kWh). For a business using 200,000 kWh/year, a broker's annual commission at 0.5p/kWh would be £1,000. This means brokers will pay for qualified leads.

Lead values depend on quality. A raw form submission (name, business, postcode) is worth roughly £10-25 to a broker. A qualified lead with meter number, consumption data, and contract end date is worth £30-60. The higher-value leads require more sophisticated forms but convert better for the broker.

Realistic traffic and revenue projections:

| Timeframe | Monthly Visits | Leads/Month | Revenue/Month |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|---------------|
| Months 1-6 | 200-1,000 | 5-15 | £75-375 |
| Months 6-12 | 1,000-3,000 | 15-40 | £225-1,000 |
| Months 12-18 | 3,000-8,000 | 40-80 | £600-2,000 |

These assume a 1-2% form submission rate and an average lead value of £15-25. The range is wide because it depends heavily on how well the content ranks and how targeted the traffic is.

At a steady state of £600-1,200/month profit, using current market multiples of 24-32× monthly profit (content/affiliate sites averaged around 24× on Empire Flippers in 2025, with well-run sites reaching 30-35×), the site would be valued at approximately £14,000-38,000. Not the £42,000 initially estimated, but still a strong return on a domain that cost very little to develop.

An alternative model is to partner with existing comparison services (Bionic, Utility Bidder, Uswitch Business) as an affiliate, earning per completed switch rather than per raw lead. This can be more lucrative per conversion but requires higher-intent traffic.

## Example 2: vetcomparison.uk

### Layer 1: What Is This Site?

**Visitor:** Pet owners looking to choose or switch vets. Mostly consumers, some searching for specific services (dental, surgery, emergency). This is an emotional purchase — it's about their pet's health.

**Intent:** Compare vet practices, understand costs, feel confident about choosing the right vet.

**Leaves satisfied when:** They feel confident about which vet to use, understand what they should expect to pay, and know what to look for in a good practice.

**Money flow:** Vet practices spend £500-5,000/month on marketing. Pet insurance companies run affiliate programmes with verified UK payouts: Petplan pays £35 per purchase, PDSA pays £15 per sale, and most other UK programmes pay £20-35 per signup. Pet product affiliates (food, supplements, accessories) add modest supplementary revenue at 5-15% commission on sales.

### Layer 2: Content Plan

**Competitive landscape:** Most vet comparison sites are either directories with no real comparison data, or insurance-focused sites that happen to list vets. A site with genuine pricing transparency (even ranges), real user reviews, and service-level comparisons would be substantially more useful. The emotional angle matters — content about choosing a vet for a new puppy, or finding a specialist for an older pet, connects with how people actually think about this.

**Decision journey:**

- Awareness: "I need a vet — what should I look for?"
- Research: "What vets are near me? What do they offer? What do they charge?"
- Comparison: "Which vet is best for my pet's specific needs?"
- Action: "Book an appointment / register with this practice"

**Content the site needs:**

| Page | Intent | Format |
|------|--------|--------|
| Vet Practice Directory/Comparison | Find and compare vets | Filterable directory by area, with services, pricing ranges, hours, emergency availability |
| How to Choose a Vet | Decision guidance | Comprehensive guide with criteria checklist |
| How Much Does It Cost to Neuter a Cat/Dog | Pricing query (heavily searched) | Pricing table by region with factors that affect cost |
| Dog Vaccination Costs UK | Pricing query | Schedule of vaccinations with typical costs |
| Emergency Vet Call-Out Fees | Pricing query (urgent intent) | Cost guide with what to expect |
| Best Vets for Exotic Pets | Specialist search | Directory filtered to exotic-capable practices |
| Vets With 24-Hour Emergency Services | Specific need | Directory filtered to emergency availability |
| Vets That Offer Payment Plans | Specific need | Directory filtered to payment options |
| Pet Insurance Comparison | High-value affiliate content | Comparison table of UK pet insurance providers |
| Best Vets in [City] Pages | Local search intent | Location-specific comparison pages (Manchester, London, Birmingham, etc.) |
| Breed-Specific Health Guides | Informational, leads to vet search | Health considerations by breed, linking to "find a specialist" |
| What to Expect at Your First Vet Visit | New pet owner guidance | Process walkthrough for new pet owners |

**What makes it compete:** Genuine pricing transparency (even ranges), which most vet sites avoid. Real service-level comparison data rather than just listings. Content that addresses the emotional dimension of choosing someone to care for your pet's health.

**Original elements:** A pricing comparison tool ("what should I expect to pay for [procedure] in [area]?"), a "vet finder" by specific need (exotic pets, 24-hour emergency, payment plans), and community-sourced pricing data that improves over time.

### Monetisation

The primary revenue driver is pet insurance affiliate commissions. Verified UK programme rates include: Petplan pays £35 per purchase, PDSA pays £15 per sale, and most other UK pet insurance programmes pay £20-35 per signup. These are confirmed rates from affiliate networks including Awin.

Realistic traffic and revenue projections:

| Timeframe | Monthly Visits | Insurance Signups | Insurance Revenue | Other Affiliate | Total/Month |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Months 1-6 | 300-1,500 | 1-8 | £25-280 | £10-50 | £35-330 |
| Months 6-12 | 1,500-5,000 | 8-25 | £200-875 | £50-200 | £250-1,075 |
| Months 12-18 | 5,000-15,000 | 25-75 | £625-2,625 | £150-500 | £775-3,125 |

These assume a 0.5-1% conversion rate from visit to insurance signup, which is typical for comparison/review content with clear calls to action. "Other affiliate" includes pet food, supplements, and accessories which add modest supplementary income.

Additional revenue from vet practice listing fees (£30-100/month for premium/featured placements) is possible once the site has meaningful traffic, but this requires direct sales effort and is harder to automate.

At a steady state of £500-1,500/month, using current market multiples of 24-32× monthly profit, the site would be valued at approximately £12,000-48,000. The wide range reflects the difference between a site with modest local traffic and one that ranks nationally for high-value terms like "pet insurance comparison" or "vet costs UK".

Note: The upper end of this range (£30K+) requires the site to rank well for competitive national keywords. Local vet comparison terms are easier to rank for but generate less traffic per page. A combination of national insurance comparison content and city-specific vet directories gives the best overall traffic profile.

## Pipeline Integration Notes

For the content generation pipeline, the 15-question framework should feed into the briefing/research phase of site creation. The key architectural points:

**Questions 1-4** can be largely inferred from the domain name by an LLM with appropriate prompting, though edge cases exist where the same domain could serve different audiences.

**Questions 5-7** require actual competitive research — these need a research agent that can examine what's currently ranking and identify both table-stakes content and gaps. This is where the pipeline adds genuine value over manual work.

**Questions 8-9** require niche knowledge that an LLM can provide but should be verified against real affiliate programme data and lead generation rates.

**Questions 10-13** are the content planning output — these become the site architecture brief that the content creation agents work from.

**Questions 14-15** are strategic context that should inform content timing, depth, and style. Seasonal patterns affect launch timing and revenue expectations. Trust thresholds determine how much research depth and authority signalling each page needs.

The quality of the final site depends heavily on the quality of the answers to questions 5-7, 12-13, and 15. These are where genuine research and original thinking happen, and where the pipeline should invest the most effort.
