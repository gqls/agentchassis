# SUMMARY 2026-08-15 — the plan is complete, four decisions are yours, and the first live case has arrived

Third summary in this lane. The first (08-12) reported why the copy reads wrong. The
second (08-14) reported the first delivery — the house voice shipping. This one reports
the execution plan finished, and the lane pausing at a set of decisions rather than at a
piece of work. Written to be read aloud.

## What we're trying to do

Have the framework write copy that reads as though an intelligent person wrote it, for
every site, without a human rewriting pages by hand. The lane's founding diagnosis still
holds: the faults were in the process — briefs, specs and examples ordering the wrong
thing — not in the writing model, and corrections had nowhere to land that reached more
than one page.

## Where we've come from

The lane opened on the 12th from a rejected homepage and spent its first day discovering
that most of what it planned to build already existed unused: an undelivered voice
decision, a shared voice carrier with one consumer, and a sister thread whose evening of
corrections had quietly superseded the voice that decision would have shipped. The 13th
and 14th turned that research into delivery: you read two candidate texts, watched a real
writer produce copy under the newer one on a locked page, and approved it.

## What we've done

Everything on the approved plan is now delivered or deliberately stopped at a gate.

The approved voice is live for every writer in the fleet, from one place, verified by
reading the prompt a real writer actually received rather than by trusting the
configuration. Every prior text is backed up and restorable with one command.

The audit mislabelling bug was fixed end to end by a concurrent thread — the same design
this lane derived, plus the deeper half that makes the mistake impossible to repeat
silently — reviewed, shipped and proven on both live replicas.

The three lending sites carrying the "authoritative" tone setting were read against the
mechanism that produced the negativity elsewhere. The verdict split: two of them have
earned their confrontational frame, because their reader's errand is a dispute with a
lender, and they should be left alone. One — the loan calculator site — carries the
latent shape in two stored fields, including a model sentence you rejected a week ago
still listed as characteristic.

The claims register for the calculator site was drafted and validated as far as the
tooling allows, then deliberately not applied, because the thread that owns that site was
mid-rewrite and a live register would have filed findings against pages in motion.
Drafting it found three things that resize the step: the fleet-wide banned-claims
protection already covers sites with no register; the one genuinely new protection the
register would add cannot be rehearsed offline with today's tools; and the most valuable
first content is not the site's own figures but the stamp-duty facts already verified
against government sources for the mortgage site — which would also catch a live wrong
figure the calculator site is serving today.

And the first live case for the new voice arrived on its own: you flagged the web design
site's homepage as sounding machine-written, and the offending construction turns out to
be one the platform already names as a tell and the new voice bans twice over. The same
page also turned out to render the same section twice — traced within the hour, by the
thread that reported it, to the page's stored plan, where the section is listed twice.
That duplication gave us an accidental experiment worth having: the same section, filled
twice by the writer with identical inputs, came back with two of four card texts
identical and two reworded. Which is exactly why the lane's acceptance checks compare
facts, links and structure rather than prose against prose — a prose comparison would
fail a page that is perfectly fine.

## Where we are now

The lane is paused on decisions, not on work. Four are yours:

1. Whether to restate the loan calculator site's stored value proposition so it stops
   promising to reveal what lenders withhold.
2. Whether to replace its "reveal the true cost of credit" model sentence, which writers
   will otherwise keep imitating.
3. How to source the £5,000-to-£7,000 figure in the claims register — external evidence,
   honest self-labelling, or opening the register with the verified stamp-duty facts
   instead.
4. Whether to build the second-stage editorial pass now, in parallel with the review
   surface being built in another thread, or wait for that surface to exist.

The web design site's copy fix is parked behind the duplicate-section bug by design —
fixing the words before the structure would destroy the diagnostic state and leave one of
the twin sections serving old copy.

## Where we're going

Once you answer, the order is set: the two one-field spec edits are minutes of work; the
register applies after the other thread's rewrite lands, opening with the stamp-duty
facts if you take the recommendation; and the editorial pass gets built against its
committed proof case — the six missing homepage links you ruled should wait for it — with
every design constraint now measured rather than assumed. The voice itself needs nothing
from anyone: the next page built on any site without its own voice specification will be
its next test, and the first correction you want made to it is one edit that reaches the
whole fleet.
