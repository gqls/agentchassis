# Every defect this workstream found, and what would stop it happening in the first place

**Written 2026-07-29** at the owner's request: *"list all the other problems you found
in bugs_open with the aim of not making these problems happen in the first place."*

Organised by **prevention lever**, not by symptom. Grouping by symptom produces a list
nobody acts on, because each entry reads as a one-off. Grouping by lever shows that
eighteen defects are about six mistakes, and that four of the six are cheap to close.

Each entry names its concrete instances and where they are filed. **Where a claim is
unmeasured it says so** — several prevention estimates below are judgement, not data,
and marking them is the point.

---

## Lever 1 — A fix must name the population it does NOT reach

**The class.** When the remedy lives in a *generator*, two populations exist and only
one is ever counted: everything generated from now on (fixed at the roll — the one the
commit message describes) and everything already generated (unchanged indefinitely,
because nothing re-runs a generator on a schedule).

**Instances.**

| what | where | how long it sat |
|---|---|---|
| Dark palettes inheriting the layout's white `card_bg` | `bugs_open/113`, `/122` | fixed in code 2026-07-27, live in both replicas, and dartsonline's homepage was still 1.12:1 on 07-29 |
| Site chrome regenerated by nothing | `bugs_open/117` | open |
| An archived page still served | `bugs_open/098` | open |
| The Guides hub's listing, written 2026-07-20 before any guide existed | this workstream, `SQL_...s` | 9 days, invisible in every status field |

**What would stop it.** In the same commit as any generator fix, two lines:
(a) the query that identifies artefacts predating the fix, and (b) what re-running the
generator costs. Both are usually one line. For 113 the query is "served
`--color-card-bg` is `#ffffff` while the palette background is dark" and the cost is one
`webdesign-agent` run — measured at **under three minutes end to end** on dartsonline.

**The tell you are in this situation:** the fix's own header confidently quantifies the
population (`113`'s says "16 of 31 palettes define no card_bg, 12 of those dark") and
nothing anywhere records how many were *re-rendered*. **A count of affected sites is not
a count of repaired ones.**

Written up as a transferable pattern in `016b §9` (2026-07-29).

## Lever 2 — Fix the table that REGENERATES the one you fixed

**The class.** A false or stale value is corrected in every place that *reads* it, and
restored at the next rebuild by the place that *writes* it.

**Instances, all on one site in one day.** dartsonline's fabricated retail identity had
**six** homes, found one at a time, each after the previous was declared fixed:

1. `site_specs.identity` — another firm's Portland address, phone, email
2. `site_specs.classification` / the About briefing
3. `site_specs.content_direction` — instructed the writer to produce shop copy
   ("product listings", "Add to Bag", "show savings"); found only by building a page and
   reading it
4. `pages.page_spec.purpose` — per page
5. **`site_plan_pages`** — the rows a reconcile rebuilds pages FROM; still said
   "Specialist Darts Equipment & Accessories"
6. **`site_plan_imagery`** — prompts for "a delivery truck with a checkmark" and
   "a curated product range", which would have spent generation credits drawing a
   delivery van for a site that has never posted anything

Plus a seventh of the same shape an hour later: `pages.sections` is a **materialised
cache**; `site_plan_sections` is what the build reads. Editing the cache did nothing and
the rebuild restored the old section list.

**What would stop it.** One question, asked once, before declaring any data fix done:
**"which table regenerates this one?"** Follow it until the answer is "nothing". For the
site tables that is `site_plans` → `site_plan_pages` / `site_plan_sections` /
`site_plan_imagery` → `pages` → `page_components`, and `site_specs` beside it.

**Cheaper still, and worth building:** a `reconcile --dry-run` that reports what a
replan would CHANGE about live pages. Every one of the six would have shown up as a
diff. `[UNMEASURED]` — I have not checked whether `reconcile_site_plan` can already be
run in a reporting mode.

## Lever 3 — Measure the artefact, not something that describes it

**The class.** Every wrong claim I made in two sessions was a claim about an artefact,
made by looking at something other than the artefact.

**Instances.**

| claim | what I read | what the artefact said |
|---|---|---|
| "All nine built pages clean of shop language" | `page_components.rendered_html`, 5 phrases | `/sale.html` served "We cut prices across our sale range" |
| "The 404 nav links persist until chrome is rebuilt" | `site_components.header` | 4 of 9 served pages had them; the other 5 were already clean |
| "The Guides hub lists the guides" | `build_status='deployed'`, `deployed_at` today, item `complete` | zero links to any guide |
| "The icons are pale" (inherited, unverified) | a prior document | true, and confirmed only by fetching the PNG and looking at it |

**What would stop it.** `scripts/render_audit.py` already exists and reads the painted
page; `curl` costs ten seconds. The gap is not tooling, it is that **a database column
answers instantly and a fetch does not**, so the cheap wrong check wins under time
pressure.

The mechanism that would actually change behaviour is narrower than "be careful":
**when a check is a list of literals, the finding is "none of these literals appear",
and that is the only sentence it licenses.** Writing it that way makes the gap between
what you measured and what you want to say visible instead of invisible. Logged in
`WRONG_CALLS.md` twice on 2026-07-29.

## Lever 4 — A queue that nobody drains is a queue that lies

**The class.** A detector fires correctly, writes a finding, and the finding is never
routed. Every status is green; nothing happens.

**Instances.**

- `bugs_open/083` — findings written `status='detected'` never reach a handler; the
  promoter runs only inside a task disabled since May. **Owner has ruled "routing is NOT
  the bottleneck" and "decision pending — do not act".**
- `bugs_open/114` — imagery planned, generated, deployed, and nothing points a page at
  it. 14 of 28 such items fleet-wide parked the same way.
- `bugs_open/115` — the brief-fidelity audit predicted the owner's complaints three days
  early and every finding died in `detected`.
- `bugs_open/033` — 292 human-review items, none ever actioned through it.
- `bugs_open/150` — the improvement loop ends at "No issues found — site is clean"
  *after promoting 67 findings*.
- Observed here: `missing_news_page` for ai-agent-orchestration.com, `detected` since
  2026-07-24 and `unresolved` since 2026-05-01. dartsonline's equivalent was raised by
  hand at `triaged` and completed in **18 minutes**.

**What would stop it.** Not another promoter — 083's owner is right that routing is not
the bottleneck. The cheap thing is an **age alarm on the queue itself**: any
`site_work_items` row in a non-terminal status older than N days is reported, by type,
with a count. One query, no new mechanism, and it turns "silently stuck" into a number
somebody sees. `[INFERRED]` that nothing like this runs today — I did not find one, and
absence of a find is not a find.

**And a rule for authors:** a detector whose findings land in a status no dispatcher
reads is not "implemented". Before shipping one, follow ONE completed item of that type
to the artefact and confirm something changed. (`bugs_open/146` is the sharper version:
the handler *ran* and could not act, which looks identical.)

## Lever 5 — One value, two consumers, no shared source

**The class.** Two mechanisms independently decide the same thing and are allowed to
disagree. Each is individually correct; the composition is not.

**Instances.**

- **The icon ground.** `build-site-planner` pins every icon prompt to `#EEEEEE`; the
  page paints the tile from a CSS variable. 92 plan rows fleet-wide carry the literal.
  On dartsonline: 17 icons generated exactly as instructed, every one unusable.
  **Fixed at cause this session** — the clause is now derived from the same palette slot
  the tile is painted from, with a test that asserts the two resolve to the *same value*
  rather than that each is independently plausible.
- **`--color-primary` as both a fill and an ink** (`bugs_open/122`). No value satisfies
  both on a dark site; measured, including the site's own brand accent, which fails at
  4.41 against a 4.5 floor. Left unfixed deliberately, with the arithmetic recorded.
- **`bugs_open/113`** — 8 palette slots supplied, 17 declared, 9 fall through.
- **`bugs_open/137`** — two mechanisms judge "is this control alive", in the same
  function, and they disagree.
- **`bugs_open/072`** — `contact-info` reads flat `identity` keys that the writer nests.
  Same shape: one value, two spellings.
- The dedup-index ↔ `workItemTerminalStatuses` contract, already paid for once.

**What would stop it.** When two things must agree, **assert the agreement, not each
side**. A test that asks "is the tile dark?" passes while the generator is told
something else entirely. The test that catches it asserts they resolve to one value —
and it is the same shape as the shared-literal discipline `ListedPageEligibilitySQL`
already uses ("the article listing and the imagery sweep MUST agree on which articles
exist").

**The generalisable form:** if you find yourself writing the same colour/status/path in
two files, the fix is not to keep them in sync, it is to make one read the other.

## Lever 6 — Say what a mechanism cannot do, where it is defined

**The class.** A mechanism is used for a job it structurally cannot do, because nothing
at the call site says so.

**Instances.**

- **Reassembly vs regeneration.** `page_rerender` re-injects chrome and preserves
  content — exactly right for the two meta-description fixes, and exactly wrong for a
  stale listing, whose contents are resolved at *write* time. Nothing distinguishes them
  at the point of choosing. Cost here: a rebuild that changed the header and left a
  nine-day-old empty list, indistinguishable from success.
- **`category-listing` on a site with no categories.** It resolves `query.category_posts`
  and can never fill. An `empty_section` item has been open against dartsonline's
  homepage since 2026-07-14 — **15 days** — and the fix was to name a different
  component, not to repair anything.
- **`icon_chip_bg`.** I derived it, then measured: the palette reaches the stylesheet
  only via `{{palette "X"}}` calls in a layout, and **0 of 18 layouts name that slot**.
  It would have been dead config that looks live. Removed, with the negative result left
  as a comment where the entry would have gone.
- **`chk_source` vocabularies.** `site_specs.source` takes `hand_authored`;
  `site_plan_imagery.source` takes `llm|classifier|manual|adoption`. Nothing at either
  site says they differ; a whole transaction rolled back on it.
- **`bugs_open/134`** — a doc convention for "optional" leaked into the real key name, so
  two config keys are inert.
- **`bugs_open/148`** — three live agent definitions name an action in no registry, and
  nothing says so until a message arrives.

**What would stop it.** For the config-vocabulary cases, a check that every `source` /
`status` / `handler_agent` literal written by seeds and scripts is in the constraint —
mechanical, and `scripts/pattern-check.py` is where it would live. For
reassembly-vs-regeneration, the honest fix is a sentence in the item spec's own schema:
**what this handler will and will not change.**

## The four smaller findings, recorded so they are not rediscovered

1. **`meta_description` empty renders `content=""`.** 18 of 21 dartsonline pages were
   shipping a blank authored answer to "what is this page", which is worse than omitting
   the tag. `assemblePage` should omit rather than emit empty. Not filed as a bug —
   one-line, and I have not checked whether other sites are affected `[UNMEASURED]`.
2. **`assets.url` holds an expiring presigned S3 URL** (7 days). Already documented in
   `queryresolve.go` ("Never `assets.url`"), but the `assets` table itself says nothing.
3. **A feed summary that is entirely a mangled markdown table.** 2 of 7,676 items
   fleet-wide — a source quirk, not a platform defect. Blanked rather than rewritten:
   authoring a summary for an article nobody read is fabrication.
4. **`bugs_open/138` decided one of my own council rounds.** Round 1 of the tool-ratio
   change came back REVISE with `decided_by: "unreadable reviewer(s)"`, 7 of 9 seats
   abstaining — a harness failure, not a judgement. The objections were still worth
   answering and one found a real defect, so the round was not wasted; but the *verdict*
   was not about the change.

## What I would build first, if only one thing

**The queue age alarm** (Lever 4). It is one query, needs no new mechanism, and it is
the only item here that would have surfaced *five* separate open bugs — 083, 114, 115,
033, 150 — without anyone knowing to look for them. Everything else on this list needs
somebody to already suspect the problem.

Second: **the "what does this fix NOT reach" line** (Lever 1), because it costs two
lines per generator fix and it is the difference between 113 being fixed and 113 being
fixed *for the sites nobody re-rendered*.
