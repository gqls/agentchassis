# PLAN — oufe.com (Oxen Unity Financial Engineering) + oxenunity.com

Started 2026-07-25. Workstream directory: `docs/agent_docs/docs024_key_docs_latest/oufe/`.

Two new domains the owner already holds:

- **oufe.com** — the product site. UK corporate finance / financial engineering:
  liability management, distressed debt, restructuring, activist campaigns.
  Research teardowns plus interactive tools.
- **oxenunity.com** — a deliberate single-page "corporate" placeholder that links
  to oufe.com. **There is no company.** Nothing on it may imply otherwise.

## 0. Where this came from, and why this document exists

The owner developed the proposition in a long conversation with Gemini, then asked
Gemini to export that conversation as a running-notes file. **Gemini failed to
produce it** — three consecutive attempts returned errors, and the fourth returned
a partial document that had silently dropped the earlier strategic reasoning and
leaked its own Python source into the answer. The conversation was pasted into this
session instead, with the instruction to unpick it.

So §1 (decisions) and §2 (challenges) below are the record that was nearly lost.
§1 is the owner's thinking, reconstructed from what he *decided* rather than from
what Gemini proposed — the two are not the same, and the difference matters:
Gemini proposed a great deal, and the owner selected, reordered and overrode.

## 1. Decisions taken (owner, in the Gemini thread)

**D1 — Identity.** oufe.com = **O**xen **U**nity **F**inancial **E**ngineering.
The name reads institutional. The owner's gloss: *Oxen* = heavy lifting, patience,
grinding through dense balance sheets and 300-page dockets that others skip;
*Unity* = lender coalitions, class consensus, joint action — which is literally the
subject matter (majority lender groups in liability management exercises);
*Financial Engineering* = capital-structure mechanics, explicitly not stock picking.

**D2 — Subject matter.** How the top financial-engineering firms actually make
money out of corporate finance. Named starting points: Elliott (contractual
loopholes, sovereign enforcement, litigation as strategy), Apollo and Oaktree
(distressed-for-control, DIP financing, debt-for-equity, priming), Third Point and
Pershing Square (operational activism, spin-offs, carve-outs, public campaigns).
Modern tactics to cover: **uptier exchanges**, **dropdown transactions**,
**double-dip structures** — the "creditor-on-creditor violence" family. Plus
hall-of-fame historical trades as worked examples.

**D3 — UK focus.** *"UK based corporate finance is better for me."* The UK
statutory machinery — Part 26A restructuring plans, cross-class cramdown, schemes
of arrangement, special administration for regulated utilities — is the recurring
analytical frame. **Flagship case: Thames Water**, chosen partly on the reasoning
that *"government is involved so we may be able to get more information"* — i.e.
the regulator and the courts publish, which is exactly what a
provenance-first site needs.

**D4 — Audience.** The owner raised the objection himself: *"bigger hedge funds
etc will already have a load of information, what can I offer?"* That rules out
competing with Reorg, Debtwire, 9fin and Bloomberg on primary information. The
answer settled on: the **mid-market / boutique professional** — boutique
restructuring advisers, corporate-finance and insolvency lawyers, private-credit
analysts at smaller shops, family offices, and ambitious buy-side associates.
They have expense accounts and no six-figure terminal.

**D5 — What we sell that a terminal doesn't.** Not more information — **synthesis
and mechanism**. Who gets primed, who gets paid, and why the documents let it
happen, in minutes rather than in a 200-page docket. And **interactive mechanics
instead of static PDFs**: sliders that show who wins and who loses as enterprise
value, new-money priming and haircuts move — for live cases *and* for historical
ones, which is the owner's explicit interest ("or were applied for historical
examples").

**D6 — Directions: all of them, eventually.** Gemini offered three positionings
(intelligence lab / playbook library / vulnerability radar). The owner: *"I like
all your suggestions and would like to incorporate all of them in the site
eventually"*, with the **playbook library** angle called out by name — the "dark
arts" framing, textbooks teach portfolio theory while real corporate finance runs
on aggressive engineering. **Primary magnet = breaking down active corporate
battles**, with the education route running in parallel.

**D7 — Monetisation, phased, and deliberately slow.** Owner: *"to start with
monetisation should be with authoritative deliverables. Subscriptions can come
later when we have confirmed that we can deliver regular value on the site."*
- **P1** free public magnet — active-battle teardowns + tools.
- **P2** one-off **authoritative deliverables** (deal packs: dossier + working
  model), transactional, no recurring promise to break.
- **P3** subscriptions **+ BYOD**.
- **P4** enterprise on-prem — run the framework inside a client's own estate.

**D8 — BYOD (the owner's own idea, offered as "a suggestion and not direction").**
*"the free version takes free data feeds and does the very best we can with it.
The paid version allows them to use their own datafeeds — privately. (We could
launch our framework on their private servers if we needed to.)"* This is a good
instinct and it survives scrutiny: the free tier's honesty constraint (only public,
citable data) becomes the paid tier's feature (your numbers, your model, and they
never leave your browser).

**D9 — Thames Water as a living dossier, not an article.** The owner's framing:
*"The Thames Water example on its own may stretch to several dozen pages by the
end of it so let's design for that in mind. We will have many such examples and
will be adding to them regularly."* So `/cases/thames-water` is a **hub** with a
standard sub-article template, designed for growth from day one, and the hub shape
is a repeatable pattern for every future case.

**D10 — Two sites.** oufe.com is the product. oxenunity.com is one page pointing
at it. *"there is no company yet so we'll have to be clear about how we present
it."*

**D11 — Process.** *"We need to be honest and challenge directions that won't work
for one reason or another."* Hence §2. The owner also wanted the discussion to run
long before building — this workstream exists so that the strategy conversation has
somewhere to live and continue.

## 2. Challenges to those decisions, and corrections to the Gemini record

The owner asked for these explicitly. Each is stated with what would have to change
if it's right.

**C1 — "Direction 3 (the radar) is lowest risk" is backwards on this platform.**
The owner wrote *"first direction 3 as that is lowest risk"*. It is the *highest*
risk we could pick first, for three reasons that are properties of our estate, not
of the idea:
1. **No market-data feed exists anywhere in the platform.** Bond prices, yields,
   maturity schedules, index membership — none of it. A distress radar needs
   priced data we do not have and would have to license.
2. **UK court dockets are scrape-hostile.** There is no docket API. Judgments
   appear on the National Archives caselaw service and BAILII *after* the fact;
   listings and filings largely do not appear at all.
3. **A wrong distress signal is a statement about a named real company.** Our own
   worst incident (vetcomparison, §5) was exactly this class — invented figures
   attached to named real businesses.

What is genuinely lowest-risk is the thing the owner already named as the primary
magnet: **one flagship dossier plus one excellent tool**. The radar returns later
as narrow, individually-verifiable slices (e.g. maturity walls read from filed
accounts at Companies House — a real, free, citable source).

**C2 — Every figure in the Gemini transcript is unverified, and some are certainly
stale.** The transcript states Thames Water's capital structure with confident
precision (£16bn Class A, £3bn super-senior, £1bn Class B, £2.5bn equity, a
£3.35bn equity injection, ~£9.4bn of write-offs, a "~25% Class A haircut"). Those
are model recollections, not sourced facts, and the case has moved since whenever
that recollection was formed. **None of them may appear anywhere** — not on a
page, not in a mission brief, not in a spec, not as a tool default — until they
exist as evidence rows with a source and a date.

> **This is not caution for its own sake.** See §5: on 2026-07-24 a site's own
> `site_specs` prose carried a hardcoded figure list, and a full re-render wrote
> those invented numbers back over grounded values *while the evidence register
> and the fleet-wide "never invent numbers" prompt rule were both live*. A number
> written into a spec is a **given**, and a given outranks every writer-side rule
> we have. The safest place for an unverified figure is nowhere.

**C2b — CORRECTION to the capabilities inventory the owner pasted into Gemini.**
That inventory says claims-verification **"V5 [DESIGNED]"** and "designed, not
built". **That is stale.** V5 (researched citations: web-search → extract atomic
claims with verbatim quotes → re-fetch the URL and assert the quote still appears)
was built, seeded and activated on chassis v1.0.1140 on 2026-07-20; the agent
`evidence-researcher` is active; its one blocker (`bugs_open/047`, batch scrape
rejected before its own handler) was fixed and closed 2026-07-21. What is true is
that **its end-to-end smoke run has never been repeated since the fix**, so V5 is
*live but never successfully exercised*. oufe is the natural first real exercise —
a site whose entire value rests on quote-verifiable citations to court judgments
and regulator publications.

**C2c — Our deterministic number-scan gate is close to inert on finance prose.**
`platform/orchestration/datahelpers/claims.go:334` scans a number only when a
business-noun regex matches its surrounding window — clients, customers, records,
awards, uptime, years of experience and so on. There is **no** debt, creditor,
recovery, tranche, leverage or covenant vocabulary in it, and `isExcludedNumber`
**structurally excludes currency amounts**. So "£16bn of Class A debt" is not
scanned at all. The same limitation is already recorded for relojistas.com.

Consequence for oufe: the load-bearing honesty layers here are **V2** (the writer
gets a whitelist of the only numbers it may assert), **banned_claims** regexes,
**V5** citations, a manual **V3** claims-auditor pass, and **human review before
deploy**. Do not read a clean automated claims report on this site as "no invented
numbers" — it cannot mean that yet. Widening the regex is a fleet-wide Go change
and belongs in front of the council, not in this workstream's first week.

**C3 — Regulatory perimeter and defamation, which the transcript treats as a
disclaimer problem.** Gemini's mitigation was *"maintain clear legal disclaimers"*.
That is necessary and nowhere near sufficient.
- **s21 FSMA (financial promotions)** bites on invitations or inducements to
  engage in investment activity. Education and analysis sit outside it; *"tools
  that can find opportunities"* — the owner's original phrasing — drifts toward
  it, as does any language implying a reader should act. The framing must be
  consistently **educational scenario modelling**, never a recommendation, never a
  performance claim.
- **Negligent misstatement (Hedley Byrne)** is the real exposure route for paid
  research, not any formal regime — this is already our recorded position from
  idea.uk's liability work. It follows that disclaimers must be **proximate**: in
  the tool, at the top of the deliverable, not only in a footer.
- **Defamation / malicious falsehood** applies the moment we characterise a named
  company as distressed or vulnerable on anything other than sourced fact.

Owner sign-off is needed on the standing wording before any of it goes live.

**C4 — The commerce gap is real, but we are not starting from zero.**
The chassis has **no** payments, paywall, checkout, public-site user accounts,
entitlement store, gated downloads or PDF generation. The `auth-service`
subscription package looks like billing and is not: no Stripe SDK, no webhook
handler, `CreateSubscription` is a bare insert that sets status active with no
payment step, and quota checks return hardcoded zeros. **Nothing may gate on it.**

What we do have: **idea.uk's live, proven £29 Stripe path** — a standalone Go
binary on a VM, entitlement granted only on a signature-verified
`checkout.session.completed`, orders in a file, live payments proven end-to-end;
and the **island pattern** (isolated VM behind a Cloudflare tunnel, own Postgres
with backups, ~£16/month, and a standing rule that no production credential ever
touches it). P2 monetisation, when it comes, reuses that shape. **P1 needs none of
it**, which is a further argument for the phasing the owner already chose.

**C5 — BYOD is right, but keep it stateless at first.** "Fork this model into a
private workspace" implies accounts, storage and a security boundary — that is P3
machinery. The honest P1/P2 version is: the tool runs entirely in the browser,
you can type your own numbers into it, nothing is transmitted and nothing is
saved. That is genuinely private, immediately buildable, and doesn't promise a
workspace we haven't built.

**C6 — CORRECTION: go-echarts does not exist.** Gemini's architecture specified
charts "built with go-echarts", following the capabilities inventory it was given.
That inventory line was itself corrected on 2026-07-24: go-echarts is not in
`go.mod`, no chart action exists, and the concept register marks data-charts
"aspirational — not started". The only chart code in the estate is
`platform/orchestration/actions/report_charts.go` — purpose-built dependency-free
inline SVG, written for one report page. **The doctrine is real and stands**
(charts are code-rendered from real series; a diffusion model never draws data).
The renderer is a gap. For oufe's waterfall the bars are drawn client-side inside
the tool's own HTML, which is the correct answer anyway for something driven by a
slider.

**C7 — Cadence is the product risk, not the technology.** "Breaking down active
battles" is only worth paying for if it tracks the case. We should promise, and
keep, **one flagship dossier kept genuinely current** before promising a second.
Related: we are deliberately **not** arming a news feed at launch. The classifier
would categorise this site as `finance` and auto-seed generic
financial-markets/interest-rates keywords — which is the opposite of a specialist
restructuring publication, and it spends credits per fetch.

**C8 — We are proposing to start at the top of a ladder we wrote to stop exactly
that.** `features_open/015` exists because *"just asking a new domain to become as
developed as idea.uk in one step is too much."* oufe's roadmap is top-rung-first:
paid deliverables and private data workspaces on a domain with no pages. Our P1
deliberately sits on the bottom rung — live honest content and one working tool —
and earns the next one. Worth saying out loud because the pull toward the top rung
will be constant.

## 3. What we are building first (agreed with the owner this session)

Owner choices, 2026-07-25:
- **Scope of first slice**: workstream docs + oxenunity.com live + oufe.com
  Phase-1 skeleton, with no unverified figures anywhere.
- **oxenunity.com framing**: wordmark, one neutral line, link to oufe.com,
  **no entity claims at all**. Rationale accepted: a page that claims nothing
  cannot say anything untrue about a company that doesn't exist.

### 3.1 oxenunity.com
Hand-authored single file, committed straight to `gqls/sites`. Deliberately **not**
run through the build pipeline: the entire point is exact, claims-free copy, and
the pipeline's job is to generate copy. No JavaScript, no forms, no analytics, no
cookies — which also means no privacy page is required for it to be honest.

### 3.2 oufe.com Phase 1
1. Seed **before** submitting: `sites` row with a contact email (bug 063: the
   hallucinated-email check *fails open* when a site has no email), an
   `evidence_base` aspect with **zero facts** and finance-specific banned-claim
   patterns, and an `imagery_style_guide` aspect (bug 027: `content_hero` renders
   unstyled on any site without one).
2. Submit as a **Tier-3** fresh domain — mission brief *and* roadmap brief. The
   roadmap brief is authoritative to the planner ("build only these pages, do not
   invent additional pages"), which is how we get a deliberately small P1 site.
   Note `082_submit_domain_unified.sh` has no `--roadmap-file` flag, so the
   envelope is hand-rolled from the script's own kcat block.
3. P1 pages: index, about, cases (hub), cases/thames-water (dossier hub), contact,
   plus hand-written legal pages including a `/disclaimer`.
4. Turn the news feed off deliberately after classification (C7).
5. Legal pages follow the migration-182 pattern: content hand-written,
   `rebuild_policy='owned'` plus a permanent component lock, `rendered_html`
   written in the migration. This also defuses bug 053 — an empty legal nav group
   makes the footer's legal slot list every footer page.

### 3.3 Thames Water evidence, then content
Evidence first, prose second. Fire `evidence-researcher` at the current state of
the restructuring; verified citation facts (verbatim quote, re-fetched and
re-matched) land in the register; only then does any page assert anything. Where
we have no verified fact, the page says so rather than reaching for a plausible
number. (Watch bug 073: a component with a *required* stat field plus an honest
empty value fails the whole page build — so the hub uses components that don't
demand numbers.)

### 3.4 The Recovery Waterfall & Priming Simulator
Client-side, deterministic, no back end. Inputs: enterprise value, new-money
super-senior tranche, per-class debt, haircut. Outputs: recovery per class in
money and percent, plus plain-language "who is in the money and who is not"
commentary derived from the arithmetic — not from an LLM at page-view time.

Two hard constraints from the estate:
- **JavaScript delivery**: inline in the tool's `html_template` with
  `render_mode='standalone'`. The `js_content` lane publishes the file and then
  injects no `<script>` tag — published-but-inert, the `bugs_open/041` class.
- **Identity must agree** across `pages.name`, `content_components.function` and
  the travelling doc's `subject_key`, or acceptance testing resolves the wrong URL.

Ships with an acceptance-criteria fence (boots, no console errors, HTTP 200, no
mobile overflow, and one real interaction assertion) and is verified in headless
Chromium. A structural pass is not a claim that it works.

Until verified Thames figures exist, the tool ships in illustrative mode with no
named-case defaults.

## 5. The two precedents this workstream must not repeat

**vetcomparison.** Fabricated prices published against 3,124 named real UK vet
practices, detected by statistical fingerprint (46 distinct consult fees across
3,124 practices), plus a proprietary-data notice asserted over third-party facts.
The rails adopted afterwards transfer to oufe unchanged: **no figure published
without a traceable source URL and capture date; where provenance is missing,
aggregates or silence, never an estimate; no data-rights claims over other
people's facts; regulatory numbers only from primary sources, preserving
proposed-versus-final; a published correction and removal route.**

**The 043 spec-poisoning.** A site's own specs hardcoded a figure list into the
writer's content direction. A re-render then wrote those invented numbers back
over grounded values, with the evidence register live and the fleet prompt rule
live. **A number in a spec is a given, and a given wins.** Hence: no figures in
the mission brief, the roadmap brief, the identity spec or the content direction —
only in the evidence register, where they carry a source.
