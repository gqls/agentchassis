webdesign.uk sells complete websites to small and medium UK businesses. A customer comes to us with a domain and a business, and we build them the whole site: the pages that business actually needs, the words on them, the design, and getting it live on their own domain. It is one fixed price paid once, with no ongoing fee to us and nothing the customer is locked into. They see the finished site on a private preview link and can walk away with a full refund at any point up until they accept it, at which point it becomes theirs. Changes they ask for afterwards are charged as work, and anything that is our own mistake we put right at no cost.

The people buying this are business owners who need a decent website and have neither the time to make one nor the appetite for a drawn-out agency process. They are not technical. They have often been quoted far more than this by an agency, or have been sold a page builder subscription and never finished the site. They are sceptical, they have been oversold to before, and they respond to someone talking plainly about what they will actually get.

The site should do three things. It should explain the offer clearly enough that someone understands it without asking. It should ask the visitor a little about themselves and what they are after, just a little, as an opening to a conversation rather than a form to be qualified by. And it should make it easy to get in touch, by email or by phone.

The tone is plain, direct British English, written for someone who does not enjoy being sold to. Short sentences and ordinary words. This is a new service with no history to trade on, so it must not reach for the things a new service does not have. It should sound like a competent small studio that is confident about the work and relaxed about proving it, not like a startup announcing itself. Restraint reads as confidence here. Overselling would read as exactly the thing these buyers have been burnt by before.

Avoid the whole register of agency marketing language: no talk of solutions, no bespoke digital experiences, no passion for pixels, no promises to transform anyone's business. Avoid the visual cliches of the web design trade as well, the wireframe sketches and fanned colour swatches and browser windows drawn at an angle. The site is itself the portfolio piece, so what it demonstrates matters more than what it asserts.
