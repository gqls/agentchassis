# Reasons for "you pay first, no refunds" — options for the owner to choose between

**Asked for by the owner, 2026-08-17:** *"No refunds. Please give me some reasons to decide
on."* Nothing here is applied. Each reason below is **true today** (that matters: whatever is
chosen becomes an attested fact, and the checker then enforces it on every page and the chat
bot speaks it). Pick one to LEAD with; the others work as support.

## The settled mechanics (owner, 2026-08-17)

Customer does **not** see the site before paying. The preview link exists for the owner at
all times, and for the customer **after payment** — before they put it on Netlify or hand it
to someone else — and it **expires after about a month**. They also get a **ZIP** of the
finished site. No refunds, with a full refund used only to settle a legal complaint (a
backstop, **not** an advertised term — see §4).

## 1. The five candidate reasons

### A. The work is already done when you pay — RECOMMENDED LEAD
The site is built *before* any money changes hands. Payment is not a deposit against a
promise; it is for a finished thing that already exists.
- **True?** Yes, and it is the strongest fact you have. Most services take money first and
  build afterwards; you do the opposite.
- **What it licenses:** "We build it first. You pay for something that already exists, not
  for a promise." It reframes pay-first from a demand into an unusual amount of risk taken
  by you.
- **Cost:** it invites *"then why can't I see it?"* — you need §3's answer ready.

### B. The price is what these terms buy
£149 exists **because** there is no revision round, no refund handling, no account manager.
Each of those is a cost that would land on the price.
- **True?** Yes — it is the actual economics, and `no_changes_included` already attests the
  first half.
- **What it licenses:** exclusions stated as the *mechanism of the price*, not apologies.
- **Cost:** weak as a lead (sounds defensive); strong as the second line. **Must not drift
  into "cheapest"** — your second brand would falsify that on launch day.

### C. You get the files, so "I don't like it" has a better fix than a refund
You receive the site's own source and a ZIP. Anything you want changed, you or any developer
can change — no permission, no waiting, nothing tied to us.
- **True?** Yes, already attested three times over (`yours_to_change`, `no_lock_in`,
  `taking_it_further`).
- **What it licenses:** this is the reason that makes "no refunds" land as *reasonable*
  rather than *harsh* — the remedy for dissatisfaction is real and immediate, it just is not
  money back.
- **Cost:** none. **Recommended as the standing companion to whichever lead you choose.**

### D. Slots are genuinely limited
Only a few sites are built at a time; when they are full, submissions close.
- **True?** Yes (`queue_limited`).
- **Cost:** it is *your* constraint, not a customer benefit. Support only — leading with it
  reads as "we're doing you a favour".

### E. It is a production site, not a desktop demo — YOUR INNOVATION POINT
Multi-page, built and deployed as a real hosted site, handed over as files you own. That is
a different object from what a chat assistant produces on a desktop.
- **True?** Yes.
- **Cost, and this needs care:** phrase it as **what you deliver**, never as what a named
  product cannot do. A claim about someone else's capability dates badly, is unverifiable by
  your own checker, and invites the comparison to be tested. "A complete multi-page site,
  built and hosted, handed to you as files" is strong and safe. "Better than what Claude can
  make" is neither.
- This is **positioning that supports the price**, not a refund reason. Use it in the hero.

## 2. What I would choose, and why

**Lead A, support with C, price-justify with B.** That gives: *we build it first, you own the
files outright, and the price is what these terms buy.* All three are true, none is a
superlative, and none is falsified by a cheaper sibling brand. D and E are true but belong
elsewhere on the page — D near the queue/submissions copy, E in the hero.

## 3. The question this position must answer, and the gap it exposes

Pay-first means a visitor cannot see **their** site before deciding. So they must be able to
see **your work** instead. That is the substitute for a preview, and it is the difference
between bold and reckless.

**⚠ You do not currently have that.** There is an open, already-FAILED work item on this
site — `dda32da9`: *"No portfolio or case studies are present in any sampled page."* Under
the old pay-after-approval terms that was a minor content gap. **Under pay-first it becomes
the main trust mechanism**, and it is missing. Whatever else the rewrite does, examples of
finished sites are now load-bearing.

## 4. Two phrasing traps in what you told me

1. **"No refunds" vs "not refundable".** You will refund in full to settle a legal
   complaint. If the register says *"the price is not refundable"* (as it does today) that is
   an absolute the backstop contradicts. Say **"We do not offer refunds"** — a statement of
   policy, which stays true while leaving you free to settle a complaint. Do **not** advertise
   the complaints route; it is a backstop, not a term.
2. **The preview expiry must not read as "your site disappears."** A link that dies after a
   month sounds like a loss unless the ZIP is named in the same breath. State them together:
   *the preview link is live for about a month; the ZIP is yours to keep.* Otherwise the
   expiry becomes the thing people ask about.

## THE NEW LEAD — F: show the work, promise nothing

> **You can see exactly what you get: real sites built with this system, and the exact prompt
> that produced each one.**

Why this fits every constraint the owner set:

- **It promises nothing about THEIR site**, which is the requirement — each build differs, so
  any specification is a hostage. Examples describe what happened, not what will.
- **It is the honest substitute for a preview.** Pay-first removes the customer's look at
  their own site, so they must be able to look at somebody's. This is that, and it is the
  answer to the trust gap flagged in §3.
- **It uses what the owner already has**: *"I can use the system on some of my own domains as
  examples of exactly what they'd get and with what prompt."* No new claim needed — the
  evidence is a page of finished sites.
- **It carries the innovation point without a capability claim.** Showing prompt → finished
  multi-page site IS the "different from a desktop assistant" argument, demonstrated rather
  than asserted, so no third party is characterised and nothing dates.
- **The cheaper sibling brand cannot falsify it.** It is not a comparison, so it survives.
- **It gets stronger on its own** as recommendations arrive — *"let the recommendations do
  the talking"* — because the same slot on the page takes them.

**Recommended shape:** lead F (show the work) · support C (you own the files, so there is a
remedy that is not a refund) · price-justify B (this is what the terms buy) · D and E as
supporting lines near the queue copy and the hero respectively.
