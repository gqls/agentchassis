Loans and mortgages, worked out together

How your loans and your mortgage affect each other

Most people carry more than one kind of debt at once: a mortgage alongside a personal loan, a credit card balance, or a car finance agreement. Each one changes what a lender will do about the others, whether you're buying, remortgaging, or deciding which debt to clear first.

Open the calculator that matches what you're trying to work out below, or start with a guide if you'd rather see how the mechanism works first.

Where the two kinds of borrowing meet

Clearing around £100 a month of loan or card repayments can add back somewhere between £5,000 and £7,000 of mortgage borrowing power, because a lender weighs what leaves your account each month more heavily than what you owe in total. Whether the next £1,000 does more good on the deposit or on the debt depends on the rate on each side, which is covered in deposit, or clear the debt first? and what clearing a loan does to what you can borrow.
