AI Review Council Simulator

Put a panel of AI reviewers in front of every change and some proportion of your work gets sent back. This tool models how much. Choose which reviewers sit on the panel, how severe an objection has to be before it blocks, and how many revision rounds you are willing to run, and it estimates how often a sound change passes.

The rates in this tool are real. Every reviewer below is a seat on the review council we run against changes to our own platform, and its objection rate is measured from 362 completed council runs between 10 and 30 July 2026. Nothing here is illustrative. Where a seat has sat on too few runs to be reliable it is marked, and the model's assumptions are stated underneath the results rather than buried.

    Where this model is honest: the per-seat objection rates, the abstention rate, the severity mix and the three comparison figures are all counted from the 362 runs, not estimated.

    Where it is not: it treats seats as independent, which they are not, since a badly shaped change tends to draw objections from several seats at once. It also treats each round as a fresh roll of the dice, which understates a real council, because a revision answers the objection it was actually given.

    Why our real number is lower: on the settings we actually run, this model reads about 70 per cent while our measured first-round approval is 51. The gap is the phrase "a sound change". The model asks how often a panel objects to nothing; reality also contains changes that deserved the objection they got. Read these figures as the cost of the panel, not the quality of your work.
