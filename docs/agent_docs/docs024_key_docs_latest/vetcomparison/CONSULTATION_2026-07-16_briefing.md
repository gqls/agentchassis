# Briefing — the CMA vets consultations: process, requirements, and our position

**Prepared:** 16 July 2026, for the owner of vetcomparison.uk.
**Purpose:** a plain-English walkthrough of how the CMA's consultation process works, what is
open now, what is coming, and what we intend to argue. Facts verified against the CMA case page
on 16 July 2026 are marked as such; where I describe general CMA practice not restated on this
case's page, I say so.

---

## 1. Where the process stands

The CMA finished its market investigation into vet services with a **final report on
24 March 2026**. The final report decides *what* the remedies are; it does not itself make them
law. The CMA implements them through legal instruments called **Orders** (and, for the RCVS,
**Undertakings** — promises the RCVS gives instead of being ordered). By statute the CMA must
put a draft of each instrument out for **public consultation** before making it final, and it
must make the remedies within six months of the final report — so by **23 September 2026**.

There are two instruments, consulted on separately:

**A. The Funding Order (open now).** Published 30 June 2026, responses close **23:59 on
30 July 2026**. This covers only how the remedies are paid for: a levy on veterinary practices
that funds the RCVS's new duties (the enhanced Find a Vet platform, monitoring). Per our review
of the consultation documents, the draft proposes a one-off set-up charge (roughly £150–250 per
practice) plus an annual fee (roughly £450–550 per practice) — **verify these figures against
the draft Order PDF before quoting them in a submission**. *(Confirmed on case page: title,
dates, portal link. Figures: from our earlier document review, unconfirmed.)*

**B. The substantive remedies Order (not yet open).** This is the big one — price lists, the
prescription fee cap, ownership disclosure, estimates, the RCVS data platform and third-party
access. The CMA's timetable said it would publish the draft for consultation in **July 2026**;
as of 16 July it has not appeared. When it opens, expect a window of roughly a month.
*(Confirmed on case page: not yet published as of 16 July.)*

## 2. How responding works

- **Who can respond:** anyone — businesses, individuals, trade bodies. You do not need standing
  or a lawyer. Comparison-site operators are directly affected parties for the substantive
  Order, which gives our response natural weight on the third-party-access provisions.
- **How:** the funding consultation takes responses through the CMA's online portal
  (connect.cma.gov.uk — linked from the case page). The case team's email is
  **VetsMI@cma.gov.uk**. Expect the substantive consultation to work the same way. *(Portal and
  email confirmed on the case page.)*
- **What happens to your response:** general CMA practice — not restated on this case page — is
  that responses may be published on the case page, usually with the respondent's name. If parts
  are commercially sensitive you mark them confidential and supply a redacted version; the CMA
  generally respects clearly-marked redactions. Assume anything unmarked becomes public, and
  write accordingly.
- **What a good response looks like:** short, specific, and anchored to the draft's actual
  wording. The CMA is consulting on *this text*, not re-opening the investigation — "paragraph X
  should say Y because Z" moves the needle; general opinions about vets do not. Evidence beats
  assertion: concrete examples, costs, or data get cited in the CMA's response document.
- **What it achieves:** the CMA must consider responses and publishes its reasoning for
  accepting or rejecting points when it makes the final Order. Realistic wins at this stage are
  drafting-level: clearer definitions, adjusted thresholds, an added express provision. The
  remedies themselves are settled and won't be reversed.
- **On the record:** a response is a public, on-the-record act by you/the business. Ours would
  be the first public statement of vetcomparison.uk's existence and interests. That is a feature
  (we're a declared, legitimate participant in the remedy ecosystem) but worth being conscious
  of.

## 3. Our positions

Two hats, declared openly in the response: we operate an independent price-comparison service,
and we side with independent practices on remedy design. The CMA weighs arguments better when
the interest behind them is stated.

### For independent practices (against structural advantage to the corporate groups)

1. **The levy should scale with size.** A flat per-practice fee is regressive: the same ~£500/yr
   is trivial across a 500-site group's back office and real money for a single-site
   independent. Argue for banding by group size (sites or turnover), or a lower small-practice
   rate. *(This is the funding consultation's core issue — the one argument worth making before
   30 July.)*
2. **Don't let the staggered deadlines become a corporate marketing window.** Large groups must
   publish price lists ~3 months in, independents get 6. Sensible as a burden concession, but
   for up to three months corporates will look transparent while independents look opaque.
   Ask the CMA/RCVS to publish free templates and a validation tool at Order-time so any
   independent can comply on day one by choice — and to say publicly that early small-practice
   publication is encouraged.
3. **Ownership disclosure should be enforced hard and worded tightly.** Independence is the
   independents' main differentiator; it only works if "part of X Group" genuinely appears
   prominently on corporate-owned local brands. Support the remedy; ask for clear minimum
   prominence standards (not buried in a footer) and active monitoring.
4. **Keep the no-free-text rule absolute.** Standardised, comparable lists are the great
   leveller for small practices — any free-text loophole will be used by the biggest marketing
   departments first. Support ¶3.92 as drafted; resist any dilution.

### For ourselves (the comparison-service provisions)

5. **Low-barrier third-party approval criteria.** The four standards (security, reliability,
   transparency, fair competition) should be applied proportionately, with costs and process a
   small operator can meet — otherwise the only "approved" comparison services will belong to
   large incumbents, defeating the remedy's purpose. (We've chosen not to seek the badge
   ourselves, but a thin approved tier shrinks the whole ecosystem the remedy relies on, and we
   may want the feed under future ownership of that decision.)
6. **An express statement that mandated price lists may be reused by comparison services.**
   The final report recognises scraping-based comparison as legitimate (Part B ¶3.320–3.321)
   but the draft Order is where that could become an operative provision — e.g. that information
   published under the Order may be accessed and reused by third parties for comparison
   purposes, and that practices may not use technical or contractual means to frustrate it.
   This single sentence would close the biggest legal ambiguity in our model (database-right
   uncertainty — see LEGAL §8) for everyone, not just us.
7. **Machine-readable structure.** The Order already bans free text; ask it to go one step
   further and specify a consistent published structure (even just "the 36 items in the standard
   order with a stable label each"), so comparison for consumers is cheap and accurate across
   all operators. Bonus ask: publication of the standard list as a schema/template annex.
8. **No selective blocking.** The report notes the risk that practices could present a biased
   picture by blocking scraping of unflattering prices. Ask that the Order treat obstructing
   access to the mandated lists as non-compliance with the publication duty itself.

## 4. What happens next, practically

1. **By 30 July (portal, funding consultation):** decide whether to submit the levy-scaling
   point (§3.1). It's a one-page response. Everything else waits for the substantive draft.
2. **Watch the case page** for the substantive draft Order (expected any day; it must move
   quickly to hit 23 September). When it lands, I read the actual text and turn §3 into a
   clause-referenced draft response within a day or two, for your review before anything is
   submitted. Nothing is ever submitted without your explicit sign-off.
3. **Formalities to have ready:** respond as the operator of vetcomparison.uk (name the
   business/entity you want on the record), state our interest in one line ("we operate an
   independent veterinary price comparison service"), keep confidential material out or clearly
   marked.
