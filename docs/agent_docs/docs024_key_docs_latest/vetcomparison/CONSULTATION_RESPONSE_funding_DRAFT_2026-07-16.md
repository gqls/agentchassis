## Response text (to paste into the portal)

**Respondent:** [Full name]
**Responding on behalf of:** [Entity name], operator of vetcomparison.uk, an independent
veterinary price comparison service.
**Interest in this consultation:** We operate a comparison website covering UK first opinion
veterinary practices. The remedies this levy funds — in particular the enhanced RCVS Find a Vet
platform and the monitoring of the price transparency remedies — directly shape the market we
operate in. We also engage regularly with independent practices, whose position we address below.

### Summary

We support the Funding Order and Undertakings in principle: the remedies need funding, and the
RCVS is the right body to administer them. Our concern is with one aspect of the drafting: a
levy charged at a flat rate per first opinion practice (FOP), without regard to the size or
resources of the business that owns it, does not fully achieve the final report's aims, because
it bears most heavily on the smallest businesses the remedies are intended to help compete.

### The flat per-FOP basis is regressive at the margin that matters

We understand the draft Undertakings Notice sets the levy at a flat amount per FOP
([confirm and cite: circa £150–£250 set-up and £450–£550 per year per FOP — Notice ¶(x)]).
Charging per FOP correctly makes a 500-site group pay five hundred times what a single-site
business pays in total. But per site — which is where the cost is actually borne — it charges
the same amount for:

- a single-vet independent practice, for which several hundred pounds a year is a visible cost
  borne by one owner, with no group back office to absorb administration; and
- one branch of a national group, for which the same charge is a rounding error handled by a
  central finance function.

The final report's remedies package aims to strengthen competition, and much of the competitive
pressure the CMA relies on comes from independent practices being viable, visible competitors
to the large groups. A funding mechanism that is proportionally heaviest for the smallest
competitors sits uneasily with that aim — particularly in the same period (the first year of
the Order) in which those same small businesses bear their own one-off compliance costs for the
price list, ownership disclosure and data submission remedies.

### Suggested drafting change

Introduce simple size banding into the levy while keeping it revenue-neutral overall, for
example:

- a reduced per-FOP rate for veterinary businesses operating [1–2] FOPs; or
- a small-business rate tied to the same "fewer than 15 FOPs" threshold the final report
  already uses to distinguish smaller businesses for implementation deadlines, so no new
  definitions are needed.

Either approach preserves the per-FOP principle and the RCVS's cost recovery while aligning the
incidence of the levy with the final report's recognition — already embodied in the staggered
compliance deadlines — that smaller businesses have materially less capacity to absorb
remedy-related costs.

### A secondary point: transparency of levy costs

We would also support a requirement that the RCVS publish an annual statement of levy income
and remedy-related expenditure. The levy is compulsory; those paying it, particularly small
practices, should be able to see what it funds. This supports the "transparency" standard the
final report applies elsewhere in the remedies package.

### Scope note

We have confined this response to the Funding Order and Undertakings. We intend to respond
separately to the consultation on the substantive remedies Order when it is published,
including on the third-party data access provisions that concern us directly as a comparison
service.
