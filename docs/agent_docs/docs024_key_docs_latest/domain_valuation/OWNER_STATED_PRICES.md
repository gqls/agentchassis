## The general lesson from cartoon.co.uk, which is bigger than one domain

**An acquisition cost is a hard floor, and we do not know any of them.**
`cartoon.co.uk` sits in the batch just released to *ordinary portfolio stock* —
it would have been priced from an algorithmic appraisal and could have been sold
keenly at a fraction of what he paid. It only escaped because he volunteered the
figure unprompted.

`[UNMEASURED]` **How many of the other 2,859 sellable domains carry a cost base
that would make a keen price a realised loss? Nobody knows, and no source in
this estate records it.** The registrar exports carry expiry and nameservers,
never purchase price. This is a real hole in the bottom-500 exercise: "keen"
assumes the alternative is holding a cheap renewal, and that assumption is false
for any domain bought at auction or aftermarket.

**Ask the owner before the tail is priced**, not after: which domains did he pay
significantly for? Even a partial list — "anything from an auction", a handful
of names, a rough cutoff year — converts an unbounded risk into a bounded one.

## `webdesign.uk` + `webdesign.co.uk` — price as a PAIR, not as two independents

⚠ **They are two distinct domains and must always appear as full strings in any
output** — a conflation here would be expensive. `webdesign.co.uk` is the
business shopfront (**151 deployed pages**); `webdesign.uk` is the one he priced
at seven figures (**14 deployed pages**).

The owner has said *"they will be the same endpoint one day"* — a stated plan to
consolidate. That changes the pricing question:

- After consolidation one is canonical and the other redirects into it. A
  redirect source has real defensive value but little standalone site value.
- **Selling either one alone would break the other**: the buyer of the redirect
  source takes traffic and brand adjacency away from the business that remains.
  A per-domain price cannot express that cost, so two independent prices would
  systematically under-price the pair and over-price the part.
- **Recommendation: quote them as a pair** — one combined figure for both,
  with individual prices only if the owner explicitly wants them separable and
  accepts what selling one does to the other.
