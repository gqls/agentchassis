# COMPARABLES — realised domain sale prices (2026-09-03)

Research for the portfolio valuation lane. **Every figure below carries its source
URL and the date the SALE happened** (not the date I fetched it). Figures are
labelled `[REALISED]`, `[ASKING]`, `[IN-PROGRESS BID]`, `[ANECDOTE]` or
`[UNVERIFIED]`. Nothing here is estimated or interpolated by me; where I found no
data I say so.

## What this does and does not let us conclude

**What it DOES let us conclude:**

1. **The UK aftermarket has a published, realised-sales record and it is good.**
   `domainlore.uk` is ~97% of all reported UK domain sales by count, it publishes
   final prices publicly with no account, and it states it reports *only fully
   completed* sales. Two independent trackers aggregate it. So for .co.uk/.uk we
   are not guessing — we have several thousand realised prices a year.
2. **The realised mean for a UK domain sale is around £750–£900**, stable across
   2025 and 2026 (seven monthly datapoints below). That is the mean of everything
   that sold, dominated by expiring/dropped names with existing SEO backlink
   profiles.
3. **.uk (standalone) realises far less than .co.uk, and is a far thinner
   market** — 3–8% of UK sales by count, and its annual top sale has been an
   order of magnitude below .co.uk's in every year I checked.
4. **There is a hard, current, published floor**: Domainlore's minimum auction
   start is £50, and the venue that used to clear sub-£50 UK names (Flip.uk) has
   closed.
5. **For .com there IS a published median, and it is low**: Sedo's median
   realised .com sale was **$595** in its 2026 Global Domain Report, against an
   overall median of $818. GoDaddy's average aftermarket transaction value was
   **$321** in Q1 2025 (up from $261) and declined slightly in Q1 2026. These are
   the two most representative realised figures in this document.
6. **The developed-site multiple is real, corroborated and ~30x monthly net
   profit** — Empire Flippers' achieved average is 31.29x monthly across 2,233
   sold sites, and Flippa's 2025 realised content multiple of 2.6x annual is the
   same number by another route. Asking multiples run ~38% higher than achieved.
   But the multiple only exists where there is profit (§4.4).

**What it does NOT let us conclude — read these before using any number:**

1. **I found no published MEDIAN for UK domain sales, and the mean is not the
   median.** Every tracker publishes count/total/mean only. The mean is pulled up
   by a small number of five-figure sales (in March 2026 the top 10 of 421 sales
   were 41% of the month's entire value). **Do not price the weak 500 off £875.**
   The honest statement is: mean ~£850, median unknown but certainly far lower,
   and the market's own floor is £50.
2. **The comparable market is an EXPIRED-DOMAIN market, not a portfolio market.**
   Domainlore's buyers are largely SEO buyers purchasing dropped names that carry
   aged backlink profiles. A hand-registered keyword name with no history is not
   the same asset, and there is no published series that isolates it. This cuts
   *against* the portfolio: our names mostly lack the one attribute that market
   pays for.
3. **Conversely, backlinks are not a floor either.** `web-sites.co.uk` — Domain
   Rating 33, 4,637 backlinks, 470 referring domains — sold for **£50 on a single
   bid**. Quality signals do not rescue a weak name.
4. **Reported sales are a subset of all sales.** Brandwise notes "many sales go
   unreported"; private and brokered deals are largely invisible. So the top end
   is *understated* and the record is a floor on what the market does, not a
   census.
5. **Two trackers disagree on the same month** (August 2026: 724 sales / £633,877
   vs 462 sales / £466,596). Coverage differs. Treat any single figure as
   indicative, not exact.
6. **The all-time UK "top sales" list is largely undated and partly unverifiable.**
   I could not confirm the money.co.uk £1.2m or cruises.co.uk £1.1m figures or
   their years from any primary source — see §2.
7. **Sample bias in 2026 aggregates**: Flip.uk (the sub-£100 channel, mean £28–31)
   appears in 2025 monthly reports and is ABSENT from all 2026 reports because it
   shut down. So the 2026 mean is measured on a market whose bottom tier no longer
   has a venue — it is biased *upward* relative to 2025.
8. **Zero UK domains appear in DNJournal's 2026 year-to-date global top 100**
   (cutoff $90,000). This is a real, checkable negative: it bounds the *publicly
   reported* top of the UK market in 2026 below ~$90k. It does not prove no larger
   private UK sale occurred.
9. **No published median exists for .com either — only for Sedo's own book.**
   The $595 figure is the median of what sold *on Sedo*, which is a make-offer
   marketplace with a self-selected inventory. It is not the median of all .com
   sales, and it certainly is not the median of *our* .com names.
10. **Nothing here measures a hand-registered keyword name with no history**,
    which is what most of the weak 500 are. Every comparable market I found
    (Domainlore, GoDaddy auctions, Sedo) is trading either expired names with
    backlink equity or names their owners chose to list. **The portfolio's tail
    is a worse asset than anything in these datasets**, so the numbers here are
    ceilings for it, not estimates of it.
11. **I could not verify the two figures the owner is most likely to have heard**
    (money.co.uk £1.2m, cruises.co.uk £1.1m) — both undated on their only source,
    both absent from primary records I could reach. Do not repeat them as fact.

**CONCLUSION on .uk vs .co.uk — this is the clearest finding in the document:**

- **A one-word commercial generic in standalone .uk realises roughly
  £2,000–£5,000.** That band is remarkably tight across 2024 and 2025 —
  smartphone, laptop, addresses, first, plate, gin, tutor, goldbullion, getaway
  all land in it. The 2026 outlier is digi.uk at £18,000.
- **The equivalent .co.uk realises £6,000–£35,000** (§1.1).
- **So standalone .uk trades at roughly one-third to one-fifth of .co.uk for
  comparable strings**, and its market is ~20x thinner by volume.
- **The tracker's own commentary agrees**: `.co.uk` commands "significantly
  higher prices than standalone .uk domains"
  (https://domainsaleshistory.uk/blog/top-uk-domain-sales-in-2024).
- **Direct relevance to `webdesign.uk`**: the honest comparable set for a
  category-defining one-word/compound generic in standalone .uk is
  **£2,000–£5,000 realised**, with digi.uk £18,000 as the best 2026 outlier and
  no published UK sale of that shape above £55,827 (and that one paired with its
  .co.uk). See §2 for why I would not price it in six figures on this evidence.

## 4. Does an attached live website change the multiple?

**Short answer: yes, dramatically — but only if the site has REVENUE. A site with
content and traffic but no revenue has no multiple at all; it is valued as a
domain plus a modest build premium.**

### 4.1 Achieved versus asking multiples — the key distinction `[REALISED vs ASKING]`

This is the cleanest asking-vs-realised comparison in the whole document, from
one source measuring both sides of the same marketplace (Empire Flippers, the
main broker for content sites), read 2026-09-03:

| Measure | Figure | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Average **SOLD** multiple, all-time (2,233 sites, $481,471,416 total) | **31.29x monthly net profit** | `[REALISED]` |
| Average **LISTING** multiple, 132 live listings ($179,832,407) | **43.22x monthly net profit** | `[ASKING]` |

https://thewebsiteflip.com/review/empire-flippers/
**Asking multiples run ~38% above achieved multiples.** The same page notes 73 of
the 132 live listings had been on market over 3 months — i.e. the asking side is
visibly not clearing.

`[REALISED]` By monetisation, Empire Flippers' 2022 State of the Industry report:
Amazon Associates sites **40.5x**, display-advertising sites **34.6x** monthly.
(2022 data — dated, quoted as such.)

`[ASKING/VALUATION FORMULA]` Empire Flippers' 2026 stated valuation method is
12-month average net profit × a monthly multiple "typically between 30 and 50".
https://exitbid.io/blog/empire-flippers-review-2026 (published 2026-08-12). That
is the *valuation input*, not what sites achieve — the 31.29x above is what they
achieve.

### 4.2 Independent cross-check — Flippa `[REALISED]`

Flippa's 2025 year-end M&A report (published 2025-12-23, covering Jan–Dec 2025)
gives **realised** multiples of ANNUAL profit:

| Asset type | Avg multiple (annual profit) | Top quartile |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS | 2.7x | 5.8x |
| **Content** | **2.6x** | 5.5x |
| Marketplace | 2.5x | 4.5x |
| Apps | 2.4x | 5.4x |
| YouTube | 1.8x | 3.9x |
| Ecommerce | 1.4x | 2.7x |
| Services | 1.2x | 2.1x |

By deal size (realised): **$10K–$100K = 1.8x** annual (top quartile 3.9x) ·
$100K–$250K = 2.1x · $250K–$1M = 2.3x · $1M+ = 2.9x.
https://flippa.com/blog/2025-online-business-ma-insights-from-flippa/

**Cross-check that matters:** Flippa's content figure of **2.6x annual ≈ 31x
monthly**, against Empire Flippers' **31.29x monthly** achieved. Two independent
marketplaces, two different measurement bases, effectively the same number. That
gives me real confidence in **~30x monthly net profit as the achieved content-site
multiple**, and in the rule that **small deals get the low end** (1.8x annual ≈
22x monthly in the $10K–$100K band that this portfolio would live in).

### 4.3 The multiple has moved — and against content `[REALISED]`

- **Flippa: content-site transactions fell 33.5% in 2025**, attributed to Google's
  AI Overviews affecting ~20% of informational queries. Premium content *with an
  authority moat* held its 2.6x; commoditised Q&A content "collapsed".
  https://flippa.com/blog/2025-online-business-ma-insights-from-flippa/
  (Flippa's H1 2025 data separately reported a 37% decline in content-site sales
  volume: https://flippa.com/blog/online-business-data-insights/)
- **Traffic damage behind it** `[REALISED, third-party analysis]`: of 671 travel
  publishers analysed, **32% (213 sites) lost more than 90% of organic traffic**
  after the Helpful Content Update. AI Overviews grew from ~6.49% of searches
  (Jan 2025) to over 50% of queries (Oct 2025).
  https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/google-ai-overviews-top-cited-domains-2025/ ·
  https://www.amsive.com/insights/seo/googles-helpful-content-update-ranking-system-what-happened-and-what-changed-in-2024/

**Implication for this portfolio: build-a-content-site-and-flip-it is a
structurally weaker play in 2026 than the estate's `010-domain-value-maximisation`
ladder assumes.** That ladder should be re-checked against these figures before
it is used to justify holding names for development.

### 4.4 A site with NO revenue — what I could and could not establish

**NO DATA FOUND** for the thing that would actually settle this: a controlled,
published comparison of the same domain sold bare versus sold developed. It does
not exist in any source I could reach. Anyone quoting an uplift multiple for
"add a site" is extrapolating.

What I did find, all weak evidence and labelled accordingly:

- `[ASKING, and stale]` Motion Invest listings, 88 sites listed 1 Jan – 30 Apr
  **2021**: average listing price $28,500, **median listing price $10,970**,
  median monthly profit $300, range $1,000–$250,000. The cheapest was **$1,000
  for a site earning $27/month** (~37x monthly, asking).
  https://thewebsiteflip.com/review/motion-invest/
  ⚠ These are **listing prices, not sold prices**, and they are **five years
  old**. The source says so explicitly. Do not use them as 2026 comparables.
- `[TOOL VALUATION, not a sale]` Flippa's own valuation tool valued a zero-profit
  site at **$1,000** on age, traffic potential and domain quality alone.
- `[ANECDOTE]` Blog and Medium accounts describe zero-revenue sites changing hands
  at $2,000–$5,000 on Flippa, template builds at $800–$1,500, and "premium sites
  with aged domains and content" at $4,000–$7,000; and one account of a $47 domain
  built out in a week and sold for $1,000. **These are individual claims on
  content-marketing pages with no verifiable transaction record. I would not
  price anything off them.**
  https://flippa.com/blog/how-to-sell-a-website-with-no-traffic-or-revenue/

**The honest rule of thumb, stated as such:** a developed site with real net
profit sells at roughly **30x monthly net profit** (achieved, both marketplaces
agreeing), dropping toward **~22x monthly for sub-$100K deals**. Below the point
where there is measurable profit, the multiple mechanism **does not apply at
all** — the asset reverts to being a domain, and the domain comparables in §1–§3
are what govern. A site earning £100/month is worth roughly £2,200–£3,000 on
these multiples; the same name bare, if it is a mediocre .co.uk, is worth
somewhere between £50 and a few hundred pounds. That gap is real and it is the
strongest argument in this document for building rather than selling — **but only
where real revenue actually arrives**, and §4.3 says that is harder in 2026 than
it was.

## ADDENDUM 2026-09-03 — the best comparable is the owner's own, and it is large

**`free.co.uk` sold for approximately £160,000** (owner, stated 2026-09-03,
"some years ago" — no exact date). `[REALISED]`, owner-reported.

This is the **only realised sale price known anywhere in this estate**, and it
is a one-word English dictionary generic in `.co.uk` — precisely the class §1.1
covers. It sits **above** the 2026 public-record UK ceiling (~£42,000) and
between the two historic outliers this research found: crypto.co.uk £100,000
(Aug 2024) and tyres.co.uk £300,000 (Jan 2025). So it is entirely consistent
with the published record while showing that the £6,000–£35,000 band for
one-word generics is **not** the top — the very best words clear six figures.

**Consequences for the model:**

1. It confirms the premium tier is real and large, and that an algorithmic
   appraisal cannot see it. The estate holds **144 single dictionary words**,
   of which the model had valued `free.uk` at **$215**.
2. `free.uk` is the retained sibling of a £160,000 sale. Standalone `.uk`
   generally realises a third to a fifth of `.co.uk` (§1.3c) — but that band was
   derived from names in the £2,000–£5,000 range, and nothing in the public
   record tests the ratio at six figures. **Do not mechanically apply 0.25 to
   £160,000.** It is the owner's most valuable retained name on current
   evidence and needs an individual view, not a multiplier.
3. It is a **years-old** figure. The 2026 market is measurably thinner at the
   top (no UK domain in DNJournal's 2026 global top 100, cutoff $90,000), so it
   should not be read as today's achievable price for a comparable name.
