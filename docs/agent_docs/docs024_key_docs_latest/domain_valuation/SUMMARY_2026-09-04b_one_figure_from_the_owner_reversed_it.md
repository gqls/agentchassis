# Domain valuation — where we are, 2026-09-04 (evening)

**Supersedes `SUMMARY_2026-09-04_the_instrument_had_three_faults.md`, written
four hours earlier. There were four faults, not three, and the fourth is larger
than the other three together.** The earlier file is left standing because the
gap between them is the useful part: it shows what we believed after a full
day of careful work, and what one sentence from the owner did to it.

---

## What we are trying to do

Unchanged. Put a defensible number against every domain the owner holds so he
can sell roughly the bottom 500 and keep the rest. The numbers inform a
conversation about what to agree; they never drop into a live price field.

## Where we have come from

This morning the lane's central finding, held since 09-03, was that the
valuation model **systematically under-values** the best names — the case being
`healthcare.uk`, which cost £40,000 and which the model priced at $149. A full
day's work found three faults, all consistent with that story.

## What we have done

**The owner mentioned that `scales.co.uk` cost him £3,500. The model had it at
$393,917.**

That is ~89× too high, and it is the first of his seven figures to catch the
model being too *generous*. Following it took about twenty minutes and found the
fourth fault, which had been sitting in plain sight all day.

For a `.co.uk` the appraisal tool returns nothing at all, so we look up the
`.com` equivalent and take 15% off. For an invented compound that is a
reasonable stand-in. **For a real dictionary word it is nonsense** — the `.com`
of a real word is a landmark asset and the `.co.uk` of the same word is an
ordinary domain. `scales.com` is valued at $463,432, so `scales.co.uk` was
carried just below it.

**72 domains were being valued that way, and they were 74% of the estate's
entire stated value.** Removing them takes the estate from about $23.7m to
**$6.1m**. We have not invented a better discount — there is no evidence for
what a one-word `.co.uk` is worth, and guessing is what produced the $393,917.
Those 72 are now marked "cannot value" and excluded from the total.

**The sharpest detail is `cartoon.co.uk`.** It has sat in the lane's notes since
yesterday as the example of the model being too *cheap*: $2,934 against the
£5,000+ he paid. Today's valuations made the same domain read **$739,424** —
~187× the other way. The same example, in the same table, inverted overnight,
with nothing in its appearance to show it had moved.

## Where we are now

**872 of 3,023 domains valued (29%). Estate value $6,143,030 across 2,957 rows,
with 72 excluded as not valuable by any method we trust.**

The central finding is corrected: the model does not under-value premium names,
it **cannot value them at all, in either direction**. That distinction is
practical rather than pedantic — "it under-values" invites someone to apply a
multiplier, and a multiplier is precisely what cannot work when the same
instrument is 340× low on one name and 187× high on another.

Two things worth stating plainly. **No price was ever at risk:** all 72 were
already held back from automatic pricing, so none ever carried an asking figure
— the guards worked while the value written beside them was wrong. And **the
inflated $23.7m never reached the owner**; it existed only in the tool's output,
which was checked before it was corrected.
