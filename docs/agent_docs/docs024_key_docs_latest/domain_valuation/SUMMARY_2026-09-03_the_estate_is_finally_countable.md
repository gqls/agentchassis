# The estate is finally countable

*Milestone read-out, 2026-09-03. Written to be read aloud.*

## What we're trying to do

Put a defensible value on every domain the owner holds, so he can sell roughly
the bottom five hundred at keen prices and keep the rest. Two constraints shape
everything. Categories stay together: the sale is assembled from whole weak
groups, never by plucking the weakest names out of a family he is keeping — so
the financial domains stay a financial portfolio. And keen means priced to move,
with a floor at the £150 he already charges anyone who wants a domain
transferred away, because below that a sale is worth less than the fee.

## Where we've come from

Two days ago nobody could say how many domains there were. Four registrars each
held a piece and only one of them, Nominet, held the biggest piece — around a
thousand five hundred .uk names that had never once been successfully listed.
An earlier attempt was believed to work, which had quietly misled three separate
workstreams into treating the recipe as ready. Alongside that, the owner
remembered discussing .co.uk and .uk valuations in an earlier conversation and
asked us to start from it.

## What we've done

We asked each registrar lane for its own list rather than rebuilding their
tooling, and they delivered: Dynadot 453, Porkbun 683, Spaceship 203. The
Nominet lane found and fixed three real bugs in their client — one of which
would have returned an empty list while reporting success at every step — and
produced 1,606 names.

We searched all 646 stored conversations for the earlier valuation discussion
and it is not on this machine. That is an honest miss, reported as one. What we
did recover became the anchors: a $12,000 floor the owner set on relojistas.com,
the £150 transfer fee, and the portfolio's own value-ladder document.

Every domain has been sorted into one of thirty-one categories — a mechanical
pass on the obvious keywords, then careful passes over the long tail. Nothing is
uncategorised.

We found the appraisal tool values *any* domain name, not only ones we own,
which matters because it refuses .co.uk entirely: half the UK estate can be
valued through its .com equivalent instead, clearly labelled as the proxy it is.

Two findings came out of questions the owner asked. He asked which domains were
missing from his registrar lists, expecting them to have expired. Almost none
had — 683 of 692 were simply the Nominet names. Five are genuinely gone, two of
those now running someone else's websites. And when we went looking for a second
opinion to corroborate the appraisals, we found the Afternic prices carry no
per-domain judgement at all: 250 of 419 asking prices are the identical figure.

## Where we are now

**2,945 domains, all categorised, provisionally valued.** The provisional is
load-bearing and we are saying so rather than presenting a number that looks
finished. Only 588 domains — twenty per cent — have their own appraisal, and
Nominet's 1,606 have none at all. The rest currently inherit a category median,
which means that for four names in five the within-category ranking is not yet
real. Since the bottom-five-hundred cut is precisely a within-category ranking
exercise, making it today would mean sorting by a number we invented. So we have
not made it.

The other thing we now know is that repricing is a bigger job than lowering
prices. The Afternic bands don't track quality — names in the $4,999 band and
names in the $25,000 band have the same median appraisal, so equivalent domains
sit five times apart. Some good names will need to go *up*.

## Where we're going

Eight daily appraisal windows, three hundred names each, working two queues:
1,482 that can be appraised directly and 875 that need the .com-proxy route.
The registrar lanes and this one now announce windows to each other, because
three sessions share one daily allowance. When coverage is high enough that
ranking means something, the cut gets proposed by category block for the owner's
decision, and one prices file drives both the Sedo import sheet and the Afternic
bulk change so the two marketplaces cannot drift apart.

Nothing is waiting on the owner. The one thing that would still sharpen the
result is the earlier valuation conversation, if he can find it.
