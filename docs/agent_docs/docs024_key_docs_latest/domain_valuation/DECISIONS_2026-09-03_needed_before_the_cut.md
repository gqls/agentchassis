## 1. What is the UNIT of the cut? (the one that genuinely blocks)

Your instruction was to keep a category together — *"not isolate some that
might be less good yet"*. Applied at the level of whole categories, that
collides with selling 500, because the estate's categories are very lumpy.

`[MEASURED 2026-09-03, structural — does not depend on the appraisals]`
There are **32 categories, median size 64, largest 352**. Selling only whole
weakest categories accumulates: health-medical 106 → pets-vet 180 → travel 261
→ food-drink 316 → **and the next one is financial at 335, which jumps you
straight to 651**. So whole-category selling gives you either ~316 or ~651 and
nothing in between — and 651 means selling *every* financial domain, the
category you named as the one to keep intact.

**Recommendation (1b): make the unit the SUB-category, not the category.**
There are **170 sub-category blocks, median size 7**, so the cut lands where you
want it. Each block is still a coherent family — `financial/insurance` (133
names) stays whole, `financial/mortgages` stays whole — and only genuinely weak
families sell. On the provisional numbers that reaches 500 by selling blocks
like `health-medical/general`, `pets-vet/general`, `travel/general`,
`brandable-short/4-char`, `financial/credit-debt` (11 names) and
`financial/banking-savings` (28) — while the financial category as a whole,
and its mortgage and insurance families entire, stay yours.

The options:
- **1a — whole categories only.** Cleanest promise, but you must accept ~316
  sold, or ~651 including all financial.
- **1b — sub-category blocks (recommended).** Reaches ~500; every family stays
  whole; some categories end up partly sold at the family level.
- **1c — rank within category.** Reaches exactly 500 and ignores the rule you
  gave; recorded only for completeness, not recommended.
