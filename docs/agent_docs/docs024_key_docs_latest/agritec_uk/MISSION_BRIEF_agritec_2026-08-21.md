# Mission brief for `agritec.uk` (Tier-3 fresh submission)

Drafted 2026-08-21 from the decisions in `PLAN_2026-08-21_agritec_uk.md`. This is the
`mission_brief` text for the submission envelope — it seeds the classifier's site-level archetype
and design intent. It is not final page copy.

**Deliberately contains no figures of any kind.** A number written into a spec is treated by the
writers as a given, and a given outranks every anti-fabrication rule we have. Every number on this
site must arrive through the evidence register with a source, and through no other route.

---

agritec.uk is a technical publication for people who operate agricultural systems in the United
Kingdom and need to get the numbers right before they spend money. It covers controlled
environment agriculture and vertical farming, hydroponic nutrient chemistry, insect bioconversion
and alternative protein, seaweed cultivation and the blue economy, and the English agricultural
support schemes that govern what a holding is paid to do.

The reader is a working operator or the person advising one. Growers running or costing a
controlled-environment facility, farm managers and agronomists deciding which scheme actions a
holding can realistically stack, engineers specifying a rearing or cultivation system, and
consultants who have to defend a recommendation to a client. They are numerate and practical, they
are used to reading a datasheet, and they are short of time. They are not persuaded by
enthusiasm, and they have seen a great many confident claims about agricultural technology that
did not survive contact with a real site.

Write for someone who already works in or around this field. Assume they know what a hectare, a
photoperiod and an electrical conductivity meter are. Explain a term the first time it appears
and then use it properly, because the reader wants the real vocabulary rather than a simplified
one. Do not teach from first principles and do not write a beginner's introduction to farming.

What this site offers is mechanism and arithmetic rather than news or opinion. Two things, and
they work together. First, calculators the reader parameterises with their own situation — their
building, their crop, their tariff, their land — that show the working rather than just producing
an answer. Second, technical explainers that teach the physics, chemistry, biology or policy the
calculator rests on, so the reader can tell when a result is telling them something real and when
their assumptions have carried them somewhere silly. Neither half is much use alone: a calculator
without the mechanism is a black box, and an explainer without a calculator is a lecture.

Positioning against the alternatives should be honest and specific. The farming trade press
reports what is happening and we do not compete with it on news. Equipment vendors publish
performance figures for their own products and we do not compete with them on access to those
figures. Government publishes the scheme rules and we never substitute for the actual guidance.
What we do that none of them does is take the mechanism apart and let the reader test it against
their own operation.

Tone: precise, practical and unshowy. Confident about mechanism and careful about outcomes,
because the mechanism is knowable and the outcome depends on a site we cannot see. Never
breathless, never promotional, and never in the register of a technology brochure. British
English throughout, UK units, and the UK regulatory frame — this is not a site that quietly
switches to another country's schemes or another country's grid.

Visual direction: the register of an engineering manual or a field handbook rather than a
brochure. Precise technical illustration — cross-sections, cutaways, isometric system diagrams,
instrument line work — with clean flat shapes, confident linework and generous space. Imagery
should be doing explanatory work, not setting a mood. This is a subject that is very badly served
by its own stock photography, and none of it belongs here: no golden-hour wheat fields, no drone
shots over tractors, no hands cupping soil or seedlings, no smiling farmers leaning on gates.

Diagrams and infographics matter here more than on most sites, because much of this material is
genuinely spatial or process-shaped and a good diagram removes several paragraphs. Any chart or
diagram carrying data must be drawn from real figures the site holds with a source. A picture of
a chart generated by an image model is never acceptable, and neither is a chart drawn from
numbers nobody checked.

This site is educational engineering and agronomic analysis. It is not agronomic advice, it is
not a determination of whether any holding qualifies for any payment, and it is not a
recommendation to plant, build, spend or apply anything. A calculator here models a scenario the
reader has described; it does not know their land, their agreement, their soil or their
obligations, and it must never be written as though it does. Where the consequence of being wrong
falls on the reader — money spent on a lighting rig, a scheme application, a system built — the
writing should be correspondingly careful about what it claims.

The site does not cover cannabis cultivation.

Absolute rule, platform-wide and non-negotiable: never invent a person, a company, a scheme, a
study, a quotation, or a statistic. Every figure needs a real source that can be checked. Where
we do not yet have a sourced figure, say so plainly. "We have not verified this" is always
publishable, and a plausible guess never is. A field asking for a number is not permission to
produce one.
