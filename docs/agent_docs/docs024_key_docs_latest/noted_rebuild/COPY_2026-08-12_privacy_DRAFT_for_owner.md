## The three commitments in this draft — read these first

Everything else below is description. These three are **promises about future
behaviour that only the owner can make**, and I have kept them to the minimum. If
any one of them is not something you want to be held to, strike it and the rest
still stands:

1. **"We do not sell what you write, and we do not use it to advertise to you."**
2. **"We will not use what you write to train anything."**
3. **"If this changes, we will say what changed, on this page, rather than

Number 3 is the one I would fight for. It costs nothing today and it is the

## What I deliberately did NOT write, and why

- **Nothing about what we can or cannot see.** The `writer_block` forbids
  characterising it in either direction, and the old site's "we can't see your
  notes" is banned precisely because it became false the moment there was an
  account to sign in to.
- **Nothing about encryption, of any kind.** Banned, and unbuilt.
- **No "your notes are safe", no "never lose anything".** Both banned, both
  unearnable today, and the second is the exact promise the old app broke for
  anyone who trusted its text-only backup button.
- **No compliance badges** (GDPR / ISO / SOC). Banned, and unverified.
- **No mention of servers, storage locations or architecture.** Your steer. The
  copy says what happens *for the person* — "there when you sign in on a computer
  you have borrowed for ten minutes" — and lets the reader draw the obvious
  conclusion, which is what the `writer_block` asks for.

## One open question I could not answer for you

**Deletion and backups.** The draft says deleting takes a note out of your account.
That is true. What it does not say is that encrypted nightly backups exist with a
30-day object lock, so a deleted note is not gone from every copy the instant you
delete it. Many services disclose exactly this in one sentence; some say nothing.
**I have not written that sentence, because how much to say is your call, not
mine.** If you want it, it would sit at the end of "Removing things" and needs to
name the real retention, not a rounded number.

## THE DRAFT

### Your notes, and what happens to them

Noted is somewhere to put a thought down quickly and find it again later. This page
says what that means in practice.

**What an account is for.** An account needs an email address and a password.
Signing in is what lets you open Noted somewhere else — your phone, your laptop, a
computer you have borrowed for ten minutes — and find your notes there. Without an
account, a note stays in the browser you wrote it in, and only there.

**What you write is yours.** We do not sell it, and we do not use it to advertise
to you. We will not use it to train anything.

**Taking it all with you, whenever you like.** You can save everything to a single
file: your notes, your voice recordings, your photographs, and the earlier versions
Noted kept as you edited. You do not need to ask, and you do not need to be signed
in to save what is already in your browser. The file is plain and it opens without
us.

**We would rather you kept your own copy.** Not because we expect to lose anything,
but because a copy on your own computer does not depend on us at all. If you only
ever do one thing on this page, do that one.

**Removing things.** Deleting a note takes it out of your account. If you would
rather none of it were here, you can close your account, and what is in it goes
with it.

**Getting in touch.** If you want to know what we hold for you, or you want it
removed, write to hello@noted.co.uk and a person will answer.

**If any of this changes.** We will say what changed, here, and we will spell it
out. We will not replace this page quietly and hope nobody re-reads it.
