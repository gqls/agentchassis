Noted is a place to put a thought down quickly and find it again later. Someone opens it, types, and closes the tab; the writing is still there when they come back, on whatever device they happen to be holding. It is for people who want the speed of a scrap of paper without the part where the paper gets lost. The people who use it are not managing projects or building a system: they are capturing a shopping list, a phone number read out too fast, a thought on the way to the station, an idea they do not want to argue with yet. Anything that makes them stop and think about the tool instead of the thought is a failure of the tool. Alongside typing they can record their voice and add a photograph, because some things are faster said or shown than written, and a recording of a person saying something is not replaceable by a transcription of it. Noted has been running as a browser-only tool where the writing lived on one device and could not travel; it is being rebuilt so a person can sign in and reach the same notes from a phone, a laptop or a borrowed computer. That is the whole point of the rebuild and the site should say so in the plainest possible terms. There is an important obligation to the people who already use it: their existing notes are still held in their own browser, and the site must offer them a clear, unhurried way to bring that writing into an account without losing recordings or photographs, and without being made to feel foolish for not having saved a copy. The site needs a page that does exactly that job and explains it in ordinary language. Write for someone who is slightly wary of signing up for another thing, and who has been promised too much by software before. Do not describe the product as powerful, seamless, effortless or intelligent; describe what it does and let them decide. Do not make claims about security, encryption or what can or cannot be seen, because the wording for anything of that kind is supplied by the owner and must not be invented. Do not state numbers of any sort about users, uptime, storage or speed. Short sentences, British English, no exclamation marks, and nothing that sounds like it was written to be scrolled past.
