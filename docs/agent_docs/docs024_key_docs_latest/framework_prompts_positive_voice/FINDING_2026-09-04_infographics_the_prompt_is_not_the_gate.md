## 2026-09-04 — why the planner never chooses `infographic`: the two categories OVERLAP in two of three triggers, and the only worked example demonstrates the overlap

The uplift lane refuted my second candidate too: the two evidence-backed sites (apis.uk, gamedesign.uk)
account for **11 of the 12** illustrations planned since 718 and **zero** infographics, so "nothing to
resolve through" is not the cause either. Their candidate was that rule 13 is a disjunction with
`illustration` named first. That is true and it is not the whole of it.

**The discriminator, verbatim, and it appears exactly ONCE in 32,755 bytes:**

> an `illustration` for a concept, **process** or **scene**, an `infographic` for numbers,
> **comparisons** or **steps**, `icon` entries for feature/benefit groups.

**A process IS a sequence of steps.** A comparison drawn as a picture IS a scene. So two of
`infographic`s three triggers are already claimed by `illustration`, which is named first here AND first
in rule 13's disjunction. **Only "numbers" is unique to `infographic`** — and figures must resolve
through registered facts, which 5 of the 7 sites planned since 718 do not have.

**The single worked example makes it worse, not better.** The one infographic in the exemplar is
`infographic_selection_steps`, and its prompt reads *"A clean vertical flow of four unlabelled stages …
abstract geometric forms connected by arrows, flat design, no text anywhere in the image"*. That is a
**steps** picture with no quantities — it exemplifies the OVERLAPPING trigger, and it is visually
indistinguishable from an illustration of a process. The prompt's only demonstration of the distinction
demonstrates the ambiguity instead.

**This predicts exactly what was observed.** apis.uk's illustration subjects are *"a crowded hive beside
a single solitary bee"* (a comparison, and equally a scene) and *"one comb showing a worker's successive
tasks"* (steps, and equally a process). Both sit in the overlap; both resolved to the first-named option.
12–0 is what an overlapping vocabulary with a fixed reading order produces.

**What would disconfirm this account:** an evidence-backed site whose section presents a genuinely
NUMERIC comparison, where the planner still chooses `illustration`. That case has not been observed —
the uplift lane notes it did not check whether the bee sections carried registered figures, and their
subjects are not numeric. finetuning.uk's £99 against `ft-market-anchor` (~$5,000) is exactly that case
and is the cheapest test of it.

**So the fix is not to reorder a disjunction.** It is to make the two categories non-overlapping and to
let the example demonstrate the unique trigger. Sketch, for the owner to read as bytes:

> an `illustration` where the point is a concept, a process or a scene; an `infographic` where the point
> is a quantity, a comparison of quantities, or an ordered sequence the reader is meant to follow in
> order. If a figure in the section's copy is the reason the picture exists, it is an `infographic`.

with the exemplar gaining a numeric case beside the existing steps one. **Not cut**: it is a second edit
to a prompt whose anchors the 450 lane's 729 has pinned, and the owner reads the bytes first.
