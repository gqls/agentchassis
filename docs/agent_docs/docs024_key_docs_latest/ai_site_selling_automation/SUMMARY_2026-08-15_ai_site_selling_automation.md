# SUMMARY — 2026-08-15 — the shop is finished; the next stage is planned and begun

**What we're trying to do.** Sell AI-built websites with almost no human
effort per sale. A visitor talks to webdesign.uk, a site gets built for £149
all-in, they approve a preview, pay, and take the finished site away. The
positioning is deliberately take-it-or-leave-it: one price, no revisions, no
refunds stated, and the honesty of that is the brand. The next stage extends
delivery: each finished site also published as a live hosted copy, with an
editor the customer can use to change their own site after handover.

**Where we've come from.** Three days ago the live site still sold a retired
offer. Since then, in order: the copy migrated to £149; the migration broke
every call-to-action button on the site and five green checks missed it, which
led to the buttons being locked, a fleet-wide defect being found on nineteen
sites, and another thread fixing it at the platform level; the owner hardened
the terms to no-changes-at-all with the counter-argument written beside them —
the files are yours, and editing a working site beats a blank page; and the
brand landed — Heath Robinson contraptions, the scruffy goose with her golden
egg, cardboard and trade counters, drawn in ink on kraft paper.

**What we've done since the last summary.** The whole visual identity is now
live end to end: five generated hero illustrations, verified by looking at
them, and the site's colours moved to the kraft-and-ink palette — the pages
are now the paper the drawings are made on, with the gold reserved for the one
thing that matters, what the customer gets. The design system held the
approved colour values exactly, and the buttons came out ink rather than gold,
which the owner kept. The FAQ finally names where customers can go to take
their sites further — six services, chosen for quality over affiliate income —
after a five-round struggle that turned out to be a real platform find: the
field every rewrite instruction travels in is connected to nothing, and never
has been. That's filed with evidence, as was the button defect before it. The
build-time promise changed to "usually ready the next day". And the delivery
idea was reviewed properly against a reference architecture: the conclusion
was that our platform already is the engine that document sketches, that
Cloudflare Pages beats Netlify once you demand full automation, and that
customer edits should flow through our own machinery so the existing locking
system referees between their changes and ours. That plan is owner-approved,
written into its own workstream, and its first phase — the next-day promise —
went live the same evening.

**Where we are now.** The site is complete against every ruling: terms, brand,
palette, imagery, services, timings. Every commercial claim traces to an
attested fact, the retired figures are mechanically banned from returning, and
the buttons cannot be deleted by automation. Payment remains deliberately
keyless — the site advertises an offer that cannot yet charge, which is the
right order. Three watchful habits earned their keep this week and are now
standing practice: count the links before and after any rewrite, read the
served page rather than the work item's status, and treat a green check as
proof of exactly what it checked and nothing more.

**Where we're going.** The delivery-and-editor build, in five reviewed phases:
a publish seam putting each finished site on its own hosted copy; the ZIP
deliverable the copy already promises; handover as a recorded state; emailed
magic-link sign-in for customers; and one small editor service through which
customers change their own sites — their edits locked against ours by the
machinery that already referees such things. It starts from a fresh session
with everything it needs written down. Before any of that can take money:
the Stripe keys and the webhook route, which remain the owner's two keys to
turn.
