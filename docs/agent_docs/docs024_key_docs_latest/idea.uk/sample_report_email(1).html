Your idea report

This report is from idea.uk. You asked us to find AI product ideas for a small firm that builds AI tools for solicitors. We came up with a wide range of ideas, then checked each one against what already exists and whether people would actually pay for it. Below are the ideas worth pursuing — with what we found and a cheap way to test each one — followed by the ideas we looked at and set aside, and why.

Who it's for

Small and mid-size law firms that have plenty of their own documents but no in-house software or AI specialists.

Why they'd pay

They would pay because their real problem is turning their own messy, confidential files into something useful, and they cannot safely paste client documents into a public chatbot like ChatGPT.

Ideas worth pursuing (best first)
1. Read scanned forms and drawings into clean data
worth considering

It reads a customer's scanned drawings and pulls out the measurements and details as clean, checked data, instead of someone retyping them by hand.

What it's built on: the scanned floor plans, wiring diagrams and survey sheets your customers email you, using an AI that reads those images and pulls the measurements and details into a tidy table.

What we found: We searched and found that tools like this already exist and have got noticeably better over the past year. The part that is hard to copy is teaching it to read your own customers' drawing styles, which the off-the-shelf tools get wrong.

How it scored: out of 5 — hard to copy 4 · people will pay 4 · easy to build 3 · reusable elsewhere 3 · built to last 3 (17 out of 25 overall)

Risk to you: 4/5 (low — refunds make customers whole)

A cheap first test: Ask one customer for twenty of their old drawings, run them through a basic reader and a version set up for their style, and show them the difference in accuracy.

2. Draft letters in the firm's own style
worth considering

Heads-up: this one may not last — as the general AI models improve, they may do it too.

Heads-up: needs legal and insurance groundwork before building (risk 2/5 — see below).

It drafts letters and contract clauses in the firm's own style and wording, rather than the generic phrasing a public chatbot produces.

What it's built on: the firm's past letters and contracts, using an AI trained on those documents so it writes the way the firm writes.

What we found: We checked and there is a real, paying market for legal drafting tools, several of them charging a monthly fee per person. The part that is genuinely hard to copy is training it on a firm's own past work.

How it scored: out of 5 — hard to copy 4 · people will pay 5 · easy to build 3 · reusable elsewhere 3 · built to last 2 (17 out of 25 overall)

Risk to you: 2/5 (high — needs a person to check every report, insurance, and carefully checked terms before building)

A cheap first test: Check there is demand before building anything: ask three firms whether they would pay a monthly fee per person for a tool trained on their own letters. Do not build it until you have insurance for handling personal data and a solicitor has checked the terms.

Didn't make the cut

We came up with these too, but set them aside — either because they'd be too easy for someone else to copy, or because not enough people would pay for them.

We set it aside because it would be too easy for someone else to copy (hard-to-copy 2/5).

To offer them safely we'd need the right professional qualifications, insurance, and legal cover, so we're flagging them for your awareness rather than recommending them.
