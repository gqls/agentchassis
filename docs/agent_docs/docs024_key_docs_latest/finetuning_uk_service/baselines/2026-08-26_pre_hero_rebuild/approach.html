How We Actually Build These Systems

We pick the model that suits the task, not the vendor we happen to prefer. A document summary, a piece of code, and a reasoning-heavy decision often need three different tools underneath. Tying a business to a single provider means inheriting that provider's weak spots along with its strengths, so we don't do it.

Your data stays yours. That can mean a privately hosted model, a version fine-tuned on your own records using techniques like LoRA, or a model that runs entirely in the browser and never leaves the machine it's on. For a firm handling client files or financial records, that's not a compliance checkbox, it's the actual reason AI adoption stalls in the first place.

Every system we build has review gates you configure yourself. You decide what gets automated outright and what needs a human to sign off before it moves. Nothing runs as a black box that produces answers you can't trace back to a source.

Systems also keep improving after they go live. Automated quality sweeps catch drift and errors between review cycles, and where a system does research or writes content, it checks facts against named sources before publishing anything. That doesn't mean the output is always right, only that you can see where it came from and check it yourself. What we do differently

Multi-model, not one vendor's model

We pick the right model for each task instead of forcing everything through one provider. Text, images, code, and reasoning each have models that do them better than the rest, and we mix them accordingly. If a better tool comes out next year, we switch to it. You're never stuck with a system built around one company's roadmap.

Your data stays yours

We build with private LLM hosting, LoRA fine-tuning on your own data, and models that can run entirely in the browser without sending anything to a server. For a finance or compliance lead, that's the difference between a genuine data risk and a system you can actually sign off on.

Open-weight models, fine-tuned for you

We work with open-weight models like Llama, Mistral, and Phi rather than locking you into a single vendor's closed platform. We fine-tune them on your domain and build custom embeddings around your own material, so the system reflects how your business actually talks and works.

You approve what matters, we automate what doesn't

Every automation pipeline can be built with review gates you control. Routine decisions run themselves, and anything you want a human eye on gets flagged before it goes further. There's no black box deciding things behind your back. If you're not sure yet how much oversight your team actually needs, the AI Project Readiness Checker is a quick way to see where the gaps are before you commit to anything.

Systems that keep improving after launch

Automated quality sweeps check our sites, data pipelines, and tools between human review cycles, not just on delivery day. The system you get in month three should be a bit sharper than the one you got on day one.

Checked against sources, not invented

Where a system does research or pulls facts, we build in source attribution and credibility checks so claims get checked against official sources before anything is published. That said, any AI system can still get things wrong, which is exactly why the review gates above exist.

Questions we get asked before anyone signs anything

How do you decide which AI model to use for our business?

We start with the task, not a favourite vendor. Some jobs suit a large reasoning model, others run better on a smaller, cheaper model fine-tuned on your own documents. We've built with Llama, Mistral, Phi, and several proprietary models, and we mix them where it makes sense, one model drafting text, another checking facts, another handling structured data. The result is a system built for the job in front of us, not whatever a single provider happens to sell.

Does our data leave our building or get used to train someone else's model?

Not unless you want it to. We deploy private hosting, local models, and in-browser models specifically so client data never has to touch a third-party server. Where we do use hosted models, we set them up so your data isn't retained or used for training. This matters more to finance and compliance leads than most vendors admit, so we treat it as a design decision from day one, not a policy document bolted on afterwards.

What happens if the AI gets something wrong?

It will, occasionally, get something wrong. That's why we build human review gates into the parts of a system where a mistake actually costs you money or trust, and automate freely everywhere else. You decide what needs a person's eyes before it goes out and what doesn't. Nothing runs as a black box that you're expected to trust blindly.

Are we locked into one AI provider once you've built our system?

No. We build primarily on open-weight models like Llama, Mistral, and Phi, fine-tuned for your specific use with methods like LoRA and DoRA. If a provider changes their pricing or their terms, you're not stuck. Vendor lock-in is a real cost that's easy to ignore until the bill changes, so we design around it from the start.

Do we need our own technical team to run this once it's live?

No, but you do need someone internally who owns the process the system supports. We handle the model selection, training, and infrastructure. Your team's job is knowing which quotes look wrong, which documents need a second read, and telling us when the business itself changes. That division of labour is described in more detail on our how we work page.

How long does it take to get a working system in place?

It depends on the task and how much your data needs cleaning before a model can use it. A quoting or document-screening system tends to move faster than a multi-agent pipeline handling thousands of records. We'd rather give you a scope on a call than a number that sounds good in a brief. Before that call, try our AI Project Readiness Checker. It's a short set of questions on your data, team, and infrastructure that gives you a read on what's likely to slow things down, and it can be wrong in either direction, so treat it as a starting point rather than a verdict. Book a discovery call and we'll tell you what's realistic for your case specifically.

Does the system just stop improving once it's launched?

No. We run automated quality sweeps after launch so the system keeps getting better between the times a human actually looks at it. That's different to most AI projects, which get tuned once at launch and then quietly drift. If you want to see what that looks like in practice, our case studies describe a few of these systems running in production.

Is AI actually the right answer for every process you're asked to automate?

No, and we'll say so. If a process is broken, badly documented, or changes every month depending on who's covering for whom, automating it just breaks it faster and with more confidence. Sometimes the recommendation is to fix the process first and revisit AI afterwards. We've written about this directly on our position on AI page, and we'd rather lose a sale than sell you a system that fails in month two.

We'll ask what's slow, error-prone, or repetitive in your business, and tell you whether AI is the fix. If it isn't, we'll say so. If you want a sense of where you stand before that call, try our AI Project Readiness Checker first.
