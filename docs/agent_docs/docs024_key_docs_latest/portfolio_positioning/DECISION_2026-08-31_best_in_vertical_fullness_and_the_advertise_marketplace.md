## 2. Never differentiate by OMISSION — copy-but-improve

*"careful not to omit information and tools just because others have them… copy but improve the
offerings that they have, even if it's just slightly better or better positioned for our target
audience."* Assessment of the held briefs: **covered in practice, not guaranteed by mechanism.**
The advertise brief already does copy-but-improve explicitly (UK rules guide *"UK-specific,
unlike the FTC-focused resources"*; Channel Matcher because *"no platform will give an honest
answer"*; the directory *"does not exist in a non-commercial, non-agency-sales form"*). But that
came from good research, same as boxingonline's — and boxingonline proves the intent can die
between research and plan. The exceptions that legitimately DO omit: compliance (buytoletcalculator
staying off rates) and estate-neighbour register claims. Rule of thumb for brief review:
**differentiate by stance and quality, never by absence.**
