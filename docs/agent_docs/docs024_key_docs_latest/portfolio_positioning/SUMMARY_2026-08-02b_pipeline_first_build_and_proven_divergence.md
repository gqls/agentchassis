# SUMMARY — the pipeline built its first site, and the positioning provably steers it

**2026-08-02, night.** Second summary today, and the day earns it: this morning's
summary recorded an error — the pipeline should be building everything, and it had
built nothing. Tonight it has built a site, and the mechanism that is supposed to make
150 sites different from each other is proven rather than recorded.

## What we are trying to do

Unchanged: ~150 finance and insurance domains as substantial sites in deliberately
different directions — built by the pipeline from the submit-domain trigger,
differentiated by configuration the register controls.

## Where we have come from

This morning: four sites live, every one hand-built or hand-adopted; the pipeline
untested on this portfolio; a six-gap list of suspected shortfalls; and a plan to run
the loancash proposition through the pipeline at a shadow domain and fix the pipeline
until it matches the hand-built benchmark.

## What we have done

**The pipeline built lendzy.co.uk.** Submitted at 10:16 with the loancash brief as its
mission, it ran the whole cascade unattended — research, strategy, briefing, plan,
design, imagery, pages, deploys — and it happened to do so while our cluster access was
down for seven hours, which is its own kind of evidence: nobody was watching, and it
built anyway. First deploy commits were landing by 16:22.

**Scored against the hand-built benchmark, the result splits cleanly in two.** What the
mission seam carried, it carried well: the task-labelled navigation arrived exactly as
specified ("Check your loan / Your rights / Free help now", the last a deep link to the
can't-pay page, not a hub — and the brand line became the homepage title verbatim);
topic coverage mapped the brief almost one-to-one; every regulatory figure in prose
sits inside the sanctioned set; rule names appear beside the numbers; no forms, no
lead-generation, nothing that looks like a lender. The failures were specific, not
diffuse: the every-page compliance lines appeared only where individual writers chose
to include them (3 of 15 pages — nothing owns "on every page"); canonical links are not
emitted at all; a favicon is referenced but never generated; the homepage meta
description is empty; the planner imposed two blog pages the brief never asked for; and
the tools — the product, for this portfolio — were refused by a deliberate guard rail
and parked for human review.

**The owner ruled: build them fully.** The tools went through the platform's own tool
pipeline and were built, rerendered and committed in about fifteen minutes — with the
right arithmetic. The price-cap checker carries the 0.8%-per-day rate, the £15 default
fee cap and the 100% total-cost ceiling; the true-cost calculator derives the
credit-union comparator from first principles more precisely than the hand-built
benchmark's rounded constant; the deadline calculator cites DISP 1.6.2R — a more
specific rule than anything we fed it. The blog pages turned out to be a filed platform
bug class (a plan-template page the builder can never build), and wiring the fix means
switching on ongoing news generation for a site that will never serve — left as an
owner call rather than done silently.

**And the proof the whole programme rests on landed at 19:55.** With the register's
positioning seeded into the site's specification and a marker sentence planted there —
and nowhere else — one page was rebuilt. The marker came out in three regenerated
sections, woven into the hero copy: "Our guides are checked against the FCA handbook,
rule by rule." Register row → seeding script → specification → writer's prompt → saved
page. Until tonight every entry in the register was an intention; now it is a
demonstrated control.

The day also produced its own errors, kept visible: two measurement corrections
(counting from component rows what only exists in the assembled artefact — the second
occurrence within an hour of documenting the first), a guard of mine that deferred my
own release, and a new variant of the self-killing shell command. All in WRONG_CALLS
and the scorecard.

## Where we are now

lendzy.co.uk: 18 of 20 planned pages built by the pipeline, tools formula-verified, one
dead target (the favicon), publicly unreachable by design. The benchmark rubric and the
scorecard turn "fix the pipeline" from a mood into a prioritised backlog where every
item carries its evidence. One honest variable: the chassis rolled mid-experiment, so
attempt 1 and attempt 2 ran on different binaries.

## Where we are going

The seam fixes, through the council gate one coherent task at a time: a chrome-level
carrier for every-page invariants (the largest remaining quality gap), canonical
emission, favicon and meta-description generation, the tool handler queueing its own
rerender, links only to built pages, and the planner's imposed blog shape. In-browser
click-through of the tool fixtures once the site can be served. On the owner's queue:
the blog call, and — now that the machine is demonstrated — the build order across the
43 propositions, because the next register row that goes in can come out the other end
as a site.
