## Proposition

WebsitePromotion.co.uk is the definitive UK-oriented practical reference for anyone who needs to grow the visibility and traffic of a website they already own or are about to launch. It covers the full craft of website promotion — organic search fundamentals, content strategy, social media distribution, email list-building, link acquisition, viral and referral mechanics, launch campaigns, influencer outreach, community-building, and analytics interpretation — at the depth a practitioner actually needs, not a surface survey. It exists because the broad advertising world is handled by the cluster flagship (advertise.co.uk), SEO tooling is handled by seotools.co.uk, and web design is handled by webdesign.uk; what remains is a rich, under-served vertical: the ongoing and launch-phase craft of making people find and return to a website through non-tool, non-paid, non-design means. The site's centrepiece is a structured directory of promotion channels, tactics, and services with factual entries; its utility layer includes practical calculators and planners; its editorial layer provides a house view on what actually works; and its news layer tracks the channel changes (algorithm updates, platform shifts, new distribution routes) that make promotion advice go stale quickly.

## Audience

**Primary:** UK small-business owners, freelancers, bloggers, and early-stage startup founders who have a website live or nearly live and are asking 'how do I get people to find it?' — they have moderate digital literacy, limited budget, and no in-house marketing team
**Secondary:** In-house marketers and content managers at SMEs who manage promotion across organic, social, and email channels and need a fast reference or a second opinion on tactics and channel prioritisation

## Reader intent

- Understand which promotion channels to prioritise for a site at their stage and budget
- Learn how to promote a newly launched website and get initial traffic
- Find a checklist or plan for ongoing organic and social promotion
- Look up a specific tactic (guest posting, email list-building, link outreach, etc.) and get a practical how-to
- Identify UK-relevant directories, communities, and platforms where they can list or share their site
- Estimate how long a promotion activity will take to produce results
- Track what has changed on a platform (algorithm, policy, feature) that affects their current strategy
- Find and compare agencies or freelancers who offer website promotion services in the UK

## Stance

WebsitePromotion.co.uk is for doing the work, not just understanding the theory — it takes the side of the site owner who wants honest, ranked, effort-calibrated advice about what actually moves the needle, and it is against vague 'create great content' platitudes that leave practitioners no clearer on their Monday morning task list.

## Differentiation

The dominant competitor in this space is generic digital marketing content from SNHU, Coursera, Investopedia, Forbes Advisor, and HubSpot — all of which cover promotion as one chapter in a broad digital marketing survey, aimed at students or career-changers, and almost entirely US-oriented. They do not provide the practitioner depth, UK-specific references, or interactive tools that a site owner actually needs. Moz and Ahrefs cover SEO tooling in depth but are tool-led rather than craft-led and do not cover the breadth of non-SEO promotion channels. The cluster's seotools.co.uk will handle SEO tooling; this site deliberately avoids SEO tool reviews and deep technical SEO (crawl budgets, schema markup, Core Web Vitals as an engineering task) to stay in its lane. advertise.co.uk handles paid advertising broadly; this site covers paid advertising only at orientation depth — what it is and when to consider it — never campaign management or platform tutorials. webdesign.uk handles design; this site refers readers there for design questions. The combination of UK orientation, practitioner depth, interactive tools, and a directory of named channels and services — on a single-subject site rather than a broad marketing portal — is the competitive gap this site fills.

  **Name:** Link-Building Effort Estimator

## Directory opportunity

Two directories are appropriate. First, the UK Website Promotion Channels Directory: entries are promotion channels and platforms (e.g. 'Google Search organic', 'LinkedIn organic', 'Email newsletter', 'Reddit community', 'HARO/journalist outreach', 'Podcast guesting', 'Content syndication via Medium/Substack', 'Affiliate programme'). Each entry carries: channel type, best site types, estimated monthly time cost, typical time-to-results, free or paid, UK relevance notes, and a getting-started link. Second, the UK Web Promotion Services Directory: entries are named UK agencies and freelancers offering promotion services (content distribution, link-building, PR, social management). Each entry carries: service types, price band, UK location, specialisms, and a link. The first is a reference centrepiece; the second is a commercial-intent resource.

## Open questions

- Portfolio position is absent — a human should confirm whether websitepromotion.co.uk has a recorded position in the cluster register and if so whether its siblings include any site that already covers non-paid website promotion craft, which would require tighter boundary-drawing than this brief can do without that information
- The previous hosted site that is being remade — what did it contain, what traffic did it have, and what pages should be preserved or redirected? This affects the content plan priority order significantly
- Should the agency/services directory include affiliate or referral arrangements with listed providers, or remain purely editorial? This changes the commercial model and the editorial integrity posture
- How UK-specific should the site be? The domain is .co.uk and the brief says UK-oriented, but some promotion channels (Pinterest, TikTok, LinkedIn organic) are global in nature — should US or global audience be explicitly included in scope or kept as secondary only?
- Is there an editorial team or will content be AI-assisted? The news feed and platform-change dispatch sections require genuinely rapid turnaround; if resource is limited these should be deprioritised to aspirational
- Should the glossary stand alone as a reference section or be integrated as contextual definitions throughout the guides? The standalone approach ranks better for definition queries; the integrated approach serves readers better in context — a human editorial decision
- The tool opportunities assume a capable interactive-tool build environment — which tools should be built as true interactive web tools versus presented as downloadable worksheets if the platform is more limited?
