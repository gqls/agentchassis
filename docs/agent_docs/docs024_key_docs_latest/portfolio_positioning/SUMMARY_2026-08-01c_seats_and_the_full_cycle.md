# SUMMARY — the seats doctrine, a decision-complete register, and the first full cycle in a morning

**2026-08-01, late morning.** Third summary today, and the last unless something turns:
each has marked a real inflection — the framework existed (a), the rulings landed (b),
and now (c) the register is decision-complete and the machine has run its full cycle on
a brand-new site without a person touching the middle.

## What we are trying to do

Unchanged: ~150 finance and insurance domains as substantial sites in deliberately
different directions, enforced by configuration. What changed today is that "different
directions" now has a second dimension nobody had named.

## Where we have come from

This morning's open problem was the same-TLD twins — `savingsrates.co.uk` against
`savings-rates.co.uk` — where the register's own doctrine said no differentiating axis
existed and defaulted them to redirects. About forty domains sat in that bucket.

## What we have done

**The owner broke the twin problem with one move: change the reader's seat, not the
reader's segment.** Every axis in the framework had segmented *consumers*. But the same
phrase means different things to the different seats around the market's table — a
shopping list to a saver, a pricing decision to the bank setting the rate, a commission
structure to a broker, a data series to an analyst, a body of rules to a compliance
officer. Those are different audiences with different search behaviour. Encoded as P7,
with the owner's commercial corrections built in rather than smoothed over: the archive
is a section, never a site (no transaction lives near it); **exactly one buyer per
phrase** (two buyer sites on one phrase is the self-competition the register exists to
prevent — this corrects "mainly buyer" for twins, where it cannot work); every seat's
money is named, so commercial intent ranks the seats instead of excluding them; and the
authority route is policy — professional seats link down to the buyer domains, where the
direct revenue is. Cleanest spelling takes the buyer seat because buyers type domains;
awkward spellings take seats reached by search and citation, turning the hyphen from
defect into asset.

**The register is now decision-complete for all 152 domains.** A 35-row seat map covers
every same-TLD variant and — at the owner's mid-stream request — the same lens was
applied portfolio-wide: `loanscentre` becomes the loan-broker layer, `moneyrates` the
cross-product price tracker, `competitiverates` the setter's benchmarking view,
`ratecomparison` the honest-comparison methodology site, `banker.co.uk` the trade title,
`finance.org.uk` the estate's .org citation anchor. Two insurance members are declared
honest residuals — five seats cannot make seven members of one phrase family distinct —
and stay flagged rather than fudged. The referee seat is per-vertical casebooks
(owner-chosen): three or four thick ones, never fifteen thin ones, information about law
and never advice.

**And loancash.co.uk ran the entire machine in one morning.** Built yesterday-morning's
way (register row first), it went: owner adds DNS → live verification (23/24 files
byte-identical, all three rule-checking tools passing a real-browser audit against the
live domain) → locked adoption (18/18 pages owned, **zero** LLM work items, the unlinked
404 page correctly skipped) → byte gate finds all 18 components carrying crawl-mutated
DOM, exactly as measured on both prior adoptions → repaired from repo bytes, 18 written,
0 suppressed → gate re-run 18/18 exact → one rerender released and proven
**byte-identical live** with an empty diff on the platform's own commit → all released →
the L10 positioning written into the fields the writer reads (+1,374 bytes into
`content_direction.formatted`, read back after write). The spec script and the byte gate
were both generalised for their second consumer, so the third site needs neither
rewritten.

## Where we are now

Four sites live: two adopted-and-positioned calculators sites, the mortgagecalculator
lane mid-flight and following its handoff, and loancash — live, adopted, gated,
positioned, and the fastest full cycle yet. The register: 152 domains, 43 entries, every
domain claimed once, zero coordinate collisions, and no remaining pile of undecided
defaults — just two declared residuals and the product holds.

Standing honest markers: loancash's regulatory facts each cite their rule but await a
citation pass against current FCA text before any regeneration is allowed; the platform
still has no cross-site overlap detection, so the register remains the only guard; and
the clusters are name-derived, not search-volume-derived.
