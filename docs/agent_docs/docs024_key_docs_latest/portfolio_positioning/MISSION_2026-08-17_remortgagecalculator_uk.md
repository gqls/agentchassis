remortgagecalculator.uk is for one person: a UK homeowner whose fixed rate ends within the next six months, who has just realised they need to do something and does not yet know what. They are typically 30 to 60, they already own, and they are moving from owning to shopping. They are not looking for education about mortgages in general — they have a deadline and a number they need.

The site's job is to give that person the 10-second answer first: what will my payment be when the fix ends, and what would it be if I moved to something else. The calculator is the product. Everything else on the site exists to get someone to it, explain what it just told them, or tell them what to do next. This is the instrument, not the magazine — a .uk domain in this portfolio means tool-first.

After the number, the second job is the deadline. Someone six months out has a sequence to run: when to start looking, how long an application takes, when the early repayment charge stops applying, what to have ready. Checklists and timelines are the right shape for this, not essays.

Tone is urgency without alarm. The deadline is real and the site should not be vague about it, but this reader is already anxious and does not need pressure language, countdown theatrics, or "act now". Calm, specific, and on their side. Assume intelligence, not knowledge: they can follow a clear explanation, they just have not had to think about this since they took the mortgage out.

Some hard limits, and they are not stylistic.

This site is not a regulated firm, does not give advice, and must never imply either. It does not publish rates as fact. It has no verified facts registered yet, so it must not state a rate, threshold, fee or saving as though it were current — worked examples in the calculator are fine and must be obviously hypothetical. Where something depends on the reader's own lender, the honest sentence is that it depends on their lender. "We have not verified this" is always publishable; a confident guess never is.

Where the site does name real lenders, it draws on the verified lender directory, and only for non-price facts: regulator status, the kinds of product they offer, who underwrites them, how long they have been going. Never a rate, APR or fee attached to a named firm. A stale price under a regulated firm's name is a financial promotion, which is a different kind of website with a different set of obligations.

On scope: this site covers remortgaging — moving an existing mortgage. It is not the place for first-time buyer material, not a rate comparison table, and not debt consolidation, each of which belongs to a different site in the portfolio. Where a reader needs one of those, point them onward rather than half-covering it here.

What success looks like: someone lands from a search like "remortgage calculator", gets their number within ten seconds without signing up for anything, understands what it means, and leaves knowing the two or three things they should do in the next month.
