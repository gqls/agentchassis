# SUMMARY — the rulings landed, and the framework built its first greenfield site in sixteen minutes

**2026-08-01, mid-morning.** Second summary today — justified because the read-out has
genuinely changed since the morning one: two owner rulings were taken and executed, every
register entry now carries full target-market coordinates, and the portfolio produced its
first brand-new site end to end.

## What we are trying to do

Unchanged: ~150 finance and insurance domains as thick sites in deliberately different
directions, with the differentiation enforced by configuration — a register every lane
must write to before building, and a check with teeth, because the platform itself would
never notice two sites converging.

## Where we have come from

This morning the framework existed but had decided nothing bespoke: 42 propositions, a
claims table, a green check, and two piles of open decisions — the twin pairs, and
whether `loancash.co.uk` should exist at all.

## What we have done

**Ruling one — "split the co.uk and .uk."** Cross-TLD twins are no longer defaulted to
redirects. One portfolio-wide convention differentiates them on the axes that survive an
identical phrase: **`.co.uk` is the authority** (thick, guide-led, the 20-minute answer);
**`.uk` is the instrument** (tool-first, the 10-second answer), cross-linked as siblings
with self-naming canonicals. The ~15 *same-TLD* twins — hyphen and plural variants —
remain owner calls, because within one TLD no axis exists at all.

**Ruling two — build `loancash.co.uk`, as the borrower's champion of the FCA rulebook.**
The register had recommended against building it; the owner inverted the risk into the
point, and the original recommendation stays visible in the entry because the inversion
is the interesting part. The most vulnerable borrowing query in the portfolio now lands
on protection: three rule-checking tools (a price-cap checker, a true-cost calculator, a
complaint-deadline calculator) and eleven guides, one FCA protection each — the price
cap, authorised-lender checks, affordability refunds, stopping CPA payments, the rollover
limit, complaining to the Ombudsman, forbearance rights, loan sharks, the cheaper routes,
the per-product rulebooks, and a jargon buster. Every page states the two identity facts:
not a lender, and independent of the FCA.

**How long it took, measured from git and CI rather than memory:** the instruction
arrived just after 09:06; the finished site was committed at **09:22:40** and the deploy
run confirmed **24 of 24 files in storage at 09:23:20** — **about sixteen minutes** from
instruction to verified-and-deployed, including: the register row written first (the
portfolio's own rule), all content authored fresh, both build assertions mutation-tested
red, all three tools passing a real-browser audit, and the cap checker's arithmetic
hand-verified on a constructed breach case and a clean case. The full wrap — workstream
docs, the register enrichment below, and the commit trail — took roughly twenty minutes
more. That speed is the framework working: the chassis, the assertions, the verification
habits and the register discipline all existed before the site did.

**And the enrichment the owner asked for mid-stream:** every family now carries the same
target-market depth the mortgage entries had — age, stage in the process, purchase size
and type, experience, emotional register — across all 43 entries. Doing it surfaced axes
a thinner pass would have missed: business finance keys on the *company's* age, not the
person's; the £85k FSCS boundary is standing content for every savings site; home
insurance's angle is that renewal-letter urgency is real but artificial; professional
indemnity's buyer is triggered by a contract clause, not fear; landlord insurance hides a
novice segment — the accidental landlord — that the "rates" grammar underserves.

## Where we are now

The register: 152 domains, 43 entries, every domain claimed exactly once, zero coordinate
collisions, check green. Two sites live and adopted, one handed off and moving
(the mortgagecalculator lane followed the handoff correctly — repo before adoption, then
fixed the two links that hid real pages from the crawl), and one — loancash — built,
verified and sitting in storage awaiting its serving path.

Honest markers: the loancash regulatory facts are each tied to their named rule but have
not had a citation pass against the FCA's current text — that is the gate before the
framework ever rewrites those guides. And the proposition clusters remain name-derived,
not search-volume-derived.

## Where we are going

Owner actions: the Cloudflare zone and Workers Route for `loancash.co.uk` (storage is
populated; it serves the moment the route exists), the ~15 same-TLD twin calls, and build
order across the propositions. Then the machine repeats: register row → build through the
proven chassis → verify against the live origin → locked adoption with the hold-first
byte gate → positioning into the live specs. The crossing-point site's two open items
continue in parallel: decomposing its guides for genuine framework authorship, and the
end-to-end proof that a positioning spec changes what gets written.
