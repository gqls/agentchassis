## What the site is

copyonline.co.uk is the UK's practical authority on writing commercial copy in the age of AI —
for the founder, in-house marketer or junior copywriter who now drafts with a machine and has
to decide what to keep. It opens on the question everyone in this field is actually asking:
what AI is genuinely good at, what it is reliably bad at, and how to get usable commercial
copy out of the combination. Around that it teaches the craft itself through thorough guides,
worked examples and checklists covering web pages, ads, email, product descriptions and tone
of voice, and lets visitors test their own drafts with free browser tools: a headline scorer,
a readability and clarity checker, a call-to-action tester and a length counter. It earns
authority by showing its working — explaining the principle behind every rule and being
specific about where machine output falls down — and it stays squarely on copy QUALITY,
leaving channel strategy to advertise.co.uk, search visibility to websitepromotion.co.uk and
SEO tooling to seotools.co.uk. One page in the navigation, and only one, converts: a lead
route for readers who decide they want a human to write it.

## Who it is for

**Primary:** UK founders, in-house marketers and small-business owners who must write their own commercial copy and know it matters but have no formal training — they are sitting in front of a blank page for a product page, ad or welcome email and need practical craft guidance right now

**Secondary:** Freelance and junior copywriters building their skills and professional vocabulary; agency teams who need a reliable reference to send clients or brief against; non-native English writers working in UK commercial contexts who need ASA/CAP-aware guidance


## What the reader came to do

- Understand how to write a specific type of copy (homepage, product page, email subject line, ad headline, CTA, tone-of-voice document) with a concrete method they can follow
- Check or improve a draft they have already written using a free browser tool
- Learn the principles behind good commercial copy — why certain structures work, what motivates a reader to act
- Find a checklist they can run through before publishing or sending
- See a worked example or before-and-after rewrite of copy similar to their own
- Understand UK-specific constraints: ASA/CAP rules for ads, plain English expectations, sector-specific tone considerations
- Get a quick reference answer to a specific copywriting question (word counts, subject-line length, CTA button copy conventions)
- Understand tone of voice — how to define, document and apply a consistent brand voice across channels

## How it differs from the neighbours — including the AI angle

The obvious UK competitors are: Copyblogger (US-centric, content marketing focus, not
commercial copy craft), the Drum (trade news, not a teaching resource), ProCopywriters (member
association, not a teaching or tool site), Hemingway App (single readability dimension, no
copy-type guidance, no UK context), CoSchedule Headline Studio (headline tool only, US-framed,
paywalled for full use). copyonline.co.uk is distinct in three ways: (1) it is explicitly UK-
registered in voice, spelling, and regulatory context (ASA/CAP throughout); (2) it combines
editorial craft teaching with free browser tools in one place, so a reader can read the guide
and immediately test their draft without leaving; (3) its editorial layout — warm paper
background, serif display headings, reading-first — is deliberately unlike both the tool-
portal aesthetic of seotools.co.uk and the magazine-grid of designblog.co.uk, signalling a
different kind of authority. The Sarah Turner Agency YouTube content surfaced in research
targets freelance copywriters building businesses (invoicing, client acquisition, community);
copyonline.co.uk targets people writing copy, not people running copywriting businesses — a
distinct and underserved intent. THE OPENING ANGLE (owner direction, 2026-09-03): AI is the
biggest force acting on this audience — threat and opportunity at once — and the honest
position is that current AI is genuinely poor at commercial copy in specific, nameable ways:
it defaults to a house style nobody chose, pads to length instead of structuring, invents
specifics, cannot hold a documented tone of voice across a page, and writes claims a UK
advertiser cannot substantiate under the CAP Code. That is the site's way in, and it is a
competitive advantage precisely because the obvious competitors either sell AI tools or ignore
AI. The site is neither an AI booster nor an AI sceptic: it is specific. Every claim about
what AI does badly must be demonstrable in a worked before-and-after, not asserted.

## Tools it proposes

- **Headline Scorer** — Headline quality is the highest-leverage single intervention in commercial copy and there is no free, UK-focused, browser-based tool that scores headlines with plain-English feedback rather than a US-centric emotional-marketing framing — A headline or subject line of up to 100 characters — Numeric score (0–100) with breakdowns for word count, power-word presence, emotional vs rational balance, clarity, and character count against platform limits; three specific improvement suggestions
- **Readability and Clarity Checker** — Commercial copy clarity is distinct from academic readability; existing tools (Hemingway, Grammarly) are US-English-biased or paywalled; this is a direct, fast, no-login alternative for UK practitioners — A block of commercial copy, minimum 50 words — Flesch-Kincaid reading age, average sentence length, passive-voice sentence count and percentage, jargon-flag list (industry terms detected), overall clarity verdict (Clear / Review / Revise) with highlighted problem sentences
- **Call-to-Action Tester** — CTA copy is under-served by existing tools which check grammar but not persuasive structure; a focused CTA tester fills a clear gap for the non-specialist writer who knows their CTA is weak but not why — A CTA phrase (1–8 words) and optional context field (what the page or email is about) — Rating on verb strength (weak/moderate/strong), specificity score, estimated character count vs platform norms, pattern match against high-performing CTA structures, and three alternative CTA suggestions
- **Length and Character Counter** — Writers constantly context-switch between formats with different constraints; a single reference tool that shows all format limits simultaneously against a live count saves the tab-switching and mental arithmetic that slows down real work — Pasted copy of any length — Live word count, character count with spaces, character count without spaces, and a comparison table showing whether the paste fits within recommended limits for: Google Ads headline, Google Ads description, email subject line, SMS (160 chars), LinkedIn post, Twitter/X post, meta title, meta description, Amazon product title
- **Tone-of-Voice Consistency Checker** — Brand voice consistency is a known pain point for growing teams and agencies managing multiple writers; no free browser tool currently addresses this; it directly supports the tone-of-voice guide content — Two or more text samples labelled as being from the same brand — Side-by-side analysis of formality register (formal/neutral/casual score per sample), pronoun balance (we/you ratio), average sentence length per sample, vocabulary overlap score, and a consistency verdict with flagged divergences
