## Proposition

designblog.co.uk is the UK's sharpest editorial voice on design across all disciplines — graphic, brand, product, UX, industrial, motion, type and spatial — published as a genuine blog with a real feed cadence and a strong point of view. It exists for designers, creative directors, brand managers and culturally alert non-designers who want commentary, critique, inspiration and trend intelligence rather than tutorials or tooling instructions. Where competitors offer encyclopaedic how-to libraries or academic syllabuses, this site offers opinion-led writing, curated inspiration, industry critique and a living reference layer — the design equivalent of a respected trade magazine that also happens to maintain a useful directory and glossary. It is editorially led, .co.uk-rooted, and sits inside the advertise.co.uk marketing cluster as the vertical authority on design as a cultural and commercial practice.

## Audience

**Primary:** Mid-career designers, creative directors and brand strategists in the UK who already know their tools and want informed opinion, trend intelligence and peer-level commentary on where the discipline is going — people who read design press the way journalists read media press
**Secondary:** Marketing managers, founders and product leaders who commission design work and want to understand what good looks like, what is happening in the field, and how to talk to designers — design-literate but not design-trained

## Reader intent

- Read commentary and opinion on a current design trend, controversy or cultural moment in the industry
- Find inspiration — curated examples of outstanding work across branding, editorial, product, spatial or motion design
- Understand what a design term or concept means, from a glossary that treats them as practitioners use them
- Discover or research design studios, agencies and independent practitioners in the UK via a named directory
- Follow the design news feed for industry moves, award results, studio launches, campaign critiques and tool announcements
- Get a considered take on AI's effect on design practice — which roles, tools and creative processes are actually changing
- Find curated reading lists, influential books and canonical references on design history and theory
- Use a comparison or selector tool to understand the landscape of design tools, award bodies or studio types

## Stance

designblog.co.uk is pro-craft, pro-rigour and pro-opinion — it believes design matters too much to be left to listicles and tool tutorials, and it is against the homogenisation of visual culture, the uncritical embrace of AI-generated aesthetics, and the reduction of design discourse to career advice and software walkthroughs

## Differentiation

The primary competitors surfaced by research are IxDF (ixdf.org) — a massive UX/UI education platform with 1,750+ articles, expert-authored, academically credentialled, but US-focused, education-oriented and essentially a course funnel dressed as a blog; Rasmussen University's design blog — career-advice content written for prospective students, not practitioners; Adobe Design (adobe.design/ideas) — high quality but product-adjacent and US-centric; and Stéphanie Walter's personal blog — practitioner-quality but personal rather than institutional. The YouTube design commentary space (Jesse Showalter etc) covers tools and career but not editorial critique or UK industry. None of these is a .co.uk-rooted editorial publication with a genuine directory, a glossary, and an opinionated house voice covering design across all disciplines as a cultural and commercial practice. designblog.co.uk is specifically not a course platform, not a career-advice site for students, not a how-to manual (that is webdesign.co.uk's job for the web discipline specifically), and not a service shopfront. The gap is: practitioner-grade, editorially independent, UK-anchored design commentary with reference infrastructure.

## Content plan

-
  **Kind:** news
  **Name:** The Design Feed
  **What:** A regularly updated editorial feed covering industry news: studio launches and closures, campaign critiques, award shortlists and winners, notable commissions, tool announcements read through a design-culture lens, and personnel moves at major agencies. Published at minimum three times a week to honour the blog format the domain name implies.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** editorial
  **Name:** Criticism & Commentary
  **What:** Long-form opinion pieces and reviews on design work, movements and decisions — a rebrand autopsy, a critique of a design system gone wrong, a defence of maximalism, a take on whether AI-generated imagery is aesthetically converging. Written with a named editorial position and designed to provoke response.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** editorial
  **Name:** Inspiration Showcases
  **What:** Curated collections of outstanding design work organised by discipline, medium, geography or theme — not a gallery dump but an edited selection with commentary explaining why each piece is notable. Covers branding, editorial, packaging, motion, spatial, digital product and type.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** Design Movements & History Explainers
  **What:** Authoritative, jargon-honest guides to design movements, schools, periods and philosophies — Bauhaus to Brutalism to Swiss International Style to contemporary post-digital aesthetics. Written for practitioners who want depth, not students who want exam notes.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** data
  **Name:** Design Glossary
  **What:** A comprehensive A–Z glossary of design terms, concepts, methodologies and proper nouns — from 'affordance' and 'bleed' to 'design thinking' and 'visual hierarchy' — defined as designers actually use them, not as academics define them for outsiders. Each entry is a short, confident, opinionated definition with cross-references.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** directory
  **Name:** UK Design Studios Directory
  **What:** A searchable directory of named UK design studios and independent practitioners, organised by discipline, region and specialism. Each entry carries: studio name, founding year, disciplines covered, notable clients or work, size band, and a short editorial note. A reference readers return to when researching partners, competitors or the industry landscape.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** research
  **Name:** Annual State of Design Report
  **What:** An original aggregated analysis of UK design industry conditions: tool adoption, rates, employment, AI integration, studio health and aesthetic trends — drawing on publicly available surveys, award data and editorial observation. Published annually as a citable, shareable document.
  **Priority:** aspirational
-
  **Kind:** editorial
  **Name:** AI & Design: Running Commentary
  **What:** A dedicated editorial thread tracking AI's actual effect on design practice — which tools are being adopted, which roles are changing, what the aesthetic consequences are, and what practitioners say off the record. Updated as developments happen rather than as a single explainer.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** tool
  **Name:** Design Award Finder
  **What:** An interactive tool that lets a designer or studio input their discipline, budget and project type and receive a filtered, ranked list of relevant design awards — D&AD, Brand Impact Awards, Design Week Awards, Core77, Red Dot and others — with entry deadlines, fee ranges and reputation notes.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** tool
  **Name:** Studio vs Freelance Calculator
  **What:** A day-rate and cost comparison tool that lets a user input project scope, duration and requirements and see an indicative comparison of typical studio vs freelance costs in the UK market, with caveats about what each model implies for process and output.
  **Priority:** aspirational
-
  **Kind:** tool
  **Name:** Design Tool Landscape Selector
  **What:** A guided selector that takes a designer's discipline, workflow and team size and surfaces the most relevant primary and secondary tools from the current landscape — Figma, Framer, Adobe, Affinity, Procreate, Spline etc — with a brief editorial take on each, updated as the market changes.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** data
  **Name:** Design Awards Calendar
  **What:** A reference table of major design awards worldwide — name, discipline scope, entry window, fee tier, prestige tier and a one-line editorial note on who enters and why. Filterable by discipline and updated annually.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** editorial
  **Name:** Designer Profiles & Interviews
  **What:** In-depth profiles of working designers, creative directors and studio founders — not puff pieces but proper editorial interviews exploring creative philosophy, process critique and industry perspective. Both established names and emerging practitioners.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** Design Careers: The Honest Picture
  **What:** Editorial guides to design career paths — not how-to-get-hired advice but honest assessments of what different roles and sectors actually involve, how they are changing under AI and economic pressure, and what experienced practitioners say about them. Complements without duplicating webdesign.co.uk's technical focus.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** research
  **Name:** Trend Dispatches
  **What:** Periodic shorter-form analytical pieces identifying emerging visual, cultural and commercial trends in design — colour cycle predictions, revival movements, typographic shifts, branding directions — with reasoning and examples rather than empty assertion.
  **Priority:** valuable

## Tool opportunities

-
  **Why:** Designers regularly ask which awards are worth entering for their specific work; no clean tool exists for this and it would drive repeat visits around award season
  **Name:** Design Award Finder
  **Input:** Designer's discipline (branding / product / packaging / motion / type / UX / illustration / spatial), project type, and rough budget for entry fees
  **Output:** A filtered, ranked list of relevant design awards with entry windows, fee ranges, discipline fit score and a short editorial note on prestige and who tends to win
-
  **Why:** The tool landscape is genuinely confusing and changing rapidly; a selector backed by editorial credibility is more useful than a comparison matrix and drives SEO on high-intent queries
  **Name:** Design Tool Landscape Selector
  **Input:** Designer's primary discipline, team size, platform preference (browser / desktop / iPad) and key workflow needs
  **Output:** A ranked shortlist of recommended primary and secondary tools with an editorial take on each — not a specs table but a practitioner's recommendation
-
  **Why:** Rate transparency is a perennial concern in the design industry; a credible benchmarker would be heavily shared and return visitors across career stages
  **Name:** Day Rate Benchmarker
  **Input:** Discipline, years of experience, city/region in the UK, and engagement type (freelance / permanent / contract)
  **Output:** An indicative day-rate or salary range benchmarked against industry survey data, with caveats and links to primary sources

## Directory opportunity

A UK Design Studios Directory is a strong centrepiece. Entries would be named studios and independent practitioners: each entry carries studio name, city/region, founding year, primary disciplines (branding, product, packaging, spatial, motion, type, UX, illustration), size band (solo / 2–10 / 11–50 / 50+), a selection of notable clients or campaigns, award history if relevant, and a short editorial characterisation of the studio's aesthetic or cultural position. The directory would be filterable by discipline, region and size, and would be the go-to UK design industry reference that no competitor currently owns at this quality level.

## Must nots

- Must not publish step-by-step tutorials or how-to technical instruction on web design or development — that is webdesign.co.uk's territory and competing for it would split the cluster
- Must not position as a course platform, education provider or skills-training destination — that directly competes with IxDF and is not the editorial brief
- Must not list design services for sale, position as an agency, or accept briefs — webdesign.uk is the cluster's service shopfront
- Must not publish negative-identity claims in site copy such as 'we do not sell courses' or 'we are not an agency'
- Must not claim to offer regulated advice of any kind — design consultancy that constitutes professional services advice is not within scope
- Must not attempt to cover advertising-as-a-subject broadly — that breadth belongs to the flagship advertise.co.uk; design's role within advertising may be covered editorially but the advertising vertical itself defers upward
- Must not publish sponsored content, native advertising or paid directory listings without clear, unambiguous editorial separation and disclosure labelling
- Must not scrape or republish third-party design work without attribution and licence verification — copyright in creative work is particularly sensitive in this community

## Open questions

- Portfolio position is unrecorded: this domain has no entry in the portfolio register. Its relationship to webdesign.co.uk (which owns the web-design discipline) needs a formal boundary decision — specifically: can designblog.co.uk cover UX/product design editorially, or does that encroach on webdesign.co.uk? The brief assumes UX coverage is permitted editorially (commentary, critique, trends) but not technically (tutorials, methods instruction). A human should confirm this boundary.
- The owner states this is a remake of an existing hosted site — what content, audience and traffic does the existing site have? Knowing the current baseline would sharpen priorities considerably and may reveal established audience expectations that should not be broken in the remake.
- Feed cadence commitment: the blog format implied by the domain name and the owner's brief requires genuine editorial output. What is the realistic publishing frequency and who is responsible for it? The brief plans for a minimum three-times-weekly news feed — is that staffed or AI-assisted, and does the owner accept that lower cadence would undermine the core proposition?
- UK vs global scope: the .co.uk TLD implies a UK-primary audience and the directory is planned as UK-focused. Should the editorial coverage (inspiration showcases, trend dispatches, designer profiles) be UK-prioritised, or is it global with a UK editorial voice? This affects SEO strategy and audience positioning.
- The cluster sibling relationship with advertise.co.uk needs boundary clarification: design for advertising (campaign design, brand identity in advertising contexts, motion for ads) is legitimately this site's territory editorially, but could a future advertise.co.uk editor reasonably claim it? A written boundary ruling would prevent future conflict.
- Monetisation model: the brief does not specify how this site generates revenue. Knowing whether the model is display advertising, affiliate links, directory listings, sponsored content or something else would affect which content types to prioritise and what the must-nots around commercial relationships should be.
