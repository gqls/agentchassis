## Proposition

IndoorPlanters.co.uk is the UK's dedicated resource for anyone who wants to house plants well — covering the containers themselves (ceramic, terracotta, self-watering, hanging, floor-standing), the plants that go in them, and the practical care knowledge that keeps both looking good. It exists because most houseplant advice is plant-first and treats the pot as an afterthought, while most pot-shopping content ignores plant welfare entirely. This site holds both together, for a British audience, in one place.

## Audience

**Primary:** UK houseplant owners aged 25–50 who are actively shopping for a planter or trying to match a plant to the right container — at the moment of purchase decision or after bringing a new plant home
**Secondary:** Interior-conscious renters and homeowners who use plants as decor and want pots that look deliberate, not accidental

## Reader intent

- Find the right type of planter for a specific plant (succulent, monstera, fiddle leaf fig, orchid, etc.)
- Understand drainage options and whether a pot without holes is safe to use
- Browse and compare planter materials — ceramic, terracotta, self-watering, fibreglass, concrete, woven
- Get care guidance for a specific houseplant they already own
- Discover which indoor plants suit a specific room condition (low light, humid bathroom, sunny windowsill)
- Learn the scientifically-supported wellbeing and air-quality benefits of indoor plants
- Find where to buy planters in the UK — retailers, independent makers, online shops
- Get styling advice on how to arrange and display plants and pots together

## Stance

IndoorPlanters.co.uk is for the plant owner who cares about keeping things alive as much as making things look good — against the trend of treating pots as pure decor objects divorced from plant health, and against vague care advice that gives no concrete guidance.

## Differentiation

The UK market has no dedicated indoor planters site that holds plant care and container advice together. Competitors fall into three camps: (1) general houseplant care sites (Homestead and Chill, The Spruce) that treat pots as secondary; (2) retailer blogs (Plantatorem, Greenhouse Studio) that are thinly disguised product catalogues; (3) academic and scientific sources (PMC, ScienceDirect) inaccessible to a general reader. IndoorPlanters.co.uk occupies the gap: UK-specific, combining honest science-backed care advice with practical container guidance, without being a shop. The .co.uk domain signals UK relevance immediately, which none of the main competitors can match.

## Directory opportunity

A directory of UK indoor planter retailers and makers is both achievable and differentiated. Each entry would carry: name, type (independent ceramicist / online retailer / garden centre chain / marketplace seller), price tier (budget / mid / premium), signature offering (e.g. handmade terracotta, self-watering systems, large floor pots, succulent-specific), ships-to-UK flag, and a one-sentence description. Approximately 30–60 entries would constitute a genuinely useful resource. No equivalent exists in focused UK form.

## Must nots

- Must not sell planters or plants directly or act as a retailer or marketplace
- Must not claim that indoor plants cure, treat, or prevent any medical condition — the wellbeing evidence must be presented as research findings with appropriate caveats, not health claims
- Must not present air-purification claims at NASA-study levels without noting that the effect at realistic indoor plant densities is modest — this is a well-documented overstatement in the popular press
- Must not recommend specific pesticides or fungicides without noting that readers should follow UK product label law and that some products legal elsewhere are not approved in the UK
- Must not present itself as a regulated health, medical, or environmental advisory service

## Open questions

- Is this site intended to carry affiliate links to UK planter retailers, or to remain entirely non-commercial? This determines how the directory and product mentions are framed.
- Should the site cover outdoor planters at all, or remain strictly indoor? The domain name implies indoor-only but some readers will have overlapping needs.
- Is there a specific plant or planter niche the owner wants to lead with (e.g. succulents, large statement plants, self-watering systems) that should anchor the early content build?
- Should the care guides cover the full range of popular UK houseplants (30–50 species) from launch, or start with a focused set of 10–15 most searched?
- Does the owner have any existing relationships with UK ceramicists or retailers that would seed the directory, or will it need to be built from desk research?
