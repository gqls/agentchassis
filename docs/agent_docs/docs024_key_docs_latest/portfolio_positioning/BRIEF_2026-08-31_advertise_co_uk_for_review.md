## Proposition

advertise.co.uk is the authoritative plain-English guide to advertising itself — what it is, how it works, who pays for it, and what it does to people who see it. Aimed at UK business owners, marketers, students, and curious consumers, it exists to explain the mechanics and economics of advertising across every medium: from the auction milliseconds that place a banner ad to the creative principles behind a TV spot, from the legal rules governing claims to the growing public debate about data, attention, and harm. The domain name is a strong generic asset that warrants a genuinely broad, reference-quality treatment of the discipline — not a service that does advertising, but a resource that demystifies it.

## Audience

**Primary:** UK small-business owners and marketing managers who spend money on advertising and want to understand what they are buying, how platforms work, and how to avoid wasting budget — arriving with questions like 'what is programmatic advertising', 'how does Google Ads bidding work', or 'what are the ASA rules on claims'
**Secondary:** Marketing and business students, journalists, policy researchers, and consumers who want to understand why they see the ads they see and what rights they have

## Reader intent

- Understand how a specific type of advertising works (Google Ads, Meta ads, TV, out-of-home, programmatic, direct mail)
- Learn what advertising is legally allowed to say — truth-in-advertising rules, ASA codes, substantiation requirements in the UK
- Understand how targeted advertising uses their data and what that means for privacy
- Compare advertising channels to decide where to spend a budget
- Learn the vocabulary of advertising (CPM, CPC, CTR, programmatic, DSP, SSP, attribution, ROAS)
- Understand how ad auctions work in real time
- Find out what the major platforms (Google, Meta, Amazon) actually offer advertisers and on what terms
- Understand the public debate around advertising: its social costs, its funding of free media, and moves to regulate or tax it

## Stance

advertise.co.uk is pro-clarity and pro-advertiser competence: it believes that most people who spend money on advertising understand it less well than they should, that platforms benefit from this opacity, and that plain, honest explanation is genuinely useful — it is not anti-advertising but it is unsentimental about how the industry works and willing to name when advertisers are being exploited or misled

## Differentiation

The obvious online resources for 'advertising' are: (1) agency and platform marketing sites that exist to sell advertising services (Google, Meta, agency blogs); (2) academic or US-centric sources like the FTC guide surfaced in the research; (3) SNHU-style educational content that covers digital marketing types at a surface level for students. advertise.co.uk occupies a different position: UK-specific, non-commercial, mechanistically honest, and aimed at people who spend money on advertising rather than people who sell it. It does not promote or broker advertising services. It is deliberately separated from websitepromotion.co.uk (which covers promoting websites specifically) and from seotools.co.uk (SEO as a discipline) and webdesign.uk (design). The advertising industry's own bodies (ASA, AA, IAB UK) publish data and rules but not accessible explainers for non-specialists. That gap is this site's territory.

## Content plan

-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** How Advertising Works: The Complete Beginner's Guide
  **What:** A structured explainer covering what advertising is, the key roles (advertiser, agency, publisher, platform, consumer), the main channels, and how money flows through the system. The entry point for every new visitor who does not yet know the vocabulary.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** The UK Advertising Rules: What You Can and Cannot Say
  **What:** A plain-English guide to the ASA/CAP Code, the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations, substantiation requirements, comparative advertising, endorsements and testimonials, and sector-specific rules (alcohol, children, health claims). Covers both broadcast and non-broadcast codes. UK-specific, unlike the FTC-focused resources the research surfaced.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** How Digital Advertising Auctions Work
  **What:** Explains real-time bidding, programmatic advertising, DSPs, SSPs, ad exchanges, and the auction mechanics behind Google and Meta ads. Covers quality scores, targeting signals, and why the same ad slot can cost different amounts to different advertisers. Directly answers the most common question business owners have about paid digital.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** How Your Data Is Used in Advertising
  **What:** Explains cookies, tracking pixels, browser fingerprinting, location data, cross-device tracking, and data brokers — from the consumer's perspective and the advertiser's perspective. Covers what data platforms hold, UK GDPR implications for advertisers, and what consent requirements mean in practice.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** directory
  **Name:** Advertising Channels Directory
  **What:** A structured reference listing every major advertising medium — Google Search, Google Display, Meta (Facebook/Instagram), YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn, Amazon Ads, programmatic display, TV (linear and connected), radio, out-of-home, print, direct mail, podcast, influencer — with for each: typical cost model, minimum realistic budget, best-fit business type, key strength, key weakness, and the regulator or self-regulatory body that governs it.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** tool
  **Name:** Advertising Channel Matcher
  **What:** Reader inputs: business type, monthly budget, target audience (age, geography, B2B or B2C), goal (awareness, leads, sales). Tool outputs: ranked shortlist of best-fit advertising channels with a one-paragraph rationale for each. Not a buying tool — an orientation tool.
  **Priority:** core
-
  **Kind:** tool
  **Name:** Ad Claim Sense-Check
  **What:** Reader types or pastes an advertising claim (e.g. 'the UK's most trusted brand', 'clinically proven to reduce wrinkles', 'up to 50% off'). Tool flags the claim type (superlative, comparative, health, price), explains what the ASA/CAP Code requires to substantiate it, and warns of common compliance failures. Not legal advice — a self-education tool.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** tool
  **Name:** CPM / CPC / ROAS Calculator
  **What:** Reader enters campaign spend, impressions, clicks, conversions, and revenue. Tool calculates CPM, CPC, CTR, CPA, and ROAS, and benchmarks them against typical UK industry ranges. Helps business owners interpret their platform dashboards without needing an agency.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** data
  **Name:** UK Advertising Spending by Medium: Reference Tables
  **What:** Annual and quarterly data on UK advertising expenditure by channel (digital, TV, out-of-home, radio, print, cinema), sourced from Advertising Association/WARC and IAB UK figures. The numbers marketers and journalists look up when they need to contextualise a budget decision or write a briefing.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** Platform Guides Series
  **What:** Individual explainer pages for each major advertising platform — Google Ads, Meta Ads, Amazon Advertising, LinkedIn Campaign Manager, TikTok Ads, YouTube — covering: how the platform makes money, how targeting works, minimum budgets, ad formats available, and the platform's specific policies advertisers must follow. Practical and mechanics-focused, not a tutorial for operating the dashboards.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** editorial
  **Name:** The Attention Economy: What Advertising Has Done to the Internet
  **What:** A substantial editorial examining how advertising-funded business models shaped the web, why social media is designed to be addictive, how local journalism was defunded, and what the public debate about digital services taxes and advertising regulation looks like in the UK and globally. The site's house view on advertising's social costs — present because the research strongly surfaces this as a live public conversation.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** Advertising on a Small Budget: A Realistic Guide for UK Businesses
  **What:** Practical guidance for businesses spending under £2,000 per month: which channels are accessible at small scale, what a realistic expectation of results looks like, common mistakes and how to avoid them, and how to measure whether it is working. Grounded, unsentimental, and targeted at the primary audience.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** research
  **Name:** How the Big Platforms Compare: What Advertisers Actually Get
  **What:** A structured comparison of Google, Meta, Amazon, and TikTok as advertising platforms — covering their share of UK digital ad spend, the data they hold on users, their targeting capabilities, their transparency tools, their track record on brand safety, and their terms for advertisers. Original synthesis, not a commercial comparison.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** guide
  **Name:** Advertising Regulation in the UK: A Map of the System
  **What:** Explains who regulates what: ASA/CAP (non-broadcast), Ofcom (broadcast), CMA (competition and consumer protection), ICO (data in advertising), and the FCA's specific rules for financial advertising. Explains how complaints are made and what happens when a company is found in breach.
  **Priority:** valuable
-
  **Kind:** data
  **Name:** Advertising Glossary
  **What:** A comprehensive, plain-English definition of every term a person encounters when reading about or buying advertising: from Above the Fold to Viewability, including programmatic jargon, platform-specific terms, and measurement metrics. Optimised as a reference page people bookmark.
  **Priority:** aspirational

  **Kind:** news
  **Name:** UK Advertising News Digest
  **What:** A selective digest of significant regulatory decisions (ASA rulings, Ofcom findings), platform policy changes, and industry-level shifts — published when events warrant it, not on a forced daily schedule.   **Priority:** aspirational

## Tool opportunities

-
  **Why:** This is the question every small business owner actually has before they spend any money, and no platform will give an honest answer because each platform has an incentive to recommend itself
  **Name:** Advertising Channel Matcher
  **Input:** Business type, monthly budget range, target audience description (age band, geography, B2B or B2C, interest category), primary campaign goal (brand awareness / website traffic / lead generation / direct sales)
  **Output:** Ranked list of 3-5 recommended advertising channels with a plain-English rationale for each, typical cost ranges, and a realistic expectation of what that budget will achieve
-
  **Why:** Platform dashboards show these numbers but do not explain them; business owners routinely misread their own campaign performance
  **Name:** CPM / CPC / ROAS Calculator
  **Input:** Campaign spend (£), impressions served, clicks received, conversions, and revenue generated (optional)
  **Output:** CPM, CPC, CTR, CPA, and ROAS calculated and displayed with a plain-English explanation of what each means and rough UK benchmark ranges by channel
-
  **Why:** Small businesses frequently run ads that breach ASA rules without knowing it; this tool educates without providing legal advice
  **Name:** Ad Claim Sense-Check
  **Input:** An advertising claim typed or pasted by the user
  **Output:** Classification of claim type (superlative, health, environmental, price, comparative, testimonial), the relevant ASA/CAP Code provisions that apply, and a plain-English summary of what substantiation would be required

## Directory opportunity

Yes. The Advertising Channels Directory should be the centrepiece reference: entries for approximately 15-20 named advertising channels and platforms, each carrying: cost model (CPC/CPM/flat rate), typical minimum monthly spend to see results, best-fit audience type, primary targeting mechanism, key regulatory body, and a plain-English verdict on who it suits. This is the kind of structured reference that gets linked to and bookmarked, and it does not exist in a non-commercial, non-agency-sales form for a UK audience.

## Must nots

- Must not offer to place, broker, or arrange advertising on behalf of any advertiser — this is not an advertising agency or media buyer and must not present as one
- Must not carry or display third-party advertising in a way that undermines its editorial independence, given that advertising opacity is a core subject of the site
- Must not present the Ad Claim Sense-Check tool as legal advice — it must carry a clear statement that it is for education only and that anyone with a compliance question should consult a qualified lawyer or contact the ASA pre-clearance services
- Must not imply authorisation by the ASA, CAP, Ofcom, ICO, CMA, or FCA — it is not a regulator and must not be mistaken for one
- Must not promise advertising results to visitors — it explains advertising, it does not sell it
- Must not scrape, republish, or reproduce ASA adjudication texts in a way that misrepresents their conclusions or implies endorsement by the ASA
- Must not make specific investment recommendations about advertising spend — the Channel Matcher is an orientation tool, not financial advice

## Open questions

- Portfolio position is unrecorded: the owner should confirm whether advertise.co.uk is intended to be a broad advertising-as-a-subject reference site (the interpretation used here) or whether it has a narrower intended role — for example, as a lead-generation site for advertising services (which would require a completely different brief and would conflict with the must-nots above)
- The three estate neighbours (websitepromotion.co.uk, seotools.co.uk, webdesign.uk) suggest a cluster of marketing-adjacent sites; the owner should define whether advertise.co.uk is the 'parent' or hub of that cluster, which would affect navigation, internal linking strategy, and how broadly to cover digital marketing overlap topics like PPC and social media advertising
- Monetisation model: a site that covers advertising honestly and does not itself sell advertising services needs an alternative revenue model — the owner should decide whether this is display advertising (potentially ironic but viable), affiliate links to advertising tools (HubSpot, Semrush, etc.), a paid directory, or something else, as this affects content tone and what the must-nots permit
- The Ad Claim Sense-Check tool covers UK law (ASA/CAP) rather than US law (FTC) — the owner should confirm the site is definitively UK-focused, since the domain is .co.uk but the research surfaced US-centric material, and the editorial angle should be consistent throughout
- News section viability: ASA rulings and platform policy changes provide a real news stream, but producing a digest requires editorial resource on an ongoing basis; the owner should decide at the outset whether this is in scope or whether the site is purely evergreen reference content

good — five substantive research results covering the mechanics of targeted advertising and data collection, the full text of US advertising law and FTC rules, the UK Digital Services Tax policy debate, platform-by-platform data collection practices, and an overview of digital marketing types; the material is US-heavy and the UK regulatory specifics (ASA/CAP, Ofcom, ICO) were not directly surfaced, but the subject is well-documented enough to support a confident brief
