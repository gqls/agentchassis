## Proposition

seotools.co.uk is the definitive UK-focused reference and working resource for the SEO tools landscape: a structured directory of every significant tool available, honest head-to-head comparisons across tool categories, and a suite of free working tools that practitioners can use directly in-browser. It exists for anyone who needs to choose, evaluate, or get more out of SEO software — from the freelancer deciding between Ahrefs and Semrush to the in-house team scoping a technical audit stack. Where websitepromotion.co.uk covers the craft of promotion and advertise.co.uk covers advertising as a discipline, this site owns the instrument layer: the software, the platforms, the checkers, the crawlers, and the data tools that make SEO work happen.

## Audience

**Primary:** UK-based digital marketers, SEO practitioners, and small-to-medium business owners who are actively evaluating, comparing, or trying to get better use from SEO tools — at the moment they need to pick a tool, understand what a category of tool does, or run a quick check without logging in somewhere
**Secondary:** Agency staff and freelancers who manage SEO for multiple clients and need a fast reference for tool capabilities, pricing tiers, and UK-relevant data coverage

## Reader intent

- Find out which SEO tool is best for a specific job (keyword research, technical audit, rank tracking, backlink analysis, content optimisation, AI visibility monitoring)
- Compare named tools honestly — pricing, feature differences, limitations — without having to read each vendor's own marketing
- Use a free working tool directly on-site without signing up anywhere (e.g. check a URL's meta tags, generate keyword suggestions, score a title tag)
- Understand what a category of SEO tool does and what to look for when buying
- Find all the tools in a specific niche (e.g. every rank tracker, every local SEO tool, every AI search visibility monitor) in one browsable list
- Look up what a specific SEO tool does, costs, and is best suited for
- Get current news about tool launches, pricing changes, and feature updates in the SEO software market
- Learn the terminology used in SEO tool dashboards and reports via a practical glossary

## Stance

seotools.co.uk is for practitioners who want straight answers about the tools, not vendor pitches and not beginner SEO tutorials — it is on the side of the person choosing or using software, and against the obscuring of limitations, pricing complexity, and feature bloat that characterises most tool marketing.

## Differentiation

The primary competitors surfaced by research are: G2 and Capterra (broad software directories, heavily review-based, not editorially independent, US-centric, no working tools), Ahrefs blog and Semrush blog (vendor-owned, inherently partial), individual review sites like OneLittleWeb and MarketerMilk (good editorial intent but no working tools, limited structure, no directory), and Tim Soulo's newsletter-style comparisons (strong analysis but narrow scope). seotools.co.uk differentiates by: (1) combining a structured directory with free working tools in one destination — no comparable site does both; (2) explicit UK relevance, including UK data coverage notes that US comparison sites never address; (3) editorial independence with a stated stance against vendor-marketing opacity; (4) a proper glossary of tool-dashboard terminology rather than general SEO jargon; (5) category comparison pages with structured feature matrices rather than narrative listicles; and (6) a dedicated AI search visibility tools section that reflects the fast-moving 2025-2026 market reality documented in the research.

## Must nots

- Must not present as an SEO agency or offer to carry out SEO work on behalf of visitors
- Must not use negative-identity claims in site copy ('we are not X', 'we do not sell Y')
- Must not publish SEO how-to tutorials or strategy guides — that territory belongs to websitepromotion.co.uk in the cluster; the site's remit is the tools themselves, not the craft
- Must not cover advertising tools, PPC tools, or paid media platforms as primary content — that breadth belongs to advertise.co.uk as the cluster flagship
- Must not make affiliate-style claims that obscure tool limitations or present paid placements as editorial rankings without clear disclosure
- Must not assert or imply that any tool recommendation constitutes professional SEO consultancy advice
- Must not scrape or reproduce tool vendor pricing without dating the data and noting it may have changed — pricing data must carry a 'verified as of' date
- Must not host user-generated reviews without moderation — the site's value is editorial quality, not a ratings platform

## Open questions

- Portfolio position is unrecorded — a human should confirm where seotools.co.uk sits relative to any other cluster siblings not mentioned in this brief, particularly any domain that might cover software tools more broadly
- The cluster boundary with websitepromotion.co.uk needs a human ruling on one grey area: tools that are primarily for content optimisation (Surfer, Clearscope, Frase) serve both SEO and broader promotion — should seotools.co.uk cover them fully as SEO tools, or treat them as shared territory?
- The free working tools require server-side fetching for Meta Tag Checker, Robots.txt Tester, Redirect Chain Checker, and Core Web Vitals Lookup — a human or technical planner should confirm whether the platform can support server-side requests or whether these tools need a proxied API approach
- The Keyword Difficulty Estimator as specified would require either a paid data API (e.g. DataForSEO) or a significant limitation in output quality — a human should decide the acceptable API budget or whether a simpler autocomplete-only version is the right scope for launch
- Should the site carry affiliate links to the tools in the directory? This is an obvious revenue model but changes the editorial independence claim — requires an explicit owner decision before any content is commissioned
- The 'Annual UK SEO Tools Report' requires original research methodology — a human should decide whether this is conducted via public data aggregation, a practitioner survey, or editorial synthesis, as this affects resource requirements significantly
- The news section requires a content pipeline decision: will tool news be manually curated, pulled via RSS aggregation, or AI-assisted? The answer affects both quality and resource planning
- Are there any existing assets, data, or tooling from the previous hosted version of this site that should be preserved or migrated?
