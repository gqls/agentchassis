# SUMMARY — 2026-09-02: the gate holds the mint, and the axis it was built under just changed

## What we're trying to do

Work out what a website visitor actually gets from a business, decide what order those things
should be said in, and make the machinery that writes the pages actually use that judgement. The
lane owns the judgement; other lanes own the writing.

## Where we've come from

The judgement existed and nothing read it. We had built a per-site analysis — 32 sites, 190 ranked
benefit statements, each with its reasoning and a note of which research field it came from — and
measured that **not one of the five agents that write a page ever opened it.** The owner then read
some finished pages and objected to how they were phrased: certain verbal tics he had banned kept
appearing, and one hero led with a true fact that was almost certainly not what a visitor wanted to
know first.

A repair ran across the estate and fixed 41 of those statements. That worked, and it did not last:
the thing that *writes* the statements had learned nothing and kept writing at the same rate. Within
six hours it had produced 75 new ones and 18 carried the same phrasings — including the first-ranked
statement on two sites, one of them the site the owner had rejected in the first place.

That is the finding the whole month turns on. **You cannot clean a well faster than it refills.**

## What we've done

Built a gate at the point where the statements are written, rather than downstream of it. When it
catches a bad phrasing it asks for the statement to be restated, and vets the result before
accepting it — the owner chose that over the alternatives of deleting the statement or throwing away
the whole analysis, both of which destroy something real.

It went through the review council twice. The first round came back asking for changes, and the
objection that earned the round forced a measurement I had not done: **the statements we gated are
one of eleven kinds of research the writers already read, and by volume among the smallest.** Ten
others carry the same bad phrasings. Three of them are worse than unguarded — they sit on an
exemption list, so a bad phrasing there is not merely missed, it *licenses* the same phrasing in
copy a visitor reads. That finding went to the copy lane, and the owner has since ruled that those
briefs get cleaned fleet-wide.

The second round was approved. Every advisory was acted on, and two of them were real: I had claimed
something was exclusive without ever querying it, and I was relying on a detail of how records merge
that I had got right by luck rather than design. Both are now measured and pinned by tests.

## Where we are now

**The gate is live and working.** It repaired its first two statements this evening, and I read what
it actually wrote rather than trusting the status: both were surgical — the banned word removed, the
grammar tidied around it, nothing else touched. That is exactly the treatment the owner's rules
prescribe.

Two honest qualifications. **Two repairs is a working mechanism, not a success rate** — the number
it has to move is about one in four, and that needs days of output, not one run. And the same
artefact showed a hole: a statement the gate marked as repaired still carries the objectionable
construction in a form written with a dash instead of a comma, which neither the rulebook nor the
scanner can see. Not the gate's fault, but a repaired statement is only clean against the rules *as
written*.

Along the way the gate found a fault in its own deployment before it could mislead anyone: for two
minutes it was live, firing, and unable to repair anything, because the agent it runs inside has no
model configured at the top level. It reported that plainly instead of failing quietly — and a
reviewer had predicted that exact failure two days earlier and asked us to check for it at exactly
this moment.

## Where we're going

The owner has ruled on two things that change the lane's next job, and they have to be done in the
right order.

He has confirmed that **heroes should be governed by what the buyer wants to know and whether it
reads well — and that distinctiveness demotes to an input.** Our analysis currently ranks on
distinctiveness: the top-ranked statement is the most distinctive one on every single site. So the
ranking is on the wrong axis, and it is the axis we deliberately built.

But re-ranking first would achieve nothing, because we already measured that the material a buyer
actually wants is *largely absent*, not merely badly ordered. Re-ordering a list cannot surface
something that was never worked out. So the sequence is: **first derive the buyer's own ordered
questions and join each existing statement to the question it answers, then re-rank against that.**
The owner has approved that build. We expect the first pass to come back mostly "nothing here
answers this" — and that is the correct result, not a failure. It is the point.
