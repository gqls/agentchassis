# Idea report — small llm training service company

**Audience:** Vertical SMBs with messy, proprietary internal text/docs (e.g., regional law firms, specialty clinics, niche manufacturers) who want a model that knows THEIR data but lack ML staff
**Willingness to pay:** These buyers pay because the bottleneck is not the model but the labor of turning their fragmented, sensitive documents into something usable, which they cannot safely paste into a public chatbot. They will pay a recurring fee for a managed, private, domain-grounded assistant—but only if it does something a generic model plus a good prompt provably cannot.

## Advancing candidates (ranked)

### 1. Drawing/Form Reader to Structured Data  [consider]
- **Idea:** use *Multi-modal input + schema-guaranteed output* on *Labeled set of the customer's engineering drawings or intake forms* to Tuned vision extraction on their specific drawing/form conventions returns validated structured fields, newly reliable in the last year.
- **Verification:** VERIFIED via search: multimodal extraction is real and recently matured — LlamaExtract offers field-level confidence scoring, plus Amazon Textract, Azure OCR, Infrrd, CoLab, and Energent benchmarking 7 platforms; market reports confirm AI now outperforms basic OCR on complex drawings (plain OCR relies on value-pair detection and struggles with engineering drawings). Competition is significant BUT the differentiator — tuning on the customer's own drawing/form conventions (proprietary symbols, title blocks) — is a real gap generic tools miss, and engineering teams pay for drawing-data automation. Premise holds.
- **Scores:** Defensibility 4/5 · Willingness 4/5 · Buildability 3/5 · Reuse 3/5 · Durability 3/5 (sum 18)
- **Operator risk:** 4/5 (low — refunds make customers whole)
- **Cheapest test:** Ask one engineering or facilities prospect to share 20 anonymised drawings. Run them through a zero-shot multimodal extraction baseline and show the error rate to the prospect. Contrast with a quick fine-tuned pass on 5 labelled examples. If the accuracy delta is visible and they say 'this would save us X hours', that is the green light.

### 8. Real-Time Voice Intake Agent  [consider] — *short-lived (low durability)*
- **Idea:** use *FRONTIER: real-time voice agents + action-taking* on *Domain script + tuned terminology model + CRM integration* to Conducts live, domain-accurate intake calls and writes structured records into the customer's system—newly viable voice latency.
- **Verification:** VERIFIED via search: the enabling tech is newly viable — OpenAI Realtime API / voice-agents support barge-in, low first-audio latency, natural turn-taking, and realtime tool use over WebRTC; newer realtime models fire multiple simultaneous tool calls. Caveat confirmed: latency drifts over long sessions (~800ms early to >2s after 20+ turns), so keep calls short/structured. Voice-AI is a crowded space, but the asset (domain terminology tune + CRM write-back) is a legitimate services differentiator; intake automation has clear WTP (replacing intake/call-center labor). Premise holds with latency/competition caveats.
- **Scores:** Defensibility 3/5 · Willingness 4/5 · Buildability 2/5 · Reuse 3/5 · Durability 2/5 (sum 14)
- **Operator risk:** 3/5 (moderate — cite sources; PII recommended)
- **Cheapest test:** Record and transcribe 10 real intake calls from a prospect. Build a text-only intake bot first (no voice) to validate the domain script and CRM write-back. Only add the voice layer once a prospect confirms they would switch from a human intake agent. This avoids the expensive voice infrastructure until demand is proven.
