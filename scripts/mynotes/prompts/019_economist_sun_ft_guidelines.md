Sun
Based on reporting by Roy Greenslade for The Guardian, the 2014 updated stylebook for The Sun—compiled by editors John Perry and Jon Moorhead—marked a shift towards modernization and "nuance" while maintaining the paper's signature punchy, tabloid tone. The analysis highlights that the guide sought to reduce overused clichés while strictly enforcing the use of conversational language, focusing on simple vocabulary designed for rapid, engaging reading, often questioning the relevance of details like hair colour (blonde/brunette) and tightening up reporting on sensitive issues.
Primary Goal	Modernise tabloid voice, reduce clichés
Tone	Conversational, direct, and sensationalist
Key Focus	Short sentences, active verbs, high emotional impact
Key Insights from the Stylebook

    Anti-Cliché Movement: The guide advised against overuse of "grotesque" clichéd phrases like "pint-sized," "stunner," "staggering," and "horror," aiming for more precise, yet still punchy, language. 
    Gender Reporting: The book instructed staff to question if mentioning hair colour (blonde/brunette) was necessary, suggesting a shift away from traditional, objectifying tabloid tropes. 
    Simplified Language: The guide reinforced the use of conversational, simple English suitable for all readers, aiming for a "12-year-old or 80-year-old" understanding. 
    Prohibition of "Child Porn": The guide deemed the term "child porn" too friendly, urging more descriptive language for serious crimes. 
    Internal Focus: The stylebook is largely aimed at producing "tabloid speak"—using short words, alliteration, and direct, emotional language.
